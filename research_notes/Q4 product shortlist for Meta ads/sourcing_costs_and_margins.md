## 1. What US import rules apply in late 2026 to small parcels from China, what do they cost, and do agents include duty in their prices?

### Takeaway
The $800 de minimis exemption is suspended indefinitely for all countries and all shipping modes, so every parcel from China is entered and pays duty on its declared product value. For most Chinese consumer goods the duty is the normal rate plus either 20 or 37.5 percentage points (old Section 301 at 7.5% or 25%, plus a new 12.5% Section 301 duty since 24 July 2026). Zendrop says duty is built into its US shipping fee; for CJdropshipping and AliExpress this could not be confirmed for 2026.

### Cited Findings

**De minimis (the $800 exemption)**
- De minimis was suspended for China and Hong Kong on 2 May 2025 and for all countries on 29 August 2025 (SNIPPET, secondary blogs) — [importcostcalculator.com](https://www.importcostcalculator.com/de-minimis-800-rule.html); [tariffstool.com](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/de-minimis-exemption-ended-2026)
- On 24 June 2026 CBP published two interim final rules that suspend the exemption **indefinitely**: one for international mail, one for all other modes (air cargo, express couriers, ocean, truck). All shipments of $800 or less now need informal or formal entry (SNIPPET) — [Federal Register 2026-12669 (mail)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12669/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-mail-shipments-and-new-postal-informal-entry); [Federal Register 2026-12670 (other modes)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other); [KPMG summary](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/06/cbp-suspends-de-minimis-exemption.html); [BDO summary](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/cbp-suspends-de-minimis-exemption-and-introduces-new-postal-entry-requirements)
- A new postal informal entry process took effect 24 July 2026 for mail shipments worth $2,500 or less. Duties on mail must be paid by the carrier or a CBP-certified "qualified party", and parcels are not released until CBP has the bond. A delayed-compliance window was extended by a notice dated 22 October 2026 (SNIPPET) — [Federal Register 2026-21600](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/22/2026-21600/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-mail-shipments-and-new-postal-informal-entry)
- The earlier flat per-parcel duty option for mail (the "specific duty", reported in 2025 as $80–$200 per item) ended on 28 February 2026; mail now pays percentage duty like everything else (SNIPPET, one source) — [strixsmart.com](https://strixsmart.com/resources/blog/de-minimis-suspension-self-filing)
- Non-postal low-value shipments are generally filed as entry type 11 (informal entry, up to $2,500), with all duties, taxes and fees applied (SNIPPET) — [als-int.com](https://www.als-int.com/insights/posts/de-minimis-suspension-ecommerce-importers-2026/)
- A further proposed rule, "Low-Value Shipments" (published 8 October 2026, comments due 7 December 2026), would require a bond on every informal entry and create a mail entry type 13. It is **not final** (SNIPPET) — [Federal Register 2026-20650](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/08/2026-20650/low-value-shipments); [strixsmart.com](https://strixsmart.com/resources/blog/cbp-low-value-shipments-proposed-rule-informal-entry-bond)
- One source reports the Court of International Trade upheld the suspension on 13 August 2026. Not confirmed by a second source (SNIPPET) — [carraglobe.com](https://carraglobe.com/us-de-minimis-exemption-suspended-2026/)

**Tariff rates on Chinese goods (dated history)**
- 20 February 2026: the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs. This voided the "fentanyl" tariff on China (which had been 10% since November 2025) and the "reciprocal" tariff (SNIPPET) — [USTR statement](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/february/ambassador-greer-issues-statement-supreme-court-ieepa-decision); [Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs-trump-responds-with-section-122-global-surcharge/); [tariffos.com](https://tariffos.com/en/measures/ieepa-fentanyl-tariffs-china)
- 24 February 2026 to 24 July 2026: a temporary global Section 122 surcharge replaced it. Most sources say 10%; one says 15%. It has **expired** (SNIPPET) — [ttnews.com](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/trump-tariffs-expire-july-24); [Zonos](https://chore-i18n-sync-locale-script.www.zonos.com/docs/guides/us-tariff-changes); 15% figure in [fulfillmen.com](https://fulfillmen.com/import-tax-from-china-to-usa)
- 24 July 2026 onwards: USTR imposed a new **12.5% Section 301 duty on Chinese products** (the "forced labor" action), published 28 July 2026 at 91 FR 47318, HTS heading 9903.05.31, with exceptions listed in annexes (SNIPPET) — [Federal Register 2026-15181](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies); [USTR notice PDF](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Releases/2026/FLIP%20301%20Investigation%20Final%20Action%20FRN%207-23-26%20FINAL.pdf)

Worked examples from secondary sources: a List 3 product with 3% normal duty pays 40.5%; a List 3 product with 4% normal duty pays 41.5% (SNIPPET) — [tariffclassify.ai](https://tariffclassify.ai/blog/section-301-forced-labor-tariffs-in-effect-july-2026); [importcostpro.com](https://importcostpro.com/blog/section-301-tariffs-2026-guide); [Polsinelli alert](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/administration-imposes-forced-labor-section-301-tariffs-section-122-surcharge-expires)
- One source notes the stacking comes from HTS note 52(a) and is not stated expressly in the USTR notice or CBP's message (SNIPPET) — [tariffclassify.ai](https://tariffclassify.ai/blog/section-301-forced-labor-tariffs-in-effect-july-2026)
- Older Section 301 duties were not affected by the court ruling: Lists 1–3 add 25%, List 4A adds 7.5% (SNIPPET) — [airliftusa.com](https://airliftusa.com/tariff-updates/section-301); [htsbase.com](https://htsbase.com/blog/section-301-china-tariffs-2026)
- The new duty exempts Section 232 goods, civil aircraft, pharmaceutical-use articles and listed agricultural items; it is narrower on finished consumer electronics than the expired Section 122 surcharge was (SNIPPET) — [gingercontrol.com](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/section-301-forced-labor-investigations); [BRG](https://www.thinkbrg.com/insights/publications/beyond-the-surcharge-how-the-end-of-section-122-accelerates-the-convergence-of-trade-and-economic-security/)

- List assignments found for specific headings: 8516.29.00 (electric heaters) is on List 3 (+25%); 8518 (speakers/headphones) is reported as List 4A (+7.5%) (SNIPPET, not checked against the tariff schedule) — [cybermaxtools.com](https://cybermaxtools.com/blog/section-301-list-3-vs-list-4b-which-china-imports-pay/); [importcostcalculator.com](https://www.importcostcalculator.com/import-duty-from-china-to-usa.html)

### Inferences
- **Working duty rate for planning (ESTIMATE):** normal duty (often 0–6% for these goods, not checked per product) **+ 20 points** if the product is on List 4A, or **+ 37.5 points** if it is on List 1–3. Until each product's tariff code is checked, budget 37.5%–42% of the declared product value.
- **Duty is small in dollars on cheap items.** On a $3 item, 40% duty is $1.20. On an $8 item it is $3.20. The bigger cost is single-parcel shipping plus any per-parcel entry fee.
- The duty base is the product's declared value, not the retail price, so a $39.99 sale of a $4 item still only carries about $0.80–$1.60 of duty.
- Because no agent other than Zendrop clearly states that duty is inside its quoted price, a quote from CJ, AliExpress or a private agent should be treated as **possibly excluding duty** until the agent confirms it in writing. If duty is not prepaid, the customer may be asked to pay on delivery, which drives refusals and chargebacks.
- The proposed bond-per-entry rule (comments due 7 December 2026) would add cost to every China-direct parcel if finalised; it favours stocking in a US warehouse.

## 2. What do the main shipping lines cost and how long do they take for a 100–500 g parcel, what are the peak-season delays, and what are realistic Christmas cutoffs?

### Takeaway
Tracked "special line" shipping for one parcel costs roughly $5–$8 at 100–300 g and $7–$12 at 500 g, with 8–17 days normal transit stretching to about 12–22 days in Q4. With today being 23 November, China-direct orders placed after about the first days of December are unlikely to arrive by 24 December; US-warehouse stock can sell until mid-December.

## 3. Which lightweight products are available from US warehouses for 3–7 day delivery, and how much more do they cost?

### Takeaway
US-warehouse stock delivers in about 2–8 days and avoids per-parcel customs entry, but the unit price is roughly 25–35% higher and domestic shipping is not cheaper than CJPacket. No source listed which specific products are stocked in the US today; that has to be checked product by product inside each platform.

- The products most likely to be in US stock are high-volume generic gadgets (lint shavers, hand warmers, pet hair removers, LED lights, massage guns). This is my expectation, not a verified list.

## 4. What extra rules raise cost or risk (lithium batteries, FDA, FCC, CPSC, Prop 65, Meta ad policy)?

### Takeaway
Three rules matter most for these products: (1) CPSC certificate e-filing has applied to low-value direct-to-consumer parcels since July 2026 with no small-parcel exemption; (2) anything sold with a treatment claim (pain, arthritis, skin conditions) is treated as a medical device, while "general wellness" wording is not; (3) Meta's health and wellness rules were reportedly rewritten on 22 July 2026, and sources conflict on what before/after imagery is now allowed.

### Cited Findings

**Lithium batteries**
- From 1 January 2026 (IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations, 67th edition), many lithium-ion batteries shipped by air, including those packed with equipment, must be at 30% state of charge or less (SNIPPET) — [Flexport](https://www.flexport.com/blog/2025-and-2026-regulatory-changes-for-lithium-ion-and-sodium-ion-batteries/)
- A valid UN38.3 test summary is required to ship a lithium battery; carriers add their own limits, and batteries shipped alone are often refused (SNIPPET) — [DHL checklist](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-vn/ship-with-dhl/export-with-dhl/lithium-batteries-dangerous-goods-checklist); [sendfromchina.com](https://www.sendfromchina.com/NewsCenter/shipping-restrictions.html)
- A US shipper was fined $170,000 for undeclared lithium batteries (September 2025) (SNIPPET) — [lion.com](https://www.lion.com/lion-news/september-2025/undeclared-lithium-batteries-lead-to-$170,000-hazmat-penalty)
- One vendor page says the Section 301 rate on non-EV lithium-ion batteries from China rises to 25% in 2026 (SNIPPET; vendor page with inconsistent dates; applies to batteries imported as batteries, and whether it affects products with a built-in battery was not established) — [large-battery.com](https://www.large-battery.com/de/?p=2604)

**FDA (medical devices and wellness claims)**
- Consumer red-light devices are "cleared" via the 510(k) route, not "approved"; clearance covers one specific intended use (SNIPPET) — [onethousandroads.com](https://www.onethousandroads.com/blogs/ewot-blog/red-light-therapy-fda-cleared-approved)
- Low-risk devices sold for general wellness (relaxation, recovery, healthy lifestyle) do not need clearance, but the seller then cannot make disease-treatment claims (SNIPPET) — [lighttherapyiq.com](https://www.lighttherapyiq.com/best/fda-cleared-red-light-therapy-devices)
- FDA establishment registration is not clearance and does not mean FDA reviewed the device (SNIPPET) — [bankrollzen.com](https://bankrollzen.com/blog/fda-cleared-red-light-therapy-devices/)
- Cleared LED face masks and wands typically use product code OHS under 21 CFR 878.4810; cleared uses centre on hair loss (scalp devices) and muscle and joint pain relief (SNIPPET) — [lighttherapyiq.com](https://www.lighttherapyiq.com/best/fda-cleared-red-light-therapy-devices)
- Shipments subject to a partner agency such as FDA or CPSC may have to be filed as formal entries rather than informal ones (SNIPPET) — [thetradelab.ai](https://thetradelab.ai/blog/de-minimis-low-value-shipment-rule)

**CPSC**
- From **8 July 2026**, importers of products covered by a CPSC rule must e-file certificate data with CBP at entry (Children's Product Certificate or General Certificate of Conformity). There is **no low-value or direct-to-consumer exemption**. The mail-shipment requirement took effect 22 October 2026 (SNIPPET) — [UPS](https://www.ups.com/us/en/shipping/international-shipping/tariffs/cpsc); [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/cpsc-rule-mandates-efiling-of-certificates-of-compliance-for-imported-consumer-products); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/06/cbp-suspends-de-minimis-exemption.html)
- CPSC has said it does not currently intend to refuse entry solely for missing certificate data, but may still seize goods and seek penalties (SNIPPET) — [hirolaw.com](https://hirolaw.com/blog/cpsc-efiling-july-8-2026-certificate-readiness)
- Products whose tariff code is flagged by CPSC but that need no certificate can file a "disclaimer" message instead (SNIPPET) — [complir.io](https://www.complir.io/resources/guides/cpsc-efiling-guide)

**Meta advertising policy**
- Meta's official Health and Wellness page lists cosmetic products and procedures "depicting before-and-after transformation" as a restricted category (the page snippet did not show the age rule) (SNIPPET) — [Meta Health and Wellness standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness); [Meta Personal Health and Appearance standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/personal-health-and-appearance)
- Several agency blogs say Meta rewrote the standard on **22 July 2026**: side-by-side before/after images are no longer auto-rejected and are judged by the claim; general cosmetic before/after is allowed for audiences 18+; new bans on disease-cure and clickbait claims (SNIPPET, third-party only) — [auditsocials.com](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-health-wellness-ad-policy-rewrite-july-2026); [oney.studio](https://www.oney.studio/post/meta-july-2026-health-wellness-ad-policy-update); [adligator.com](https://adligator.com/blog/meta-health-wellness-ad-policy-update-2026); contradicted by older guides that still describe a blanket before/after ban, for example [adsuploader.com](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ad-guidelines)
- Still prohibited across sources: disease-cure claims, pinched-fat imagery, sensational health claims, negative statements about a person's appearance, and copy that implies the viewer has a health condition ("personal attributes") (SNIPPET) — [zappush.com](https://www.zappush.com/blog/meta-personal-attributes-policy-health-wellness-ads); [auditsocials.com](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-ad-misleading-claims-personal-attributes-prohibited-content-policy-2026)
- Interest targeting on health conditions is unavailable; custom audiences are allowed only if their names, rules and data do not suggest a health condition (SNIPPET) — [lor-media.com](https://www.lor-media.com/resources/meta-health-wellness-ad-rules)
- Regulated medical devices fall under Meta's drugs and pharmaceuticals rules; wellness devices get extra scrutiny when marketed with medical claims (SNIPPET) — [accelerateddigitalmedia.com](https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/guide-to-social-media-health-ad-restrictions-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Higher-risk product types** from the list (treatment-style claims are the usual selling angle): red-light wands and LED masks, heated eye masks sold for dry eye, posture correctors, neck stretchers sold for pain, compression gloves sold for arthritis, TENS-type devices, massage guns sold for pain relief. Each is safer sold on comfort, relaxation or convenience wording.
- **Battery items** (sleep-mask headphones, hand warmers, mini blenders, massage guns, spin scrubbers, neck lights, lint shavers, arc lighters) need a battery-accepting line; expect a higher shipping rate and fewer line choices.
- Rechargeable hand warmers that double as power banks carry the largest cells of any product here and are the most likely to be refused by standard lines.
- The CPSC e-filing rule mainly bites on children's products and on categories with a specific CPSC standard. Avoiding anything aimed at children under 12 removes most of that exposure.

### Gaps
- **FCC:** no source was found on FCC authorisation rules for imported Bluetooth and other electronic products. Not covered.
- **California Prop 65:** no source was found on current warning rules. Not covered.
- **FDA status of heated eye masks, posture correctors, compression gloves, TENS units and massagers** was not found; the search returned only red-light material.
- Battery surcharges and acceptance rules for CJPacket Sensitive, YunExpress and 4PX battery lines were not found.
- Meta's live policy text could not be opened, so the 22 July 2026 rewrite rests on agency blogs. Meta's rules on health-related pixel data were not found.

## 5. What payment processing, Shopify fees, refund/return rates and chargeback rates should be assumed?

### Takeaway
Assume 2.9% + 30¢ per order on Shopify Basic with Shopify Payments (about $1.46 on a $39.99 order), a $15 fee per chargeback, and returns/refunds of roughly 5–15% for gadgets and accessories. No reliable chargeback-rate benchmark was found.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify Payments, US online cards, Basic plan: **2.9% + 30¢**; Advanced plan 2.4% + 30¢ (dated October 2026 by one blog) (SNIPPET) — [eseospace.com](https://eseospace.com/blog/shopify-transaction-fees/); [Shopify on card processing fees](https://www.shopify.com/blog/credit-card-processing-fees)
- Using a third-party gateway instead of Shopify Payments adds a 2% Shopify transaction fee on Basic (so Stripe on Basic costs about 4.9% in total) (SNIPPET) — [eightx.co](https://eightx.co/blog/compare/how-much-does-shopify-payments-cost)
- Chargeback fee: **$15** per dispute for US stores, refunded if the seller wins; the disputed amount is withdrawn as well (SNIPPET) — [ecommerce-platforms.com](https://ecommerce-platforms.com/articles/how-much-does-shopify-take-per-sale); [craftshift.com](https://craftshift.com/shopify-transaction-fees-explained/)
- Dropshipping return rates: one May 2026 analysis uses 16–20% overall as a planning figure, apparel 25–40%, electronics 8–12% (SNIPPET) — [productlair.com](https://productlair.com/blog/dropshipping-returns-true-cost)
- Home goods, accessories and low-consideration products: 5–15% (SNIPPET) — [alidropship.com](https://alidropship.com/dropshipping-wiki/returns/)
- Beauty and skincare: about 10–12% per one source, 15–20% per another (SNIPPET) — [branvas.com](https://branvas.com/blogs/news/best-products-dropship-2026); [dojobusiness.com](https://dojobusiness.com/blogs/news/dropshipping-return-rate)
- Shopify's 2023 figure for general merchandise returns was 3–5% (SNIPPET, cited second-hand) — [usadrop.com](https://usadrop.com/dropshipping-return-policy/)
- Electronics and beauty are the top categories for "item not as described" disputes; a dispute costs the refund plus a $15–$25 fee (SNIPPET) — [productlair.com](https://productlair.com/blog/dropshipping-chargebacks)
- One analysis says a "tools" product goes negative at only a 10% return rate (SNIPPET) — [productlair.com](https://productlair.com/blog/dropshipping-returns-true-cost)

### Inferences
- **Per-order assumptions for the margin model (ESTIMATE):** payment fee 2.9% + $0.30; refunds/returns 8% of orders for simple non-electric items, 12% for electrical items, most handled as refund-without-return because sending a $5 item back to China is uneconomic; chargebacks 0.5–1% of orders at $15 each plus the lost sale. Together these take about 12–18% of revenue on top of landed cost.
- On a $39.99 order: payment fee $1.46 (3.7%), refund allowance $3.20–$4.80 (8–12%), chargeback allowance about $0.30–$0.55.
- Slow Q4 delivery raises both refund and chargeback rates; late-December orders are the riskiest.

### Gaps
- No reliable average chargeback rate for dropshipping or for these categories was found.
- Card-network dispute monitoring thresholds (for example Visa's programme) were not found.
- Shopify's own pricing page could not be opened; rates come from blogs and a Shopify blog post.

## 7. Which product types can realistically reach a landed cost of 20–30% of selling price or less at $30–$60, and which cannot?

### Takeaway
Only small, non-electric, low-unit-cost items reach 20–30% landed at a $30+ price: personalised necklaces, posture correctors and neck stretchers. Most battery-powered gadgets land at 35–50% of price when realistic unit costs and shipping are used, well above the seller's current 20.8%.

### Cited Findings
- The seller's reference is $8.33 landed on $39.99, which is 20.8% (given in the brief; no external source).
- Single-parcel shipping alone is about $4.90–$8 for 100–300 g and $7–$12 for 500 g (SNIPPET) — [fulfido.com](https://fulfido.com/shipping/china-to-us); [oneshippros.com](https://oneshippros.com/how-to-ship-from-china-to-usa-fast/); [easync.io review](https://easync.io/articles/cj-dropshipping-review/)
- Duty adds 20–37.5 points on the product's declared value, on top of normal duty (SNIPPET) — [htsbase.com](https://htsbase.com/blog/section-301-china-tariffs-2026); [importcostpro.com](https://importcostpro.com/blog/section-301-tariffs-2026-guide)
- Fast shipping "can eat margins on cheap products" on CJ (SNIPPET) — [eDesk review, updated July 2026](https://www.edesk.com/blog/cj-dropshipping-everything-to-get-started/)

### Inferences
All groupings below are ESTIMATES drawn from the section 6 table (midpoint of each landed range against the estimated price).
