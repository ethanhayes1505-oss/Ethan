# Meta Ads Manager and Events Manager: how to diagnose a high CPM and apply the changes Meta documents (click by click, November 2026)

How to read these notes (retrieved 19 November 2026):

- Every source below is a Meta Business Help Center article (one is an Instagram Help page). No practitioner blogs were used.
- How the text was obtained: the Help Center pages could not be opened directly from this session (a browser-style request to www.facebook.com returned an HTTP 400 error page, and the web fetch tool could not resolve the host). The article text was instead returned by Meta's own help-article lookup inside the connected Meta Ads tool. That lookup returns the article body as plain lowercase text.
- Consequence for button names: the WORDS of every button, menu and column name below are Meta's. The CAPITAL LETTERS are mine, because the tool lowercases everything. Also, the text is US English; an account set to English (UK) will show UK spellings (for example "Customise columns", "Optimisation"). Exact UK on-screen spelling: could not confirm current wording.
- "(read in full)" means the tool returned the article through to its natural end or its closing "Learn more" list. "(excerpt only)" means the returned text was cut off part-way.
- Several articles carry Meta's own notices such as "this feature may not be available to you yet" or "we are gradually introducing this feature". Where that applies it is stated, because the owner's screen may differ.
- Meta's list of "significant edits" (the edits that restart the learning phase) is quoted in the Reducing-ads section and referred to throughout.

## Diagnosis: breakdowns, columns, Delivery status, learning status, Inspect and auction overlap

### Takeaway
Breakdowns and columns are read-only views, so the whole diagnosis can be done without touching learning. Meta's current Help text gives exact paths for Breakdown and Customize columns and says the reason for "Learning limited" shows as a hover tooltip, but it no longer mentions an "Inspect" tool at all; auction overlap is now surfaced through Opportunity score recommendations.

### Cited Findings
Breakdowns
- Steps as Meta writes them: "1. Select the campaign, ad set or ad that you want to view. 2. Click the Breakdown icon. 3. Click By time, By delivery or By action and select a breakdown." — [Navigate to breakdowns in Meta Ads Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1798966537090251) (read in full)
- For website (offsite) conversions such as Purchase, the breakdowns Meta supports under By delivery are: "age, gender, age and gender, country, impression device, platform, platform & device, and placement." — [Navigate to breakdowns](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1798966537090251) (read in full)
- Day is a time breakdown: "Time (e.g. day, week, month). This category shows more detail by day, week, 2-week periods and months." — [About breakdowns, metrics and filtering](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/264160060861852) (read in full)
- You can stack one breakdown from each category but not two from the same category: "you can select Age (under Delivery) and Day (under Time) ... Note: you cannot combine breakdowns under the same category." For offsite conversions the delivery breakdowns can only be combined "with breakdowns under Time or dynamic creative asset." — [About breakdowns, metrics and filtering](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/264160060861852) (read in full)
- Meta's own tip for device: "Select Platform & device to see if more people interacted with your ad on a specific platform or device like an iPhone, iPad or Android smartphone." — [About breakdowns, metrics and filtering](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/264160060861852) (read in full)
- Warning that applies directly to the 25-65 age suggestion: "If you used Advantage+ audience audience suggestions for age, gender and detailed targeting we'll show ads to people matching your suggestion, and other audiences when it's likely to improve performance." So the age breakdown can legitimately show people outside 25-65. — [About breakdowns, metrics and filtering](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/264160060861852) (read in full)
- Conversions that cannot be attributed to a breakdown row "will show up as uncategorized", and "statistical modeling may be used in breakdowns". — [About breakdowns, metrics and filtering](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/264160060861852) (read in full)
- Hourly view: Breakdown > By delivery > "Time of day (ad account time zone)" or "Time of day (viewer's time zone)". Reach and other "unique" metrics are not available in this view, and it cannot be combined with age, gender or location. — [View hourly breakdowns](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/223337234674946) (read in full)

Columns
- Steps: "1. Click Campaigns, Ad sets or Ads depending on what you want to update. 2. Click the Columns dropdown menu and then select Customize columns. 3. Select the columns you want to see. If you want to save this customization to use again later, click to check the box that says Save as a column preset. 4. Click Apply." To reuse it: "click Columns and then select it from the Column presets section." — [Customize columns in Meta Ads Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1452248935049010) (read in full)
- Columns sits "next to Breakdown". — [Simplify metrics selection with customized columns](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/273560376590079) (read in full; Meta notes "we are gradually introducing this feature")
- Metric groups inside Customize columns: Performance ("results, reach, frequency and impressions"), Engagement ("page post, messaging, media, clicks and awareness"), Conversions, Settings ("start date, end date, ad set name, ad ID, delivery, bid and objective"). — [Customize columns](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1452248935049010) (read in full)
- Exact metric names confirmed in Meta's glossary pages: "CPM (cost per 1,000 impressions)" — calculated as "the amount spent on an ad campaign, divided by impressions, multiplied by 1,000". — [CPM (cost per 1,000 impressions)](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/753932008002620) (read in full)
- "CTR (link click-through rate)" — "calculated as link clicks divided by impressions". — [CTR (link click-through rate)](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/877711998984611) (read in full)
- "Link clicks", "Landing page views", "Cost per landing page view" and "CPC (cost per link click)" are the names Meta uses. Landing page views count clicks where the site "successfully loaded"; link clicks count clicks on links in the ad. Meta's listed reasons for a gap between the two: "The web page isn't fully loading" or multiple link destinations. — [Understand the difference between link clicks and landing page views](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/172641445757289) (read in full)
- "Cost per result" is "amount spent divided by results" and "can be affected by many factors, such as your auction bid, target audience, optimization type, ad creative and messaging, and schedule." — [Cost per result](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/762109693832964) (read in full)
- Two extra columns Meta tells you to add for learning: "Last significant edit" ("shows when you last made a change that could reset the learning phase. If you don't see this column, click Columns and add it to your view") and "Results". — [About the learning phase](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700) (read in full)
- To count optimisation events: "1. Open Columns. 2. Click Customize columns and check Optimization events. 3. Click Apply." — [About learning limited](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981) (read in full)

Delivery status, Learning and Learning limited
- "The Delivery column of Meta Ads Manager is where you can check the status of your campaign, ad set or ad and find information about any problems." Check all three levels because they can differ. — [View campaign, ad set or ad delivery status](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/650774041651557) (read in full)
- Meta's definitions: "Learning: The ad set and all of its ads are still in the learning phase ... Performance is less stable during this time period and CPAs are usually worse." "Learning limited: The ad set didn't generate enough results to exit the learning phase." "Active: The ad is running normally". Other statuses listed: Rejected, Scheduled, In review, Processing, Preparing, No ads, Ads off / Ad set off, Completed. — [View delivery status](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/650774041651557) (read in full)
- Where the reason appears: "You can hover over some statuses, like errors and warnings, to see more information about the status and if there are any actions to take." — [View delivery status](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/650774041651557) (read in full). And specifically: "If an ad set is limited by a low bid or cost control, a tooltip will appear when you hover over the Learning limited status." — [About learning limited](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981) (read in full)
- Exit rule: ad sets leave learning "after about 50 results in the week after the ad set's last significant edit." — [About the learning phase](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700) (read in full)
- Learning limited trigger and Meta's list of causes: "An ad set becomes learning limited when it is unlikely to receive about 50 optimization events in the week after your last significant edit ... generally, an ad set becomes learning limited when the ad set is limited by small audience size, low budget, low bid or cost control, high auction overlap, an infrequent optimization event, or other issues such as running too many ads at the same time." "Learning limited isn't a penalty". — [About learning limited](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981) (read in full)
- Meta's five fixes for Learning limited: combine ad sets and campaigns; expand your audience; raise your budget; raise your bid or cost control; "Change your optimization event. Consider choosing an optimization event that occurs more frequently. For example, move from purchases to add to cart." — [About learning limited](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981) (read in full)
- Mobile app: the Ads Manager app lists campaigns with their status (Active, Scheduled, In review, Learning, Not approved, Completed, Off) and has Filters by Status, Date and Campaigns. — [View delivery status in Meta Ads Manager app](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1057493129159441) (read in full; the tool dropped the icon names in steps 1-3)

Auction overlap and "Inspect"
- Auction overlap is about several ad sets from the same advertiser entering the same auction: "we choose the ad with the highest total value to compete ... other ads from that advertiser are not considered in this auction." It "can occur across" ad accounts belonging to the same advertiser. — [Understand auction overlap](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/537699989762051) (read in full)
- How Meta now says to diagnose it: "Use Opportunity score recommendations: in some cases, you may see a recommendation under Opportunity score in Account overview" and "Create an automated rule to automatically detect and manage auction overlap." No Inspect tool is mentioned. — [Understand auction overlap](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/537699989762051) (read in full)
- A metric called "Campaign auction overlap" exists; it counts "ad sets in a single campaign that have entered the same ad auction as one or more of the ad sets in the same campaign." — [Campaign auction overlap](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/957407462768373) (read in full)
- Meta's delivery troubleshooting also says to "Check the activity history of your ad set and look for changes to the setup that were made just before delivery slowed down". — [Troubleshoot ad delivery: your ad set slowed down or stopped](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/414038972972624) (read in full)
- Cost context from Meta: "Ads that are more relevant cost less and see more results." — [About ad relevance diagnostics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/403110480493160) (read in full). During learning, "ad sets are less stable and usually have a higher CPA." — [About the learning phase](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700) (read in full)

### Inferences
- With one campaign and one ad set, auction overlap inside the campaign cannot be the cause. It could only matter if the same Page or business runs other ad sets or accounts (for example the four ad sets created earlier in this project, if they are still on).
- The 50-results-a-week rule against this budget: £35 a day is about £245 a week, so the ad set can only reach 50 purchases a week if a purchase costs about £4.90 or less (245 divided by 50). If the real cost per purchase is higher, Meta's own definition says the ad set will be "Learning limited". This is arithmetic on Meta's rule, not a Meta statement.
- A sensible column preset built from confirmed names: Delivery, Results, Cost per result, Amount spent, Impressions, Reach, Frequency, CPM (cost per 1,000 impressions), Link clicks, CTR (link click-through rate), CPC (cost per link click), Landing page views, Cost per landing page view, Last significant edit, Optimization events, plus the three ranking columns in the next section.
- A large gap between Link clicks and Landing page views points at page load, not at the auction (Meta's listed reason).

### Gaps
- "Inspect" / "Delivery insights": a Help Center search for those terms returned no article describing either tool, and Meta's current auction overlap article does not mention them. Could not confirm that Inspect exists in late 2026; could not confirm current wording.
- Where exactly inside the Customize columns panel CPM, CTR (link) and CPC (link) sit (which sub-heading): not stated in the text read. Use the search box in that panel if one is shown; presence of a search box: could not confirm.
- The click path to "activity history": not given in the article read. Could not confirm current wording.
- No Meta article was found that explains "why is my CPM high" as such. Meta's documentation talks about cost per result, relevance and learning, not CPM causes.
- Whether the "Frequency" and "Reach" glossary wording has changed: glossary pages for those two were not returned; only their names in the Customize columns article were confirmed.

## Ad relevance diagnostics: where the three ranking columns are, minimum impressions, what each low ranking means

### Takeaway
The three rankings are columns at the Ads level, already included in the Performance column preset, and they do not appear for an ad with fewer than 500 impressions. Meta says to use them only to diagnose under-performing ads, and gives a table of what each low ranking means and what to change.

### Cited Findings
- Steps: "1. In Ads Manager, click the Ads tab. 2. Click Columns. In the dropdown, click Customize columns. 3. Select Quality ranking, Engagement rate ranking and Conversion rate ranking. 4. Click Apply." They "are default columns in the Performance column preset but must be added manually to custom column presets." — [About ad relevance diagnostics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/403110480493160) (read in full)
- Minimum: "ad relevance diagnostics aren't available for ads with fewer than 500 impressions." Also: "Ad relevance diagnostics aren't inputs into the ad auction." — [About ad relevance diagnostics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/403110480493160) (read in full)
- Definitions: Quality ranking is "how your ad's perceived quality compared to ads competing for the same audience"; Engagement rate ranking is "how your ad's expected engagement rate compared"; Conversion rate ranking is "how your ad's expected conversion rate compared to ads with the same optimization goal competing for the same audience." They cover "the date range you've selected". — [About ad relevance diagnostics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/403110480493160) (read in full)
- Possible values: Above average; Average ("average represents the 35th to 55th percentile"); Below average (bottom 35% of ads); Below average (bottom 20% of ads); Below average (bottom 10% of ads). "This metric is only available for the last 35 days." Engagement means the likelihood a person "will click, react to, comment on, share or expand an ad", and "engagement-baiting ... will not improve your ad's performance." Not available for "dynamic creative" or the optimisation goals "ad recall lift, impressions, reach, custom conversions or value." — [About engagement rate ranking](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2351270371824148) (read in full)
- What each pattern means and what Meta says to change (Quality / Engagement / Conversion): — [How to use ad relevance diagnostics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/436113280262012) (read in full)
  - All average or above: "You're all good! Optimize for your advertising objective."
  - Quality below average (others average or above): "The ad is perceived as low quality. Improve the quality of your creative assets or target an audience more likely to perceive the ad as high quality. Avoid low-quality attributes from your creative."
  - Engagement below average: "This ad isn't spurring interest. Improve your ad's relevance to your audience (for example, by making it more engaging, interesting or eye-catching) or target an audience more likely to interact with this ad."
  - Conversion below average: "The ad isn't producing conversions. Improve the call-to-action of your ad or post-click experience, or target a higher-intent audience. Some products and services naturally exhibit lower conversion rates than others."
  - Quality and Conversion below average, Engagement fine: "This ad is click-baity or controversial. Adjust your ad to more clearly represent the product or service you are advertising."
  - Engagement and Conversion below average: improve both relevance and "the call-to-action of your ad or post-click experience."
  - All three below average: "There's room for improvement across the board. Try testing different targeting strategies, creative, optimization goals, post-click experiences".
- Priorities: "It's more impactful to move a ranking from low to average than it is to move a ranking from average to above average". "Use ad relevance diagnostics to diagnose underperforming ads – not to optimize ads that are already meeting your advertising objectives." "Sometimes high performing ads have below average ad relevance diagnostics rankings and that's OK." — [How to use ad relevance diagnostics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/436113280262012) (read in full)

### Inferences
- With 10 ads sharing about £35 a day, several ads may sit under 500 impressions for the chosen date range and show no ranking. Widening the date range (up to the 35-day limit) is the only fix that does not involve an edit.
- A dash in a ranking column means "not enough data", not "bad".

### Gaps
- The separate "About quality ranking" and "About conversion rate ranking" articles were not returned; their value lists are assumed to match the engagement article but that was not read.
- Meta does not publish what the "low-quality attributes" are in the articles read here (a linked article exists but was not returned).

## Reducing the number of ads: turning ads off, whether that restarts learning, and Meta's guidance on ad volume

### Takeaway
Ads are switched off with the toggle on the Ads tab. Turning an existing ad off is NOT on Meta's published list of significant edits, but Meta does not say in so many words that it is safe; adding an ad or changing an ad's creative IS on the list. Meta's stated guidance is to "decrease ads per ad set, but maintain diverse creative assets".

### Cited Findings
- Steps: "1. Go to Meta Ads Manager. 2. Select the Campaigns, Ad sets or Ads tab at the top of the screen. 3. Click the toggle next to each campaign, ad set or ad you want to turn on or off." "When the status is blue the ad is on and when the status is gray the ad is off." Turning off "doesn't delete it" and "may take a few minutes" to stop delivery. Meta marks this page "This feature is only available on computer." — [How to turn an ad on or off in Meta Ads Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/172764286113080) (read in full)
- Meta's full list of significant edits: "any change to targeting; any change to ad creative; any change to optimization event; adding a new ad to your ad set; pausing your ad set for 7 days or longer (the ad set reenters the learning phase once you unpause the ad set); changing bid strategy". Edits that "may or may not be significant, depending on the magnitude of the change": "ad set spending limit amount; bid control, cost per result goal or ROAS goal amount; budget amount". Example: "if you increase your budget from $100 to $101, that isn't likely to cause one or more ad sets to reenter the learning phase. However, if you change your budget from $100 to $1000, one or more ad sets may reenter the learning phase." "Only a significant edit causes an ad set to reenter the learning phase." — [Significant edits and learning phase](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/316478108955072) (read in full)
- Ad volume: "When an advertiser runs too many ads at once, each ad delivers less often. This means that fewer ads exit the learning phase, and more budget is spent before the delivery system can optimize performance. In other words, too many ads can result in worse performance." — [About managing ad volume](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2720085414702598) (read in full)
- Meta's instruction: "Decrease ads per ad set, but maintain diverse creative assets per ad set. One ad can contain multiple (up to 10) creative assets." — [About managing ad volume](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2720085414702598) (read in full)
- "Avoid high ad volumes. When you create many ads and ad sets, the delivery system learns less about each ad and ad set". — [About the learning phase](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700) (read in full)
- "Running too many ads at the same time" is one of Meta's named causes of Learning limited. — [About learning limited](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981) (read in full)
- Meta does not give a number of ads per ad set in these articles. The ad volume article still recommends "dynamic creative" for creative variants, but the same page carries the notice that "starting in June 2024, you may no longer be able to use dynamic creative ... when you select Sales or App promotion as your objective ... using flexible ad format to achieve a similar result is recommended." — [About managing ad volume](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2720085414702598) (read in full)
- Edits cannot be undone and results are not split before/after: "Once you change any element of your published campaign, the new version replaces the old one. There's no option to reverse changes and no way to distinguish between the metrics from before and after edits are made." — [How to edit ad campaigns in Meta Ads Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2169779963333459) (read in full)

### Inferences
- Significant edit? Turning off individual ads: not listed by Meta as a significant edit. Because it is neither "any change to ad creative" nor "adding a new ad", the plain reading is that it does not restart learning, but Meta does not state this explicitly.
- Switching ads off (not deleting them) keeps the option to switch the same ad back on later; switching back on is also not on Meta's list.
- Merging several images into one ad would mean creating a new ad (a significant edit); simply switching off the weakest ads is the lower-risk way to cut volume.

### Gaps
- Meta gives no recommended count of ads per ad set in the articles read. Any specific number would come from practitioners, not Meta.
- Whether turning an ad off or back on restarts learning: not addressed directly by Meta. Could not confirm.
- Whether the Ads Manager mobile app can toggle individual ads (as opposed to whole campaigns): Meta's ad-level article says computer only; the app article covers campaigns only.

## Audience: editing Advantage+ audience, controls versus suggestions, minimum age, maximum age, account-level controls

### Takeaway
In Advantage+ audience, minimum age is a hard "control" but the upper age and the 25-65 range are only a "suggestion" that Meta may ignore. The maximum age can only be enforced by choosing "Further limit the reach of your ads" and unticking "Use as a suggestion", which Meta says may turn Advantage+ audience off. Any targeting change is a significant edit.

### Cited Findings
- Controls: "Audience controls limit who can see your ads. You can choose locations, minimum age, custom audiences to exclude and languages." — [About audience controls and audience suggestions in Advantage+ audience](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/938372127764391) (read in full)
- Suggestions: "You can suggest age, gender, detailed targeting and custom audiences to include. Suggestions don't always constrain your audience. For example, if you suggest the gender women, it's possible that your ads could also deliver to men if Meta's AI finds them likely to respond." "Audience suggestions won't be shown outside your audience controls." — [About audience controls and audience suggestions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/938372127764391) (read in full)
- Enforcing an age range: "If reaching a specific audience is more important than getting best performance, select Further limit your audience or Further limit the reach of your ads to select age, gender, detailed targeting and custom audience inclusions as audience controls. You may have to uncheck the box Use as a suggestion." — [About audience controls and audience suggestions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/938372127764391) (read in full)
- Where the settings are, as Meta writes the steps for Sales campaigns: "Find Audiences at the ad set level." "(Optional) Select controls ... You can select: Location to edit the location. Show more controls to add a minimum age, custom audience exclusion and languages." "(Optional) Select Add suggestions, under Suggest an audience ... You can select: custom audience inclusions, age, gender, detailed targeting." "(Optional) Select Further limit the reach of your ads ... You can uncheck Use as a suggestion for these settings: age, gender, custom audience inclusions". — [Choose audience settings in Advantage+ campaigns](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/25941857932125812) (read in full; steps are written for creating a campaign)
- Cost of enforcing: "Only remove a suggestion to reflect business needs, so you don't miss any opportunities to maximize performance. If you uncheck the box to remove suggestions, you may no longer be using Advantage+ audience. The audience section will show Advantage+ off." — [Choose audience settings in Advantage+ campaigns](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/25941857932125812) (read in full)
- To edit an existing ad set: Ad sets tab, tick the ad set, click Edit above the table, then the Audience section ("Audience: make changes to your audience targeting (such as age, gender and location demographics)"), then Publish. — [How to edit ad campaigns in Meta Ads Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2169779963333459) (read in full)
- Significant edit: "any change to targeting" restarts learning. — [Significant edits and learning phase](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/316478108955072) (read in full)
- Account-level controls live in Advertising settings: "1. Go to Advertising settings. 2. Click Audience controls. 3. (Optional) Toggle on My business can only advertise in specific locations ... 4. (Optional) Toggle on My business sells or advertises age-restricted goods or services. Select the Minimum age dropdown to choose an age. 5. (Optional) Toggle on My business restricts advertising to some audiences to protect its brand ... 6. (Optional) Toggle on My business doesn't show ads to its own employees ... 7. Click Review changes. 8. Check your settings. Select Apply if they are correct." — [Set audience and placement controls for your ad account](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1438478636941047) (read in full; Meta notes "we are gradually introducing this feature. It may not be available to you right now.")
- Scope of account controls: "Controls set in Advertising settings will be applied to all existing campaigns in your ad account." "Changes to existing campaigns may take up to 48 hours." "These settings cannot be changed in Ads Manager creation." — [Set audience and placement controls for your ad account](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1438478636941047) (read in full)
- Advertising settings is reached from Ads Manager: "Go to Ads Manager. On the left, click Advertising settings." — [How to set up value rules in Meta Ads Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1172579563848778) (read in full)
- Meta's alternative to hard age limits is value rules (bid more or less for an age group) — "Audience controls: when you have hard requirements ... Value rules: when you're willing to bid more for higher value customers ... Audience suggestions: when you have preferences, not strict rules". — [Use audience suggestions, audience controls and value rules](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2016171032241412) (read in full)
- Meta's cost guidance runs the other way from narrowing: "Expand your audience: if your audience is too specific it may cost more to reach your relevant audience." — [Best practices to potentially reduce cost per result](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/321695409726523) (read in full)

### Inferences
- Minimum age: can be enforced, at the ad set (Show more controls) or the account (Advertising settings > Audience controls). Maximum age: cannot be enforced while staying on Advantage+ audience; enforcing it means unticking "Use as a suggestion", which is both a significant edit and, per Meta, likely to show "Advantage+ off".
- The age breakdown from the Diagnosis section shows whether delivery is actually leaking outside 25-65 before deciding to change anything.
- Setting the minimum age to 25 as a control at ad set level is still "any change to targeting", so it is a significant edit.

### Gaps
- The highest value the Minimum age control accepts (Meta has historically capped it): not stated in the text read. Could not confirm.
- Whether changing an account-level audience control restarts learning on existing ad sets: not stated by Meta. It changes targeting for "all existing campaigns", so treat it as likely significant.
- The exact label seen when editing an existing ad set (Meta shows both "Further limit your audience" and "Further limit the reach of your ads"): could not confirm which one this account shows.

## Placements: what can still be changed for a Sales campaign, exclusions at ad set versus account level, Audience Network, creative per placement

### Takeaway
Meta's late-2026 documentation says that for Sales campaigns "you no longer choose placements manually at the ad set level" (rolling out gradually); the remaining levers are account-level placement controls in Advertising settings (which include both Audience Network options), placement value rules, and giving each placement its own media. Meta's own position is that excluding placements raises cost.

### Cited Findings
- The change: "We're introducing updates to how placement settings are managed for campaigns using the following objectives: Leads, App promotion, Sales. For these objectives, you no longer choose placements manually at the ad set level." Awareness, Traffic and Engagement are not affected. Meta notes "This feature is being rolled out gradually and may not be available to you yet." — [What's new in placement settings in Meta Ads Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1546753450586085) (read in full)
- The three options Meta lists instead: "Customize media for each placement in ad creative; Increase or decrease bids for placements ... by using placement value rules; Set account-level placement exclusions by objective in account controls." — [What's new in placement settings](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1546753450586085) (read in full)
- If the account has NOT yet received that change, Meta's steps for a Sales ad set are: in the Placements section "you may see the Advantage+ on label ... Click Show more settings." ## Events Manager: event match quality, Conversions API status, deduplication, data-source restriction notices

### Takeaway
All four checks are in Events Manager under the dataset: per-event "View details" tabs cover match quality, coverage, deduplication and freshness; the Diagnostics tab flags connection problems; and the Settings tab has a "Manage data source categories" section that shows whether Meta has put the site in a restricted category such as "Health and wellness", which matters for a sleep product.

### Cited Findings
- Path to event details: "1. Go to Events Manager. 2. Select the name and ID of your data. 3. Find the event you want to see details for and click View details. 4. Select a tab". Tabs: Event overview, Event coverage, Event matching (also called the "event quality tab" in the same article), Event deduplication, Data freshness. These exist only "for website events sent through the Conversions API". — [View server event details in Meta Events Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1541268312717919) (read in full)
- Event match quality: "Meta calculates a score from 0 to 10 based on the quality of customer information you're sending for a specific server event and the percentage of event instances matched to Meta accounts." "The last 48 hours of data are used". It "is available for website events sent through the Conversions API". "Better event match quality ... can result in better ad performance and lower cost per action." Highest-priority parameters: Email and Click ID. — [About event match quality](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/765081237991954) (read in full)
- Conversions API status and redundancy: the Event overview tab shows events received from the pixel (browser) and the Conversions API (server). "Events from the Conversions API should match or exceed those from the Meta pixel." Event coverage: "Advertisers should aim for a 75% event coverage ratio of Conversions API to Meta pixel events." — [View server event details](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1541268312717919) (read in full)
- Connection problems: "Regularly monitor your Conversions API connection in Events Manager. Hover over the ! icon in the Diagnostics tab for more information about an issue and recommendations for improvement." Also "Verify events using the Test events tool." — [Monitor and improve your Conversions API setup](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/586304118741779) (read in full)
- Deduplication tab: "The deduplication keys table shows the percentages of events from the pixel and the Conversions API that were received with each deduplication key ... The higher the percentage, the better." "Overlap refers to the percentage of events with a given deduplication key that are received by both". — [View server event details](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1541268312717919) (read in full)
- How deduplication works: the event_name must match and "the event_id must be identical for both events"; otherwise event_name plus external_id or fbp. "Partner integrations: many platforms (e.g., Shopify, WooCommerce) automatically handle deduplication parameters." - Data source category check: "1. Go to Meta Events Manager. 2. Click Datasets in the left menu of the page. 3. Select the name and ID of your data. 4. Click the Settings tab at the top of the page. 5. Scroll to the Manage data source categories section. You may not see the Manage data source categories section if your data sources are not in any of the categories. 6. Click Manage. 7. Check your data sources, categories and statuses." - Health and wellness definition: "is associated with medical conditions or specific health statuses, provider/patient relationships, services for accessing personal health information, or health-related products and services (examples include, a patient portal, wellness tracker, telemedicine platform, pharmacy, health insurance marketplace, or condition-specific support group)." — [About data source categories in Meta Events Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1402913027039332) (read in full)
- What a restriction does: "Core setup: restricts the sharing of custom parameters and parts of URLs following the domain. Restriction on certain standard events: restricts the sharing of specific mid and lower funnel events. Full restrictions: fully restrict the sharing of all events". Notice arrives "via email and notifications in Meta Events Manager". Under event restrictions Meta suggests "leveraging more upper-funnel events". — [Understand data sharing restrictions based on data source categories](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698) (read in full)

### Inferences
- If the "Manage data source categories" section is absent from the Settings tab, Meta's wording means the site is not in a restricted category.
- If the store's dataset has been categorised "Health and wellness" with a restriction on lower-funnel standard events, Purchase data could be limited or blocked, which would starve a Purchase-optimised ad set of signal and show up as poor delivery and high cost. This is a reasoned link, not a Meta statement about this account.

### Gaps

- Which specific standard events are restricted under "Restriction on certain standard events": not listed in the articles read.
- The exact name of the top-level tabs on the dataset page (Overview, Test events, Diagnostics, Settings): only "Diagnostics tab", "Settings tab" and "Test events tool" are named in the text read; any others could not be confirmed.
