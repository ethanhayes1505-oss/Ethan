## 1. What are Meta's data source category restrictions (categories, what is restricted, how assigned, how to see it, how to request review)?

### Takeaway
Meta assigns website domains/apps that send pixel or Conversions API data to "data source categories"; "Health and wellness" is one of twelve named categories that "may have certain data sharing restrictions", and the restriction comes in three levels (core setup, restriction on "specific mid and lower funnel events", full restriction). The category is visible in Events Manager > Datasets > [dataset] > Settings > "Manage data source categories", and Meta says that section may not appear at all if the data source is in none of the categories; a "Request review" button exists there for Meta-assigned categories.

### Cited Findings

**Official (Meta Help Center)**

- Purpose and mechanism: "As part of our ongoing efforts to help protect users' privacy, we categorize data sources, such as website domains and mobile apps, that send us data through the Meta Business Tools. These categories help us better understand what topics are related to your business so that we can tailor your product experience." — [About data source categories in Meta Events Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1402913027039332) [HC, read in full]
- How a category is assigned: "We may assign a category to data sources, such as websites and apps, that share data with us through the Meta Business Tools based on the topics related to the data source and the products and/or services provided. Certain data source categories may have additional data sharing restrictions." — [About data source categories](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1402913027039332) [HC, read in full]
- Health and wellness definition (current wording): "Health and wellness: Is associated with medical conditions or specific health statuses, provider/patient relationships, services for accessing personal health information, or health-related products and services (examples include, a patient portal, wellness tracker, telemedicine platform, pharmacy, health insurance marketplace, or condition-specific support group)." — [About data source categories](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1402913027039332) [HC, read in full]
- The full list of categories "that may have certain data sharing restrictions include, but are not limited to": Health and wellness; Financial service; Unsuitable content; Politics; Race; Religion; Sexuality and gender identity; Gender identity; Nationality; Trade union; Personal hardship; Economic vulnerability. "Note: This list is not exhaustive and may evolve over time." — [About data source categories](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1402913027039332) [HC, read in full]
- Three restriction types, quoted: "Core setup: Restricts the sharing of custom parameters and parts of URLs following the domain." / "Restriction on certain standard events: Restricts the sharing of specific mid and lower funnel events." / "Full restrictions: Fully restrict the sharing of all events in specific regions or all regions. In these circumstances, Meta Business Tools cannot be used for ads purposes where restrictions are in place." — [Understand data sharing restrictions based on data source categories](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698) [HC, read in full]
- Restrictions vary: "These restrictions may vary across data source categories and can be specific to certain countries or regions, or could be applied globally." — [Understand data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698) [HC, read in full]
- Custom events: "Note: If your Meta Business Tool has data restrictions, custom events are automatically blocked until you review and confirm them, or they may not be available to you (for example, if your Meta Business Tools have full restrictions)." — [Understand data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698) [HC, read in full]
- Notification: "If we decide to limit or fully restrict a data source from sharing event data with us, we will inform you via email and notifications in Meta Events Manager." — [Understand data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698) [HC, read in full]
- Meta's suggested response per level: for restricted standard events, "Evaluate which events are restricted and explore alternative events that can help you meet your business objectives ... For example, you may want to consider leveraging more upper-funnel events to maintain effective audience targeting and campaign optimization."; for full restrictions, "Consider adjusting your campaign to optimize for certain available campaign objectives that do not rely on data shared through the Meta Business Tools, such as awareness (e.g., reach, impressions), engagements (e.g., likes, comments, shares), and traffic (e.g., link clicks)."; for core setup, "While most campaigns should continue to run as expected, you may see limitations in certain advertiser experiences". — [Understand data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698) [HC, read in full]
- What core setup removes: "Core setup restricts the transmission of custom parameters and anything in a URL following the domain." Example given: "the URL https://jaspersmarket.com/clothes/summer/dresses?item=10 would be shortened to 'https://jaspersmarket.com/'." Standard parameters such as "value and currency parameters for a purchase event, or the content_id parameter for a view content event" are not custom parameters. — [About core setup](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/124742407297678) [HC, read in full]
- Core setup side-effects listed by Meta: custom audiences that rely on custom parameters or URL rules "can't be prefilled and may take longer to populate or not be available"; "Automatic advanced matching may not be available"; "Adding items to your catalog via Meta Pixel may no longer work"; custom events need to be reviewed and confirmed. — [About core setup](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/124742407297678) [HC, read in full]
- Four reasons core setup can be on: Meta determined the data source falls in a category that requires it; the advertiser self-assigned a restricted category; "You received multiple notifications that data you're sharing potentially goes against the Meta Business Tools terms"; or the advertiser turned it on. — [About core setup](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/124742407297678) [HC, read in full]
- Where to see the category (exact steps): "1. Go to Meta Events Manager. 2. Click Datasets in the left menu of the page. 3. Select the name and ID of your data. 4. Click the Settings tab at the top of the page. 5. Scroll to the Manage data source categories section. You may not see the Manage data source categories section if your data sources are not in any of the categories. 6. Click Manage. 7. Check your data sources, categories and statuses. You may not see data sources in this tab if they are not in any of the categories with data sharing restrictions." — [How to manage data source categories in Meta Events Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/467621355878794) [HC, read in full]
- How to request a review: "If Meta has assigned a category to your data source and you think the categorization is not accurate, click View details and then click Request review. You'll see a message that says that you've successfully requested the review." and "If you requested a review, you'll be notified through email of our decision." Permission needed: "full control of the business portfolio or an admin role on the ad account that owns the dataset". — [How to manage data source categories](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/467621355878794) [HC, read in full]
- Self-categorisation exists and is optional; it is a second place to look ("Dataset categories" in the same Settings tab: "Note: If you haven't assigned a category yet, you will see None."). Meta-assigned categories cannot be edited by the advertiser: "you will not be able to modify a Meta-assigned categorization for your data source." If both exist, "the categories that come with the most restrictions will be applied". — [How to self-categorize your datasets and data sources in Meta Events Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1666235380728268) [HC, read in full]
- Custom audiences and custom conversions are covered by separate flagging rules (not by the category itself): "Your custom audiences or lookalike audiences will be flagged and cannot be used for running ad campaigns if they include information that is not allowed under our terms. For example, references to specific health conditions (e.g., arthritis) or financial status (e.g., credit score) aren't allowed." Review can be requested "via Ads Manager under the campaigns table" or in Audience Manager / the Custom Conversions page. — [Understand restrictions on certain custom audiences](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1055828013359808) and [Understand restrictions on certain custom conversions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2455915321411996) [HC, both read in full]

**Practitioner / press**

- Trade press reported in December 2024 that Meta planned the change and that Meta's memo defined the health and wellness category as "associated with medical conditions, specific health statuses or provider/patient relationship". — [Marketing Brew, 9 Dec 2024](https://marketingbrew.com/stories/2024/12/09/meta-plans-crackdown-on-health-related-user-data) [excerpt only]
- Vendor write-ups describe the same three tiers under their own names ("Core Setup (least restrictive), Mid-Restricted, and Full-Restricted"; "Mild ... Moderate ... Severe") and say the checks apply to both the Pixel and the Conversions API. — [Stape news, dated 5 Oct 2026 in search results](https://stape.io/news/meta-data-sharing-restrictions-healthcare); [Stape blog, dated 2 Oct 2026](https://stape.io/blog/healthcare-ads-facebook-conversion-tracking) [excerpt only; which Stape page carries which phrase was not confirmed]
- Vendors say that under the middle tier "lower funnel standard events (e.g., purchases) cannot be sent", and that brands classified as health and wellness lost bottom-of-funnel event tracking from early 2025. — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-health-and-wellness-brands); [CustomerLabs docs](https://www.customerlabs.com/docs/destinations/ad-platforms/meta-health-and-wellness/restrictions/) [excerpt only]
- A vendor rule of thumb for reading the Events Manager label: "If you see 'care portal' you are fully restricted. Any other labels are partially restricted." — appears in search excerpts attributed to [Our Privacy](https://oursprivacy.com/meta) / [CustomerLabs docs](https://www.customerlabs.com/docs/destinations/ad-platforms/meta-health-and-wellness/restrictions/) [excerpt only; exact source page not confirmed; **not in Meta's documentation that I read**]
- Vendors report that switching to Conversions API or a first-party tracking tool does not bypass the restriction. — [Stape blog](https://stape.io/blog/healthcare-ads-facebook-conversion-tracking) [excerpt only]
- A LiveRamp help page title indicates Meta announced further "restrictions on certain custom audiences and custom conversions" around 17 July 2025. — [LiveRamp docs](https://docs.liveramp.com/connect/en/announcement--upcoming-meta-restrictions-on-certain-custom-audiences-and-custom-conversions--7-17-25-.html) [excerpt only; title seen, body not read]

### Inferences
- Meta's Help Center never names "Purchase" as a restricted event. It says "specific mid and lower funnel events". That Purchase is among them is a practitioner statement (consistent across several vendors, but excerpt only).
- The current Help Center definition is broader than the December 2024 wording reported by Marketing Brew: it now adds "services for accessing personal health information, or health-related products and services" and names "wellness tracker" as an example. This suggests the definition was widened after launch; I could not confirm when, because the Help Center text I received carries no dates.
- "Introduced January 2025" is supported only by press/vendor excerpts (Marketing Brew Dec 2024 "plans"; vendor pages dated/updated January 2025). Meta's own pages, as read, give no start date.

### Gaps
- The exact list of standard events blocked under "Restriction on certain standard events" is not given in any Meta page I read.
- No Meta page I read states how the classifier decides (site text, product catalogue, ad content, event names, human review). Meta says only "based on the topics related to the data source and the products and/or services provided".
- Article "last updated" dates could not be read; anything above may have changed between its writing and November 2026.

## 2. Would a sleep product store plausibly be categorised as health and wellness?

### Takeaway
It is plausible but not established: Meta's definition now includes "health-related products and services" and lists "wellness tracker" as an example, which sits close to a sleep wristband store, yet I found no documented case of a sleep-gadget or silent-alarm store being categorised. The only reliable answer is the store's own Events Manager Settings tab.

### Cited Findings

**Official**

- Meta's examples of health and wellness data sources: "a patient portal, wellness tracker, telemedicine platform, pharmacy, health insurance marketplace, or condition-specific support group". — [About data source categories](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1402913027039332) [HC, read in full]
- Meta's list of health information that must not be shared through the pixel includes: "Diseases, medical conditions and injuries"; "Mental health and psychological states"; "Types of medical condition devices and medical condition trackers"; "Prescription medications, and over-the-counter (OTC) and supplements for specific medical conditions"; "Body specifications, bodily activities, and biological cycles". — [About prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809) [HC, read in full]
- Event, audience and conversion names matter too: "the names you choose and criteria you establish for your events, conversions, and custom audiences must not reflect, imply, or be based on any prohibited information." Things Meta tells advertisers to check include "product IDs, content names, custom properties and URL parameters (including ... UTM parameters)". — [About prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809) [HC, read in full]
- Meta can categorise regardless of what the advertiser selects: "Meta may also continue to categorize your data sources including assigning your data source to a category that comes with restrictions." — [About data source categories](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1402913027039332) [HC, read in full]

**Practitioner / press**

- A vendor cites "a sleep-tracking app that runs ads for paid subscriptions" as the kind of business that could be affected. This is the only sleep-specific example found, it is an app not a physical gadget store, and it is a hypothetical illustration rather than a reported case. — [Stape](https://stape.io/blog/healthcare-ads-facebook-conversion-tracking) [excerpt only]
- Search summaries of law-firm and vendor commentary describe the category as broad: it "can include supplement or device sellers, telehealth practices, pharmacies, fitness or dieting programs, and mental health services", and can apply "even to general wellness or medical benefits businesses, not only those treating specific conditions". — candidates for the source are [Foley Hoag, January 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/security-privacy-and-the-law/2025/january/meta-s-new-advertising-rules-key-considerations-for-health-and-wellness-businesses/) and [SmartNews Ads blog](https://ads.smartnews.com/home/us/en/blog/metas-2025-advertising-shift-what-health-and-wellness-brands-need-to-know) [excerpt only; which page said which phrase not confirmed]
- A contrary vendor view: "General wellness products may be less likely to fall in the category", and misclassified brands can appeal. — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-health-and-wellness-brands) [excerpt only]
- Vendors give a supplement example: a purchase of a supplement for a specific medical condition "could reveal sensitive health information", and a supplement brand "might have to switch to ViewContent or another permitted event". — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-health-and-wellness-brands); [wetracked.io](https://www.wetracked.io/post/meta-health-and-wellness-restrictions) [excerpt only]
- A Shopify Community thread titled "Meta pixel restriction on health and wellness category" exists, showing ordinary Shopify merchants were hit. — [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/meta-pixel-restriction-on-health-and-wellness-category/394715) [excerpt only; title seen, posts not read, so I do not know what those stores sold]

### Inferences
- Factors that would push this store toward the category: the word "sleep" framed as a problem or condition on the site (insomnia, sleep disorder, hearing loss/deaf, tinnitus, apnea), any tracking function, product or collection URLs and event `content_name` values containing condition words, and custom audience / custom conversion names that mention a condition.
- Factors that pull away: the product is an alarm (a clock function), not a tracker; no medical claims; price point and consumer-gadget positioning. A silent vibrating alarm marketed as "wake up without waking your partner" reads as a lifestyle gadget; the same product marketed "for the hard of hearing" or "for insomnia" reads as health-related.
- "Wellness tracker" in Meta's example list is the single closest official hook to this product, and it describes a tracker, which this wristband (as described: an alarm, not a tracker) is not.

### Gaps
- I found no first-hand practitioner report (blog, forum or Reddit) of a sleep gadget, mattress, sleep-aid, fitness-tracker or wellness-device store being categorised. Searches specifically for "sleep", "mattress", "sleep aid" and "wearable" returned only general guides. Absence of a report is not evidence it does not happen.
- No Reddit threads surfaced in search and none could be fetched.
- Jon Loomer's post "What's going on with health and wellness??" ([URL](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/health-and-wellness-restrictions/)) exists but could not be opened; its content is unknown to me.

## 3. Symptoms of a restricted data source, and does restriction raise CPM or only block optimisation?

### Takeaway
Meta documents notification by email and in Events Manager, blocked custom events, missing parameters/URLs, and lower-funnel events no longer being shared; **none of the Meta data-source-category pages I read says anything about CPM, cost or auction price**. If the store's Purchase events are arriving in Events Manager and the Sales campaign is optimising for and reporting Purchases, a "restricted standard events" or "full" restriction is very unlikely to be the explanation for a $70 CPM.

### Cited Findings

**Official (documented effects)**

- You are told: "we will inform you via email and notifications in Meta Events Manager." — [Understand data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698) [HC, read in full]
- For prohibited-information detections generally: "you may receive notifications via email or in-product alerts in Meta Events Manager or in Meta Ads Manager ... More detailed information about the issue can be found in the Diagnostics tab for the relevant dataset in Events Manager." — [About prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809) [HC, read in full]
- Under full restriction the pixel is unusable for ads: "Meta Business Tools cannot be used for ads purposes where restrictions are in place." — [Understand data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698) [HC, read in full]
- Under core setup: "Information may be unavailable in Meta Events Manager and other surfaces. For example, custom parameters and anything in a URL after the domain. This includes sampled activities and the test events tool."; "Your ad sets may be affected if a custom audience decreases, or can no longer be updated because your Meta Business Tool is in a core setup." — [About core setup](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/124742407297678) [HC, read in full]
- Flagged custom conversions/audiences: "You won't be able to use flagged custom conversions when creating new campaigns."; for an active campaign using a flagged audience "you should edit or pause it and choose a different audience to avoid performance and delivery issues." — [custom conversions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2455915321411996); [custom audiences](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1055828013359808) [HC, both read in full]
- The only performance language in the category articles is general: the suggested adjustments are offered "to help prevent performance and delivery issues over time". Sharing prohibited information "can lead to a variety of consequences, such as performance issues, data restrictions, or suspension or termination of your use of our products and services". — [Understand data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698); [About prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809) [HC, both read in full]
- **Meta's documentation does not say** that a health-and-wellness category, core setup, or an event restriction raises CPM or changes auction price. The words CPM, cost and price do not appear in the five data-source-category/core-setup/prohibited-information articles read. — [1402913027039332](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1402913027039332), [511197658391698](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698), [124742407297678](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/124742407297678), [467621355878794](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/467621355878794), [361948878201809](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809) [HC, all read in full]
- Separate, related Meta statement about an ad set that cannot get enough optimisation events: "Learning limited isn't a penalty - it's an indication that your budget isn't being spent effectively because the ad delivery system can't optimize performance with your current setup." An ad set becomes learning limited "when it is unlikely to receive about 50 optimization events in the week after your last significant edit." — [About learning limited](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981) [HC, read in full]

**Practitioner (reported or expected effects)**

- Vendors report the practical symptom as losing the ability to send or optimise for lower-funnel events such as purchases, checkouts and leads. — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-health-and-wellness-brands); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/meta-s-targeted-healthcare-advertising-changes-go-effect) [excerpt only]
- Vendor description of the Events Manager screen: restrictions "also appear under the Settings tab in a Data restrictions section"; a screenshot is described with "a red block icon and Review rejected status" and "an orange warning triangle". — from search summaries of [CustomerLabs docs](https://www.customerlabs.com/docs/destinations/ad-platforms/meta-health-and-wellness/restrictions/) / [Elevar docs](https://docs.getelevar.com/docs/navigating-metas-restricted-events) [excerpt only; exact page not confirmed]
- Under restricted categories "all their custom events must be registered in Events Manager, or they will be dropped." — vendor excerpt, likely [Stape](https://stape.io/blog/healthcare-ads-facebook-conversion-tracking) or [Elevar docs](https://docs.getelevar.com/docs/navigating-metas-restricted-events) [excerpt only; source page not confirmed]
- Cost effect is stated only as expectation, not measurement: a healthcare agency blog argues weaker data leads to broader, less efficient reach so CPA and ROI may worsen; media buyers quoted by Digiday expected affected brands to spend less on Meta. — [Impression Digital](https://www.impressiondigital.com/blog/meta-ads-limiting-measurement-for-healthcare-advertisers/); [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/as-health-and-wellness-brands-brace-for-ad-restrictions-on-meta-marketers-and-advertisers-seek-more-transparency) [excerpt only]
- Review success: one vendor says "across the accounts we manage, we see successful appeals very infrequently", and another that you should "request a review if the option is available". — search summary across [Elevar docs](https://docs.getelevar.com/docs/navigating-metas-restricted-events), [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-health-and-wellness-brands), [Digital Position](https://digitalposition.com/resources/blog/ppc/metas-new-restrictions-on-health-and-wellness-ads-what-you-need-to-know/) [excerpt only; which vendor said it not confirmed]

### Inferences
- Mechanism separation. A restricted data source removes signal (events, parameters, audiences). It is not described anywhere as a surcharge. CPM is the price of the impressions the system chooses to buy; a restriction could change CPM only indirectly, by forcing a different optimisation event or audience. With Purchase optimisation intact, that indirect path is not in play.
- A quick self-test follows from Meta's own wording: (a) if "Manage data source categories" is absent from the dataset's Settings tab, the data source is "not in any of the categories"; (b) if Purchase events with value/currency show as received in the dataset Overview and the ad set's Purchase optimisation publishes without error, the mid/lower-funnel event restriction is not applied.
- Core setup alone could be present without the owner noticing (Purchase still works). Its documented effects are on URL/custom-parameter-based audiences, automatic advanced matching and pixel-fed catalogues. Loss of automatic advanced matching could lower match quality, which could weaken optimisation; Meta does not quantify this and does not link it to CPM.
- Purchase-optimised campaigns generally buy more expensive impressions than traffic or awareness campaigns because they bid for people predicted to buy; that is ordinary auction behaviour and is a more likely contributor to a high CPM than a category restriction. (No source read here quantifies it; another researcher's notes should cover benchmarks.)

### Gaps
- No measured data (case study, benchmark, Meta statement) was found on CPM before/after a health-and-wellness restriction. Search specifically for CPM/CPA effects returned only vendor expectations.
- The exact wording of any Ads Manager warning shown when a restricted event is selected for optimisation was not found in Meta's pages or in excerpts.
- Whether Meta's auction applies any separate cost adjustment to advertisers in a restricted data source category is not documented in anything I read; I have no evidence either way.

## 4. Advertising standards that touch sleep products, and how policy issues or negative feedback affect ad quality and auction cost

### Takeaway
The ad-content rules most relevant to a sleep gadget are Personal Attributes (do not imply the viewer has a health condition or disability) and the Health and Wellness ad policy's ban on health clickbait and time-bound outcome promises; none of them restricts a non-medical alarm wristband as a product. Meta states plainly that low ad quality, repeated violations, incomplete advertiser verification and poor customer feedback make ads "cost more", and this, unlike the data-source category, is a documented cost mechanism.

### Cited Findings

**Official: content policies**

- Personal attributes: "We don't allow advertisers to run ads that share or ask for private information about users or assert or imply the personal attributes of the viewer of an ad". Prohibited attributes include "Disability", "Medical or genetic condition" and "Physical or mental health (including medical conditions)". "We also don't allow ads that assert or imply personal attributes in alternative ways. For example, we don't allow ads that ask questions about personal attributes." "You can use the words 'you' or 'your' as long as your ad doesn't mention any prohibited personal attributes". — [About Meta's Privacy Violations and Personal Attributes advertising policy](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2557868957763449) [HC, read in full]
- Meta's violating examples: "Are you disabled? We can help!"; "Have you been diagnosed with cancer? Come to our clinic for treatment."; "If you are tired of dealing with depression, call us for free therapy sessions." Suggested alternative: "Provide information on your product or service and the unique benefits it provides." — [Personal Attributes policy](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2557868957763449) [HC, read in full]
- Meta's stated consequence of personal-attribute wording: "it can lead to negative experiences and lower ad engagement (for example, fewer clicks, views and conversions)." — [Personal Attributes policy](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2557868957763449) [HC, read in full]
- Health and Wellness ad policy (the Help Center page for what was called Personal Health and Appearance): "we don't allow ads that imply or attempt to generate negative self-perception in order to promote diet, weight loss, or other health related products." Advertisers can't run ads that "employ clickbait tactics in a health, weight loss or weight gain context, such as sensational language with exaggerated or extreme claims, or promises of specific outcomes within a set timeframe without disclaimers or qualifiers." — [About Meta's Health and Wellness advertising policy](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2489235377779939) [HC, read in full]
- Cure claims list is specific and "exhaustive": diabetes, herpes, thyroid, psoriasis, ebola, cancer, autism, Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, ALS, HIV. Sleep conditions are not on it. — [Health and Wellness advertising policy](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2489235377779939) [HC, read in full]
- The "wearable" clause is about weight: ads can't "claim that results can be achieved solely by use of wearable products" in the context of "weight loss or weight gain products or services". — [Health and Wellness advertising policy](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2489235377779939) [HC, read in full]
- Before/after: the Help Center page allows, for 18+ audiences, "general cosmetic products, procedures or surgeries that depict before and after transformations" and shows before/after skin-whitening images as non-compliant. It contains no general rule on before/after imagery for non-cosmetic gadgets. — [Health and Wellness advertising policy](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2489235377779939) [HC, read in full]
- Transparency Center policy pages exist for Health and Wellness, Personal Health and Appearance, Unrealistic Outcomes and Privacy Violations and Personal Attributes; search excerpts say Unrealistic Outcomes covers "claims of unrealistic results within specific timeframes and exaggerated claims". — [Health and Wellness](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness); [Unrealistic Outcomes](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/deceptive-content/unrealistic-outcomes); [Personal Health and Appearance](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/personal-health-and-appearance); [Privacy Violations and Personal Attributes](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/) [excerpt only; transparency.meta.com is blocked in this session]

**Official: how quality and feedback affect cost**

- Auction formula: the winner is "the ad with the highest total value", a combination of "Bid", "Estimated action rates" and "Ad quality: A measure of the quality of an ad as determined from many sources including feedback from people viewing or hiding the ad and assessments of low-quality attributes in the ad, such as withholding information, sensationalized language and engagement bait." — [About ad auctions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/430291176997542) [HC, read in full]
- Cost statement: "Ads with a lower quality ranking tend to cost more, which may reduce distribution of the ads and lead to fewer results." — [About ad quality for Meta ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/423781975167984) [HC, read in full]
- Account-level spillover: "if you repeatedly post policy-violating or lower quality ads, or if you have not completed advertiser verification, our systems may start considering all ads from your Page, domain, ad account or other associated entities as lower quality." And: "Lower-quality associations mean that ads will cost more and advertisers will see fewer results than if the ads did not contain these attributes and associations." — [About ad quality](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/423781975167984); [Best practices to improve Meta ad quality and performance](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1767120243598011) [HC, both read in full]
- Violations themselves lead to rejection, not a surcharge: "If we detect that an ad violates our Advertising Standards, we reject it." "Editing or deleting an ad that was rejected ... will not remove the violation from your account and you can be restricted from advertising for severe or repeat ad violations." "If your ad rejection is successfully overturned, this will not count as an ad violation." — [About ad quality](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/423781975167984); [How to troubleshoot a rejected ad](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1210227555661027) [HC, both read in full]
- Customer feedback: "Customer experience score is one of several signals used in the ad auction to help determine which ads are shown to people. Businesses may see increased costs or improved competitiveness in the auction depending on their score." Feedback is collected by survey about "unexpected charges, shipping delays, or product quality concerns, as well as any refund attempts and outcomes." — [About customer experience score](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1491186119412011) [HC, read in full]
- Meta reserves broad auction adjustments: "We may use data about you and your ad account to make adjustments that could affect auction outcomes and prices, such as to improve ad quality and relevance ... enforce our terms, policies and other standards ... But such auction adjustments will not cause us to charge you more than your bid to show your ad." — [About ad auctions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/430291176997542) [HC, read in full]
- Relevance in general: "Ads that are more relevant cost less and see more results." — [About ad relevance diagnostics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/403110480493160) [HC, read in full]

### Inferences
- For this product the personal-attributes risk is in copy such as "Can't sleep?", "Struggling with insomnia?", "Hard of hearing?", "Are you a heavy sleeper who...?" when it names or implies a health condition or disability. "Wake up without waking your partner" describes a product benefit and names no attribute. Sleep itself is not in Meta's attribute list; "physical or mental health (including medical conditions)" and "disability" are, so insomnia/apnea/deafness framing is the part to avoid. This is my reading of the policy text; Meta gives no sleep example.
- Promises like "fall asleep in 5 minutes" or "fix your sleep in 7 days" would fit the "promises of specific outcomes within a set timeframe without disclaimers or qualifiers" clause if the ad is judged to be in a health context.
- "Customer feedback score" (older name, still referenced as "Review your feedback score" in Business Support Home) and "customer experience score" appear to be the same family of signal; the Help Center now uses the latter name and says its dashboard "is rolling out gradually to some verified businesses". For a new, low-volume store there may be no score yet.

### Gaps
- The full Transparency Center policy texts could not be read, so any wording there that differs from the Help Center summaries (including change-log dates in 2025-2026) is unknown to me.
- Meta does not publish how much a given quality ranking or feedback score changes CPM. No figure exists in anything I read.

## 5. Does the ad quality system penalise particular creative or landing-page features?

### Takeaway
Meta names exactly three low-quality ad-content attributes (withholding information, sensationalized language, engagement bait) and five low-quality landing-page attributes, plus six behavioural signals including bounce rate and dwell time. Text-heavy images are no longer limited, and page speed and "unverifiable review claims" are not named as such, though "misleading experiences" and bounce/dwell signals could capture them.

### Cited Findings

**Official**

- "Attributes of low-quality ad content": "Withholding information: Ads that withhold information to entice someone to click a link to understand the full context of something." / "Sensationalized language: Includes using exaggerated headlines or commanding a reaction from people to a degree that creates an unexpected experience when people click to a landing page." / "Engagement bait: When an ad uses spammy content that urges people to engage with it in an inauthentic way to get more likes, comments and shares." — [Best practices to improve Meta ad quality and performance](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1767120243598011) [HC, read in full]
- Effect: "If an ad contains any of these attributes, the ad quality component of an ad's total value may be relatively low." The article's own summary: "Learn how Meta measures ad quality and which low-quality ad content and landing page attributes can make your ads cost more and get fewer results." — [Best practices to improve Meta ad quality and performance](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1767120243598011) [HC, read in full]
- Signals Meta monitors: "Hide ad"; "Hide all ads from this advertiser"; "Hide ad due to repetition"; "Report ad"; "Landing page bounce rate"; "Landing page dwell time". — [Best practices to improve Meta ad quality and performance](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1767120243598011) [HC, read in full]
- "Attributes of a low-quality ad landing page experience": "Lack substantive or original content"; "Disproportionate volume of ads relative to content"; "Pop-up ads or interstitial ads: This includes any ads that disrupt the user experience - it doesn't include legally required disclosures or logins to private content, like paywalls"; "Unexpected content experiences ..."; "Misleading experiences: This includes websites that misrepresent products, expected shipping times, customer support experiences and associated feedback from customers such as being dissatisfied with advertising". — [Best practices to improve Meta ad quality and performance](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1767120243598011) [HC, read in full]
- Related policies the same article names: "Adult Content, Non-Functional Landing Page, Low Quality or Disruptive Content, Sensational Content, Commercial Exploitation of Crises and Controversial Events and Unrealistic Outcomes." — [Best practices to improve Meta ad quality and performance](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1767120243598011) [HC, read in full]
- Text in images: "Note: There is no longer a limit on the amount of text that can exist in your ad image. The text overlay tool is no longer available." Advice only: "If you want to add text to an image it shouldn't obstruct the visuals." — [Best practices for image ads on Facebook and Instagram](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/388369961318508) [HC, read in full]
- Clickbait does not help: "Engaging in clickbait and engagement bait does not improve ad performance." — [About ad auctions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/430291176997542) [HC, read in full]
- Quality ranking is the visible readout: values are "Above average", "Average", "Below average (bottom 35% of ads)", "Below average (bottom 20% of ads)", "Below average (bottom 10% of ads)"; "This metric is only available for the last 35 days"; not available for dynamic creative. — [About quality ranking](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/303639570334185) [HC, read in full]
- Caveats on that readout: "Ad relevance diagnostics aren't available for ads with fewer than 500 impressions." "Ad relevance diagnostics aren't inputs into the ad auction." — [About ad relevance diagnostics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/403110480493160) [HC, read in full]
- Meta's interpretation table: Quality below average with engagement and conversion average or above = "The ad is perceived as low quality ... Avoid low-quality attributes from your creative."; quality below, engagement average or above, conversion below = "This ad is click-baity or controversial." Also: "Sometimes high performing ads have below average ad relevance diagnostics rankings and that's OK." — [How to use ad relevance diagnostics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/436113280262012) [HC, read in full]
- Customer-experience best practices relevant to a small gadget store: "Pictures, videos and all other ad creative should accurately represent what you sell"; "The shipping information you provide should be inclusive of the full amount of time it takes to ship"; "If you source goods from suppliers or operate a dropship business, it's important that you maintain quality control." — [Best practices for positive customer feedback and a high-trust ad ecosystem](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/108127923199330) [HC, read in full]
- Notification: "In certain instances, we'll send you a notification so you know when low-quality attributes are severely affecting the performance of your ads." — [About ad quality](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/423781975167984) [HC, read in full]

**Practitioner**

- "The content above is related to low-quality, but allowed, ad content. Too much of it can negatively impact delivery and costs." (distinguishing it from the Low Quality or Disruptive Content policy, which gets ads rejected). — [Jon Loomer, "Low-Quality Ad Content and Post-Click Experiences"](https://www.jonloomer.com/low-quality-ad-content-and-post-click-experiences/) [excerpt only]
- Older press coverage of the same policy: "Individual ads with low-quality attributes will see reduced distribution in our ad auction, or will be disapproved. Multiple ads flagged with low-quality attributes may impact the performance of all ads from that advertiser." — [MarTech](https://martech.org/facebook-cracking-down-on-ads-with-clickbait-headlines-sensationalized-language/) [excerpt only; this is coverage of Meta's original announcement, several years old]

### Inferences
- Page speed is not named, but a slow page raises bounce rate and cuts dwell time, both of which Meta lists as monitored signals. So speed can plausibly feed ad quality indirectly; Meta does not say so in those words.
- A thin single-product page risks "Lack substantive or original content". Review widgets showing unverifiable or implausible reviews, or shipping promises the store cannot keep, risk "Misleading experiences" and poor survey feedback. Meta's text does not mention "unverifiable reviews" specifically.
- Because diagnostics need 500 impressions per ad, at a $70 CPM each ad needs roughly $35 of spend before a quality ranking can appear (500/1000 x $70). Arithmetic from the two numbers above, not a Meta statement.

### Gaps
- Meta does not publish thresholds for bounce rate, dwell time or load time.
- No Meta text read addresses review-claim substantiation on landing pages directly.

## 6. Age-related or audience-related limits for health content that would change who sees the ads

### Takeaway
None that I could find applies to a non-medical sleep alarm wristband: Meta's 18+ requirements in the Health and Wellness ad policy cover dietary weight-loss/weight-gain products, cosmetic procedures and sexual/reproductive health, and the store already targets US adults. Nothing Meta publishes says a health-and-wellness data source category narrows the audience.

### Cited Findings

- 18+ required for: "Dietary weight loss or weight gain products and services, such as supplements or pills"; listed cosmetic products, procedures or surgeries; and "sexual and reproductive health and wellness products or services". No sleep, wearable-alarm or general gadget category is listed. — [About Meta's Health and Wellness advertising policy](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2489235377779939) [HC, read in full]
- Search excerpt of the Transparency Center version says it restricts "weight-loss, cosmetic, and supplement ads from reaching under-18s". — [Transparency Center: Health and Wellness](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness) [excerpt only]
- Audience limits (age fixed 18-65+, no gender selection, no ZIP targeting, no lookalikes) belong to the housing, employment and financial products special ad categories, not to health. — [About audiences for housing, employment and financial ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2220749868045706) [HC, read in full]
- Minimum age is an advertiser-set "audience control"; an account-level "age-restricted goods and services setting under Audience controls in Advertising settings" exists. On WhatsApp Status, "We don't show ads featuring age-restricted goods or services to people of unknown age." — [Age and gender targeting](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/151999381652364) [HC, read in full]
- Generative-AI creative features may be withheld from some verticals: "certain industry verticals such as financial services and pharma/health ... may not have immediate access to all generative AI features." — [Creative best practices for text in ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/223409425500940) [HC, read in full]
- The data-source-category articles describe limits on data sharing only; they contain no statement about audience eligibility, age gating or delivery reach. — [About data source categories](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1402913027039332); [Understand data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698) [HC, both read in full]

### Inferences
- With US adults 18+ already targeted, no health-content age rule would shrink this store's audience. If the owner had set an account-level minimum age above 18 in Advertising settings, that would reduce the pool, but that is the owner's own setting, not a health rule.
- Detailed-targeting options referring to health conditions have been removed by Meta in past years (my background knowledge from before this session; not verified against a page read today), so interest targeting around sleep disorders may be unavailable. With broad/Advantage+ audience this has no bearing on CPM.

### Gaps
- I did not find a current Meta page listing which health-related detailed-targeting interests are unavailable in 2026.
- Whether Meta internally labels ads (as opposed to data sources) as "health" and limits their delivery to some users is not documented in anything I read.

## 7. Practical checklist: where the owner should look

### Takeaway
Six places answer the question: the dataset's Settings tab and Diagnostics tab in Events Manager, the Custom conversions and Audiences lists, the ad-level relevance-diagnostics columns in Ads Manager, and Business Support Home (the renamed Account Quality). If all are clean, Meta's health-and-wellness rules are not a documented cause of the $70 CPM.

### Cited Findings

**A. Events Manager: is the pixel categorised or restricted?**

1. Events Manager > Datasets (left menu) > select the dataset > Settings tab > scroll to "Manage data source categories" > Manage. Meta: "You may not see the Manage data source categories section if your data sources are not in any of the categories." If it is there, read the category and status per domain; for a Meta-assigned category use View details > Request review; the decision arrives by email. Needs full control of the business portfolio or ad-account admin. — [How to manage data source categories](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/467621355878794) [HC, read in full]
2. Same Settings tab > "Dataset categories" (self-assigned). "If you haven't assigned a category yet, you will see None." Do not self-assign Health and wellness unless intended: "Depending on your selection, Meta may apply data sharing restrictions to your dataset and all connected data sources." — [How to self-categorize](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1666235380728268) [HC, read in full]
3. Same Settings tab: check whether core setup / data restrictions are on. Core setup can be on because of Meta's categorisation, self-categorisation, repeated prohibited-data notifications, or the advertiser's own toggle. — [About core setup](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/124742407297678) [HC, read in full]. Vendors describe this as a "Data restrictions" section in the Settings tab. — [CustomerLabs docs](https://www.customerlabs.com/docs/destinations/ad-platforms/meta-health-and-wellness/restrictions/) [excerpt only]
4. Dataset > Diagnostics tab: look for notices that potentially prohibited information was removed. "More detailed information about the issue can be found in the Diagnostics tab for the relevant dataset in Events Manager." — [About prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809) [HC, read in full]
5. Dataset > Overview events table: confirm Purchase (with value and currency), AddToCart, InitiateCheckout are being received and that no custom events sit blocked awaiting review. Meta's path: "go to Events Manager, click the Data sources tab, select the name and ID of the data you want to review, scroll down to the events table and then select the event you want to review." — [About prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809) [HC, read in full]
6. Events Manager > Custom conversions tab: check none is flagged. — [Understand restrictions on certain custom conversions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2455915321411996) [HC, read in full]
7. Check the email inbox for the business and the Events Manager notifications: Meta says restriction decisions are sent "via email and notifications in Meta Events Manager." — [Understand data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698) [HC, read in full]

**B. Ads Manager: audiences and ad quality**

8. Ads Manager > Audiences (left-hand menu): check no custom or lookalike audience is flagged; flagged ones "cannot be used for running ad campaigns". Review can be requested "via Ads Manager under the campaigns table" or in the audience's Summary tab. — [Understand restrictions on certain custom audiences](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1055828013359808) [HC, read in full]
9. Ads Manager > Ads tab > Columns > Customize columns > tick Quality ranking, Engagement rate ranking, Conversion rate ranking > Apply (they are also in the "Performance" preset). Needs 500+ impressions per ad; quality ranking covers the last 35 days and is not shown for dynamic creative. — [About ad relevance diagnostics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/403110480493160); [About quality ranking](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/303639570334185) [HC, both read in full]
10. Read the three rankings together using Meta's table (for example below-average quality with average-or-better engagement and conversion = "perceived as low quality"; below quality, average-or-better engagement, below conversion = "click-baity or controversial"). — [How to use ad relevance diagnostics](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/436113280262012) [HC, read in full]
11. Ad set Delivery column: "Learning limited" means the ad set "is unlikely to receive about 50 optimization events in the week after your last significant edit"; "Learning limited isn't a penalty". Add the "Optimization events" column via Customize columns to see counts. — [About learning limited](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981) [HC, read in full]

**C. Business Support Home (formerly Account Quality)**

12. "Note: Account Quality is now Business Support Home." — [Best practices to improve Meta ad quality and performance](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1767120243598011) [HC, read in full]
13. Business Support Home > calendar icon (last 14, 30 or 90 days) > "Recent account issues" > Outstanding tab (and Resolved). There the owner can "Review the status of your business portfolios, ad accounts, commerce accounts, catalogs and Pages", "View and track the status of any rejected ads", "Review your feedback score", and "Request a review of ads or assets". — [About Meta Business Support Home](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/254088759757736) [HC, read in full]
14. Rejected ads: reason shown in Business Support Home; re-review via select account > select the ad > Request review > Submit; "We aim to review and provide a decision within 48 hours." — [How to troubleshoot a rejected ad](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1210227555661027) [HC, read in full]
15. Confirm advertiser verification is complete, because Meta ties incomplete verification to lower quality treatment: "Do complete advertiser verification for your ad accounts." — [About ad quality for Meta ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/423781975167984) [HC, read in full]
16. Customer experience score / insights dashboard, if available to the account ("rolling out gradually to some verified businesses"; needs full control of the business portfolio). — [About customer experience score](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1491186119412011) [HC, read in full]

### Inferences
- Reading the results: section absent in step 1 + "None" in step 2 + Purchase events flowing in step 5 + no Diagnostics notices = the pixel is not under a health-and-wellness restriction, and that explanation for the CPM can be set aside. Average-or-better quality ranking in step 9 and nothing outstanding in step 13 = no documented quality or policy penalty either. The CPM would then need another explanation (auction competition, audience size, season, optimisation goal, creative engagement), which is outside these notes.
- Mid-to-late November is the most competitive retail auction period of the year; that is a separate, non-health reason CPMs run high right now (general knowledge, not sourced here).
- This session's Meta Ads connector exposes read-only dataset tools (dataset details, dataset quality, auction ranking benchmarks). I did not run them because this assignment was documentation research only; whether they expose the data-source category field is unknown to me. The coordinator could use them to check the owner's actual dataset rather than rely on the owner's screen.

- The exact on-screen wording of the status labels inside "Manage data source categories" (for example what a cleared or approved review looks like) is not in Meta's articles; only vendor excerpts describe icons.
