# Meta (Facebook/Instagram) ad cost benchmarks for US e-commerce, Q4 / BFCM focus

*Research date: 19 Nov 2026. Exchange rate assumed throughout: £1 = $1.29 (the rate implied by the brief's own £54 ≈ $70 conversion; not independently checked). The advertiser's numbers being judged: CPM ≈ $70 (£54), link CPC ≈ $4.50 (£3.50), link CTR ≈ 1.5%, Sales campaign optimised for Purchase, US 25-65, ≈ $45/day (£35), 15-19 Nov 2026, product $39.99.*

*Access caveat that applies to every section: page fetching was blocked in this session (triplewhale.com, lebesgue.io, wordstream.com, shopify.com all failed to resolve - they are outside the session's allowed network domains). Every figure below therefore comes from search-result extracts of the cited pages, not from a full read of the page. Where a number was only seen on a third-party blog quoting the original, it is labelled "secondary". November 2026 data is not yet published by any source found; November 2025 and the latest 2026 months are used instead and labelled.*

## 1. Typical US Meta CPMs for e-commerce in 2025-2026 by month, and the November / BFCM uplift

### Takeaway
Credible US and e-commerce panels put normal Meta CPM at roughly $15-23 through 2026 and at roughly $25-28 in the peak month (November 2025); the week-and-a-half before Black Friday typically runs only about 15-25% above the September baseline, with the big jump (+59% to +84%) confined to Black Friday week itself. A $70 CPM on 15-19 November is about 2.5-4.5 times any published median, so seasonality explains only a small part of it.

### Cited Findings

**Annual / rolling medians (e-commerce)**
- Triple Whale (e-commerce brands on its platform, primary page, window Nov 2025 - Oct 2026): overall median Meta CPM $15.72. — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)
- Triple Whale, earlier window Aug 2025 - Jul 2026: median CPM $15.06, up about 13.2% year over year; median CPA $38.99. — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks); repeated by [Single Grain](https://www.singlegrain.com/?p=81379)
- Triple Whale 2025 calendar year (about 35,000 brands, secondary): median CPM about $13.48; Meta CPM rose 20.03% year over year in 2025 (April 2026 report). — [Foundry CRO](https://foundrycro.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-by-industry-2026/)
- Lebesgue e-commerce data, March 2026: CPM about $16.08 in the US, $11.81 in the UK, $1.36 in India. — [Lebesgue CPM by country](https://lebesgue.io/?p=305), as quoted by [Pace](https://paceads.com/blog/meta-ads-cpm-benchmarks-2026)
- AdAmigo country guide (2025 data, revalidated 29 Jan 2026): US CPM about $23, US average CPC $2.69 (all objectives, includes lead generation). — [AdAmigo by country](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/meta-ads-cpm-cpc-benchmarks-by-country-2026)
- MHI Growth Engine (DTC e-commerce): $22.05 CPM across all brands in January 2026; e-commerce conversion campaigns at CPM $16.80. — [MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/meta-ads-benchmarks-ecommerce-2026/)
- Biddy (US, only its 23 highest-spending accounts, so not small advertisers): median CPM $15.96 in June 2026 rising to $20.10 in August 2026; $18.73 median overall. — [Biddy](https://www.biddyco.com/blog-posts/meta-ads-benchmarks)

**Month-by-month series (sources conflict on level, agree on shape)**
- AdMake (vendor blog, original source not named): all-market path Jan 2025 $17.73, Oct 2025 $21.69, Nov 2025 $25.22 (peak), Dec 2025 $22.04, Jan 2026 $15.74 (13-month low). US-specific path: started 2025 at $20.41, peaked at $28.09 in November 2025, $17.12 in January 2026. — [AdMake](https://admakeai.com/blog/cost-per-impression-cpm-explained)
- SuperAds US series: peak $27.47 in November 2025, trough $20.24 in July 2026. — [SuperAds US CPM](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cpm-cost-per-mille/united-states)
- Shopify: average Facebook CPM for November 2025 was $16.06 (underlying data source not visible in the extract). — [Shopify](https://shopify.com/blog/facebook-ads-cost)
- AdKit panel (2,000+ advertisers, all objectives, median): $6.83 in November 2025, $6.46 in July 2026, $8.41 in September 2026, $8.37 in October 2026 - far lower than every other source, probably because it is global and all-objective. — [AdKit](https://adkit.so/benchmarks)
- Gupta Media (global, multi-vertical, includes reach campaigns - not US-only): Meta CPM $6.59 in October 2025; weekly $8.15 for week of 3 Nov 2025 and $12.46 for the week of 24 Nov 2025; $14.94 average over 28 Nov - 1 Dec 2025; $6.48 average across "Q5" (post-Christmas), 57% below the BFCM window. — [Gupta Media Q5](https://www.guptamedia.com/insights/q5-marketing-holiday-advertising)
- Gupta Media 2024 (dated): Black Friday week averaged $13.42 CPM and Cyber Monday hit $17.70, 138% above Meta's annual average; Q4 CPMs "can surge up to 66%". — [Gupta Media social ads cost](https://www.guptamedia.com/social-media-ads-cost)

**Timing of the November rise (most relevant to 15-19 Nov 2026)**
- Lebesgue (October 2026 analysis of about $22.6M of spend among its largest advertisers, median brand, versus that brand's September CPM): CPM stayed within 7% of September from September through the first week of November 2025; first clear increase in the week of 10 November 2025; +15% two weeks before Black Friday; +24% the week before; +59% in Black Friday week (2024: +80%); +84% on Black Friday itself (28 Nov 2025); +71% on Cyber Monday (1 Dec 2025); still +20% on 3 December. — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/are-meta-ads-worth-it-black-friday)
- Same Lebesgue analysis, Black Friday week versus September: conversion rate (purchases per link click) +48%, cost per purchase -20%, Meta-reported ROAS +19% for the median brand - i.e. the higher CPM was more than paid for by better conversion. — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/are-meta-ads-worth-it-black-friday)
- Olifant Digital (one agency's own accounts, baseline October rather than September): the week before Black Friday was already +72% on CPM with ROAS only +20%; Black Friday day CPM was 2.12x October with ROAS 2.29x. — [Olifant Digital](https://olifantdigital.com/blog/black-friday-ad-costs-meta-google)
- Triple Whale: pre-BFCM Meta ad spend 24-27 Nov 2025 was $396.6M, 65% of the four-day BFCM weekend spend (shows how much budget piles into the days just before). — [Triple Whale BFCM](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-bfcm)

**Meta's own price-per-ad commentary (direction of 2026 inflation)**
- Q3 2026 (released 21 Oct 2026): average price per ad +14% year over year, ad impressions +10%; ad revenue $63.1B, +26%. — [Meta Q3 2026 release via Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/405782/meta-reports-third-quarter-2026-results); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/meta-beats-q3-expectations-with-strong-earnings/)
- Q3 2026 regional price growth: about +23% in US and Canada, +10% Europe, +1% Asia-Pacific, +24% rest of world; CFO attributed it to better ad performance and friendlier macro conditions. — [Merca2.0](https://www.merca20.com/meta-raises-the-average-price-of-its-ads-by-14-as-advertising-on-facebook-and-instagram-drives-its-revenue/)
- Q4 2025 (released 28 Jan 2026): average price per ad +6% for Q4 and +9% for full-year 2025; impressions +18% for Q4 and +12% for the year. — [Meta investor relations](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results/)

### Inferences
- Black Friday 2026 is 27 November (Thanksgiving 26 November), so 15-19 November is 8-12 days before Black Friday. On the Lebesgue 2025 pattern that is the "+15% to +24% over September" zone, not the "+59% to +84%" zone.
- A rough "expected" US CPM for this window: take the US/e-commerce November 2025 peak month figures ($25-28) and add Meta's reported US price inflation of about 14-23% year over year, giving roughly $29-35 as a monthly November 2026 average; mid-November should sit below that monthly average. Health & wellness runs about 40% above the all-category median (see section 2), which could push a typical figure to the high $20s-$30s. $70 is still about double the top of that range.
- Multiples: $70 is about 4.5x the Triple Whale all-category median ($15.72), about 3.1x its health & wellness median ($22.44), about 2.5x the highest published US monthly figure (November 2025, $27-28).
- Conclusion: seasonality and 2026 inflation together plausibly explain a 30-60% premium over a mid-year baseline. They do not explain a 3x premium. The rest has to come from account-specific factors (new pixel/account with no purchase history, tiny budget, Purchase optimisation narrowing delivery to expensive users, older skew in delivery, creative quality ranking).

### Gaps
- No November 2026 monthly data is published yet by any source found (Triple Whale's latest window ends October 2026; AdKit's latest month is October 2026).
- Gupta Media's tracker and eMarketer's "Meta Ads CPM (US)" KPI page (AdScale data from 1,071 US e-commerce brands, updated Q1 2026) could not be opened, so no clean US-only, e-commerce-only month-by-month 2026 series was obtained. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/industry-kpis/meta-ads-cpm-us)
- No Varos, Tinuiti, Emplifi or Statista figures surfaced in search; Varos data in particular appears not to be publicly indexed.
- The monthly series that were found are vendor blogs with unclear methodology; November 2025 levels differ by 30% or more between them ($16.06 Shopify vs $27-28 SuperAds/AdMake US).

## 2. CPM, link CPC, link CTR and cost per purchase for Sales (Purchase-optimised) campaigns versus traffic/engagement, with health & wellness / electronics splits

### Takeaway
For Purchase-optimised e-commerce campaigns the published medians are roughly: CPM $15-25, CPC about $0.60-$1.10, CTR about 1.4-2.4%, click-to-purchase conversion about 1.2-1.6%, cost per purchase about $39-42 (health & wellness $42, electronics $51). The advertiser's 1.5% link CTR is normal; the $70 CPM and $4.50 CPC are about 3x and 4-7x the benchmarks.

### Cited Findings

**Triple Whale e-commerce medians (accounts are overwhelmingly Purchase-optimised DTC brands)**
- All categories, Nov 2025 - Oct 2026: median CPM $15.72, median ROAS 1.88, median conversion rate 1.51%. — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)
- All categories, Aug 2025 - Jul 2026: median CTR 2.39% (up 16%), CPM $15.06, CPA $38.99, ROAS 1.88. — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks); [Coupler.io](https://blog.coupler.io/facebook-ads-statistics/)
- Health and Wellness (Nov 2025 - Oct 2026): median CPM $22.44 (+14.06% YoY, the highest of any category), median CTR 3.08% (+20.23%), median CPA $42.30 (+10.73%, the sharpest CPA rise of any industry), median ROAS 1.42 (-5.54%). — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)
- Health and Wellness conversion rate (Aug 2025 - Jul 2026 window, secondary): median 1.50%, down 13.99% year over year. — [Enrich Labs](https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/meta-ads-benchmarks-2025)
- Electronics: median CPA $51.06 (+4.70%). Other categories for comparison: Apparel and Accessories $37.41, Beauty $40.44, Toys/Art/Collectibles $34.63. — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)
- Earlier Triple Whale snapshot (calendar 2025, dated, secondary): Health and Wellness CPM $20.70, CTR 2.70%. — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2026-cpc-cpm-ctr-industry)

**Other Purchase / e-commerce conversion-campaign figures**
- MHI Growth Engine, e-commerce conversion campaigns (2026): median CPC $0.87, CPM $16.80, CTR 1.93%, CPA $32.22. — [MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/meta-ads-benchmarks-ecommerce-2026/)
- Small first-party DTC sample (15 brands; attribution to this page inferred from its title): CPM $13.52, CPC $0.57, median click-to-purchase rate about 1.24% (75th percentile 3.24%), median cost per purchase $49.04, purchase ROAS 2.96. — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/dtc-ecommerce-benchmarks/meta-ads-benchmarks/)
- A roundup citing Triple Whale (different window): CTR 2.19%, CPM $14.19, CPA $38.19, ROAS 1.86, conversion rate 1.57%; rule of thumb that CPA $35-40 is normal and above $45 "deserves a closer look". — [Triple Whale via roundup](https://triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)

**By objective**

- AdAmigo cross-industry guide (2025 data, single-agency, revalidated Jan 2026): awareness $10-15 CPM, traffic/engagement $15-25 (its own page says $12-18), sales $20-30, leads $30-45; conversion campaigns cost 2-4x the CPM of reach campaigns. — [AdAmigo by objective](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/meta-ads-benchmarks-2026-by-objective-and-placement); [Pace summary](https://paceads.com/blog/meta-ads-cpm-benchmarks-2026)
- CTR by objective (AdAmigo 2025 data via Adside): Leads 2.59%, Traffic 1.71%, Engagement 1.42%, Sales 1.38% (with about $30 CPA), Video views 1.21%, Awareness 0.94%. — [Adside](https://www.adside.ai/blog/good-ctr-meta-ads-2026)
- WordStream/LocaliQ 2026 Facebook benchmarks (about 1,800 US campaigns; covers traffic and lead campaigns only, not Purchase): traffic campaigns average CTR 1.93% and CPC $0.60; lead campaigns cost per lead $27.39. — [WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2026); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/facebook-lead-costs-hold-at-27-39-as-traffic-clicks-drop-to-0-60/); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/facebook-ad-costs-fall-as-traffic-and-lead-performance-improves-report-488433)
- WordStream's 7.72%-8.54% conversion rate is for lead-form campaigns and must not be used as a purchase benchmark. — [WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2026)

### Inferences
- Implied benchmark CPC from Triple Whale (CPM / CTR): all categories $15.72 / 2.39% ≈ $0.66; health & wellness $22.44 / 3.08% ≈ $0.73. The advertiser's $4.50 is about 6-7x that, and about 4-5x the $0.87-$1.11 conversion-campaign medians.
- The advertiser's three numbers are internally consistent ($70 CPM / 1.5% CTR ≈ $4.67 CPC), so the high CPC is entirely a CPM problem, not a creative click-through problem. Link CTR 1.5% is in line with the Sales-objective average (1.38%) and within the normal 1-2% link-CTR band; it is below Triple Whale's medians (2.39% / 3.08%), but those may be all-clicks CTR rather than link CTR (the extracts do not say which).
- Sales/Purchase campaigns carrying higher CPM than traffic is expected (roughly 1.5-2x), because the algorithm bids for the small set of users likely to buy. That accounts for a $20-30 CPM, not $70.

### Gaps
- No source gives a clean link-click CPC and link CTR split by objective for the US in 2026; the click definition (all clicks vs link clicks) is not stated in most extracts.
- No "consumer electronics / gadgets" CPM or CTR was found, only Triple Whale's electronics CPA ($51.06). A secondary blog quoted $51.86 for "electronics and medical devices", a slightly different grouping.
- No benchmark specific to sleep or wearable gadgets.
- One secondary site listed health & wellness at "CPC $1.32, CVR 11.00%, ROAS 3.5x" - inconsistent with Triple Whale and not used.
- Unattributed figures seen in search summaries (could not be tied to a specific page, so treat as unverified): US all-objective Meta CPM $21.98 from a 400+ advertiser panel; an e-commerce median CPC of about $1.11 for Jan 2025 - Jan 2026.

## 3. Small-budget advertisers (under $100/day) versus larger ones

### Takeaway
No reputable dataset publishes Meta CPM by spend tier at the under-$100/day level; the only tiered figure found (an unexplained index) suggests the smallest budgets pay about 30-45% more CPM than mid-size accounts. The more important small-budget effect is statistical: at about $45/day the campaign cannot exit the learning phase and five days of data is too little to judge.

### Cited Findings
- Stackmatix budget-tier index (methodology not explained): Meta CPM index 130-145 for budgets under $1,000/month versus 100 for $3,000-10,000/month and 80-90 for $50,000-200,000/month. Stated reason: a small budget reaches fewer unique users, so frequency rises and cost per incremental conversion climbs. — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/ad-cost-benchmarks-startup-vs-enterprise)
- Learning-phase rule of thumb: an ad set needs about 50 optimisation events per week to stabilise. — [Up Business Co](https://upbusinessco.com/blogs/meta-ads-cost/)
- Benchmarks that are explicitly large-account only: Biddy's $18.73 US median covers its 23 highest-spending accounts; Lebesgue's BFCM analysis covers about $22.6M of spend among its largest advertisers. — [Biddy](https://www.biddyco.com/blog-posts/meta-ads-benchmarks); [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/are-meta-ads-worth-it-black-friday)
- AdKit's panel of 2,000+ advertisers (median $8.37 in October 2026) is the broadest by count but is all-objective and not US-only. — [AdKit](https://adkit.so/benchmarks)

### Inferences
- The campaign's own volume: about $45/day x 5 days ≈ $226 (£175) of spend; at $70 CPM that is roughly 3,200 impressions and about 50 link clicks. At a typical 1.2-1.6% click-to-purchase rate that predicts 0.6-0.8 purchases - i.e. zero or one sale is the statistically expected outcome, whatever the product quality.
- 50 purchases a week at the health & wellness median CPA ($42.30) would need about $2,100/week ($300/day), roughly 7x this budget. With Purchase optimisation at $45/day the algorithm has almost no conversion signal and tends to bid into a narrow, expensive slice of the audience, which is a well-known cause of very high CPMs on new, small accounts.
- Applying the Stackmatix 30-45% small-budget premium to a $22-28 health & wellness / November baseline gives about $29-41 - still well short of $70. So "small budget" alone, like seasonality alone, does not fully explain the figure; the combination of new account + Purchase optimisation + tiny sample probably does, and with only about 3,200 impressions the measured CPM is itself noisy.

### Gaps
- No Varos, Triple Whale, Lebesgue, Birch/Revealbot, WordStream, Gupta or Madgicx cut by daily-spend tier was found. Triple Whale's page may include revenue-tier breakdowns, but the page could not be opened.
- No published evidence on how much CPM falls as a new pixel accumulates purchase data.
- Meta's own peer-benchmark tools (Ads Manager auction ranking / "performance vs peers" insights) would give an account-specific answer; they were outside this research scope.

## 4. CPM by placement, age band and gender (US)

### Takeaway
Reels and Stories are usually 20-50% cheaper per thousand impressions than Feed, and Audience Network is cheapest; published placement ranges top out around $20-22, so no single placement explains $70. Evidence on age and gender is thin: one Lebesgue figure shows women costing about 22% more than men, and no usable US age-band CPM data was found.

### Cited Findings
- Flighted (2026, typical ranges, geography not stated): Facebook Feed $13-19, Instagram Feed $15-22, Instagram Stories $11-16, Instagram Reels $9-15, Audience Network $4.30-8.60 (CTR 0.45-0.9%). — [Flighted](https://www.flighted.co/blog/meta-ads-performance-benchmarks-by-industry)
- Influee (date range and US scope not shown; lower overall level): Facebook Feed $8-14, Instagram Feed $7-12, Facebook Stories $5-9, Instagram Stories $5-9, Facebook Reels $4-8. — [Influee](https://influee.co/ca/blog/good-cpm-facebook-ads)
- Reels placements are reported as 20-40% (one source: 30-50%) cheaper than Feed for a comparable audience, with the warning that lower CPM on Reels does not guarantee lower CPA. — [Pace](https://paceads.com/blog/meta-ads-cpm-benchmarks-2026)
- CTR by placement (AdAmigo 2025 single-agency data via Adside): Instagram Stories 1.34%, Instagram Feed 1.01%, Instagram Reels 0.76%, Audience Network about 0.58%. Facebook Feed CTR conflicts: 2.23% in Adside's table versus 1.11% on AdAmigo's own CTR page. — [Adside](https://www.adside.ai/blog/good-ctr-meta-ads-2026); [AdAmigo CTR](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/meta-ads-ctr-benchmarks-industry-2026)

- Gender (Lebesgue "2025 update"; sample and US scope unclear; absolute level is far below other panels): average CPM $6.01 for ads targeting women versus $4.92 for men. — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/facebook-cpm-by-gender)
- Shopify states that age, location and interests can move costs up or down but gives no age-specific numbers. — [Shopify](https://shopify.com/blog/facebook-ads-cost)

### Inferences
- If the advertiser's delivery skewed to Facebook/Instagram Feed (typical for a single-image or short-video Purchase campaign), placement mix would add perhaps 10-30% versus an all-placements blend; it cannot produce a 3x gap.
- Women running about 22% above men (Lebesgue ratio) is a modest effect of the same order.
- For a Purchase-optimised campaign aimed at 25-65, Meta commonly concentrates delivery on older Facebook Feed users, who are widely reported by practitioners to carry higher CPMs because they convert well; no dataset found quantifies this for the US, so it should be checked in the account's own age/placement breakdown rather than assumed.

### Gaps
- No source found gives US CPM by age band (25-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55-64, 65+) for 2025-2026. The search returned only audience-share statistics, not costs.
- No US-only, dated, single-methodology placement table was found; the two placement tables differ by roughly 2x in level and neither states its sample.
- Audience Network CPM appears in only one source.

## 5. Break-even maths for a roughly $40 AOV store (cost per purchase, conversion rate, CPC)

### Takeaway
At a $39.99 order value, typical benchmark funnel rates (1.2-1.6% click-to-purchase; Shopify store average 1.4%) only break even if link clicks cost roughly $0.35-$0.65; at $4.50 a click the store would need an 11-19% click-to-purchase rate, which is 3-4x even top-decile Shopify stores. Note that the median Meta cost per purchase across e-commerce ($39-42) is itself about equal to this product's entire price, so a single $40 item is structurally hard to sell profitably on first purchase even at normal costs.

### Cited Findings
- Littledata Shopify benchmark: average store conversion rate 1.4%; above 3.2% is top 20%, above 4.7% is top 10%; average add-to-cart rate 4.6%; average checkout completion 45%. — [Red Stag Fulfillment](https://redstagfulfillment.com/average-conversion-rate-for-shopify-stores/); [Growth Suite](https://www.growthsuite.net/resources/shopify-conversion-rate/complete-guide/benchmarks-by-industry)
- Date conflict on the 1.4%: Growth Suite says it is from Littledata's 2023 benchmark of 2,800 stores (dated); another source describes a Littledata cut of 426 stores over the 90 days to 3 October 2026 that also gives a 1.4% median. Growth Suite's own 2026 data from 958 stores gives 2.2%. — [Growth Suite](https://www.growthsuite.net/resources/shopify-conversion-rate/complete-guide/benchmarks-by-industry)
- Littledata device split (secondary, unconfirmed): about 1.2% mobile, 1.9% desktop. — [Thunderbit](https://thunderbit.com/blog/shopify-conversion-rate-statistics)
- Meta-specific conversion rate (purchases per click): Triple Whale median 1.51% (Nov 2025 - Oct 2026); health & wellness 1.50%; small DTC sample median 1.24%, 75th percentile 3.24%. — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks); [Enrich Labs](https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/meta-ads-benchmarks-2025); [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/dtc-ecommerce-benchmarks/meta-ads-benchmarks/)
- Median Meta cost per purchase: $38.99 all e-commerce, $42.30 health & wellness, $51.06 electronics; median ROAS 1.88 overall, 1.42 health & wellness. — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)
- During Black Friday week 2025 the median brand's click-to-purchase rate was 48% above September and cost per purchase 20% lower. — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/are-meta-ads-worth-it-black-friday)

### Inferences
(All arithmetic below is mine, using AOV $39.99; "margin" means contribution margin after product cost, shipping and payment fees, which the advertiser must supply.)

| Assumption | Break-even cost per purchase | Max CPC at 1.5% click-to-purchase | Max CPC at 3.0% | Conversion rate needed at $4.50 CPC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue only (100% margin) | $39.99 (£31) | $0.60 | $1.20 | 11.3% |
| 70% margin | $27.99 (£21.70) | $0.42 | $0.84 | 16.1% |
| 60% margin | $23.99 (£18.60) | $0.36 | $0.72 | 18.8% |
| 50% margin | $20.00 (£15.50) | $0.30 | $0.60 | 22.5% |

- Implied cost per purchase at the advertiser's current numbers: $4.50 / 1.5% ≈ $300 (£233); even at a top-decile 4.7% conversion rate it is about $96. Break-even ROAS is 1 / margin (1.67 at 60%, 2.0 at 50%); current implied ROAS is about 0.13.
- At benchmark costs instead (CPC about $0.73 for health & wellness, 1.5% conversion) cost per purchase would be about $49 - still above the $39.99 price. So even "normal" Meta performance loses money on first order at this price point; the Triple Whale health & wellness median ROAS of 1.42 says the same thing for the category. Profitability at $40 normally requires some mix of higher order value (bundles, multi-packs), above-average conversion (2.5-3%+), cheap clicks ($0.40-0.70), or repeat/referral revenue.
- With about 50 clicks in the test, zero purchases is consistent with a perfectly average 1.5% store, so the test says almost nothing yet about landing-page conversion.

### Gaps
- No benchmark isolates paid-social-to-Shopify purchase conversion for sub-$50 AOV stores. Two unattributed figures appeared in search summaries and could not be tied to a page: "low-ticket ($15-50 AOV) typically lands at $18-35 CPA" and "CPA $42.91 for orders under $60". Treat both as unverified.

## 6. Does billing in GBP for a US audience change anything (beyond currency)?

### Takeaway
Nothing found indicates that a GBP-denominated ad account pays a different auction price for US impressions; the account currency only sets the unit for budgets, bids and charges, so the benchmark comparison is a straight currency conversion. The only currency-specific cost is the bank/card FX margin if the payment method is not in the account currency.

### Cited Findings
- The ad account currency determines the unit Meta uses to charge the payment method and calculate auction bids; performance shifts seen after a currency change are attributed to learning resets, tracking reconnection and budget conversion errors, "not the currency code itself" (vendor blog, not Meta documentation). — [AGrowth](https://agrowth.io/blogs/facebook-ads/facebook-ad-account-currency)
- Account currency cannot be changed in place; Meta creates a new ad account and deactivates the old one, which resets optimisation history and audiences. — [AGrowth](https://agrowth.io/blogs/facebook-ads/facebook-ad-account-currency)
- If the card or bank account is in a different currency from the ad account, the bank converts at a rate worse than mid-market; this margin is the main hidden cost. — [Wise](https://wise.com/gb/blog/facebook-advertising-costs-uk)
- A payments vendor (commercially interested) says bank FX spread and Meta's digital-services-tax surcharge can stack for UK advertisers. — [PhotonPay](https://www.photonpay.com/uk/blog/article/meta-ads-hidden-costs)
- For scale: Lebesgue's March 2026 e-commerce CPMs were $16.08 in the US versus $11.81 in the UK, so a UK advertiser used to UK costs should expect US impressions to cost about 36% more regardless of billing currency. — [Lebesgue via Pace](https://paceads.com/blog/meta-ads-cpm-benchmarks-2026)

### Inferences
- A GBP account paying from a GBP card has no FX step at all: Meta bills in GBP, and the £54 CPM is simply the sterling expression of the US auction price. Converting at £1 = $1.29 gives the $70 used above; a few percent of exchange-rate movement does not change the verdict.
- Because the account converts budgets internally, a £35/day budget is a roughly $45/day budget in a US auction where the median advertiser's cost per purchase is about $39-42 - i.e. the daily budget buys about one benchmark-priced purchase per day.

### Gaps
- No official Meta Business Help Center page was retrieved to confirm the auction-currency mechanics; the statements above come from vendor and agency blogs.
- Not verified from a fetched source (my background understanding only, needs checking): Meta's UK digital-services-tax surcharge (about 2%) is applied to ads delivered in the UK, so it should not apply to ads shown to US users; and UK VAT (20%) is added to invoices for advertisers without a VAT number but is not included in the "amount spent" that Ads Manager uses to calculate CPM and CPC. If either is wrong, the effect on the comparison would still be small relative to a 3x gap.
- The actual GBP/USD rate on 15-19 November 2026 was not looked up; 1.29 is the rate implied by the brief.
