# E-commerce upsell benchmarks: take rates and AOV lift (as of November 2026)

Verification note for the report writer: page fetching was blocked in this session (hosts outside the network allowlist), so every figure below was taken from web-search result summaries of the linked pages, not from reading the full page. Numbers are reproduced as the search tool reported them. Where the search summary did not make clear which of several pages a figure came from, the entry says "attribution uncertain". Treat exact decimals as needing a click-through check before publication. Labels used: [VENDOR] = app/service seller's own data or marketing; [VENDOR-DATA] = vendor's aggregated platform dataset with stated sample (better than a case study, still self-selected to that app's users); [AGENCY] = CRO agency write-up of a client test; [INDEPENDENT] = regulator, platform policy, research institute, trade publication; [BLOG] = third-party blog/guide with no stated data.

Two definitions that make sources non-comparable: "take rate" = accepts ÷ offer views; "attach rate" = orders containing the add-on ÷ all orders. Attach rate is lower whenever not every buyer sees the offer. ([LaunchTip, 2026](https://www.launchtip.com/upsell-statistics) [BLOG/VENDOR])

## 1. Product-page order bumps and tick-box add-ons (take rates by price point; digital add-ons)

### Takeaway
There is no verified, published benchmark of order-bump take rate by price band for physical-product Shopify stores. The best large datasets put product-page add-on take rates in the low single digits at the median (2.4% median, about 9-10% for the top decile); the widely quoted 30-38% "order bump" figures come from digital-product/course checkouts and are not transferable.

### Cited Findings
- Median upsell take rate (upsells sold ÷ upsells viewed) is 2.4% across 3,199 Shopify stores using the Candy Rack app, January-August 2026; top 10% of stores are "close to 10%" (another summary says 8.9-9%). By placement, medians: product-page pop-up 2.4%, cart pop-up 1.9%, slide-cart offer 1.4%, thank-you page 0.7%. [VENDOR-DATA, 2026, n=3,199 stores] — [Digismoothie / Candy Rack upsell benchmarks](https://www.digismoothie.com/blog/upsell-benchmarks)
- Note a conflict in how the same Candy Rack figure is described: one secondary source describes the 2.4% (median) and 8.9% (top 10%) as incremental revenue added by upsells rather than a take rate. The two readings were not reconciled. — [Digismoothie](https://www.digismoothie.com/blog/upsell-benchmarks); secondary: [Upnova, 2026](https://upnova.io/blog/best-shopify-upsell-apps-2026) (attribution of the secondary reading uncertain)
- Pre-checkout pop-up offers (shown after add-to-cart, before checkout) converted 13.3% of views to a purchase across 123.9M offer views and 16,600 Shopify stores using Zipify OneClickUpsell, 1 Nov 2025-31 Oct 2026; published 16 Nov 2026. Only 6,985 of those stores used the pre-checkout pop-up. Mobile acceptance was higher than desktop in all six placements (1.07x post-purchase to 1.53x cart drawer). [VENDOR-DATA, 2026, n=16,600 stores; not a random sample of Shopify stores] — [Zipify press release via EIN Presswire](https://www.einnews.com/pr_news/957117063/zipify-study-of-123-9m-upsell-offers-finds-pre-checkout-pop-ups-convert-best-yet-half-as-many-shopify-stores-use-them)
- Planning ranges (explicitly "not promises", no dataset): product-page add-on 8-20% of offer views; cart or cart-drawer offer 4-12% of offer views; post-purchase one-click 10-25% of offer views. [BLOG/VENDOR, 2026] — [LaunchTip upsell statistics](https://www.launchtip.com/upsell-statistics)
- Product-page upsell "2% to 5% average acceptance", cart page 5-10%, post-purchase 8-15%. [BLOG, 2026, no method stated; attribution uncertain between the two pages] — [Loopvoice](https://loopvoice.ai/blog/best-shopify-upsell-apps) / [Gochyu](https://gochyu.com/blog/all-about-shopify-upsell)
- Product-page upsell accept rates "typically 2 to 6%". [BLOG, no data; attribution uncertain] — [Bookscloud](https://bookscloud.io/blogs/bookscloud-blog/can-too-many-book-cross-sells-hurt-your-product-page-conversion-rate)
- "Order bumps convert at 37.8%" from a study of 1,847 digital businesses in North America, data collected 15 Jan-28 Jul 2025. This is a digital-business/checkout-funnel figure, not physical DTC. The same publisher gives an e-commerce average of 19.8% (a secondary summary says 18.7%), and device rates of desktop 28.9% vs mobile 18.7%. Secondary sources report its post-purchase figure inconsistently (14.6% vs 9.9%). [AGENCY research, 2025-2026, n=1,847; method not verifiable, tables did not load] — [Focus Digital 2025 report](https://focus-digital.co/average-upsell-conversion-rate-2025-report/); [Focus Digital 2026 report](https://focus-digital.co/average-upsell-conversion-rate-2026-report/); secondary: [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/upsell-and-cross-sell-rates), [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/post-purchase-upsell-thank-you-page-2026-ecommerce-playbook)
- Digital-product (course/info) platforms: JVZoo reports 15%+ take rates for bumps priced $27-$97 among its own users; Teachable says order bumps "should convert at 30% or higher"; SamCart's 35% on a $27 bump is a hypothetical worked example, not data. [VENDOR, digital products only] — [JVZoo](https://blog.jvzoo.com/?p=5428); [Teachable](https://teachable.com/blog/order-bumps-drive-sales); [SamCart](https://www.samcart.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-order-bumps)
- A course platform states plainly that it has not measured bump revenue and "there's no published benchmark we could verify"; its 10-30% price-ratio guidance is a rule of thumb. [VENDOR, candid] — [Ruzuku](https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/order-bumps-for-courses)
- General (not bump-specific) glossary range: retail 10-25% take rates, tech products 5-10% "due to steeper prices". [BLOG, no data; attribution uncertain] — [Alexander Jarvis glossary](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/what-is-upsell-take-rate-in-ecommerce/)
- Measurement warning: a merchant "reads '11% take rate' and never learns that only 62% of orders were even eligible"; a bump can also cannibalise a higher-margin item the customer would have added anyway. [BLOG analysis] — [Lazyweb](https://labs.lazyweb.com/research/percent-apps-in-flow-upsell-screen.md)
- A Candy Rack anecdote: a product-page "family pack" upsell converted at 0.25% yet was called worthwhile because of the AOV jump per acceptance. [VENDOR anecdote] — [Digismoothie upsell strategy guide](https://www.digismoothie.com/blog/upsell-strategy-guide)

### Inferences
- For a $39.99 physical gadget, a realistic planning range for a tick-box add-on on the product page is roughly 2-10% of viewers (Candy Rack median to top decile), with the 13% Zipify pop-up figure as an upper reference for a well-matched, post-add-to-cart offer. The 30-38% figures apply to info-product checkouts and should not be used.
- No source gave take rate by add-on price band (under $10 vs $10-25). Directionally, the cheapest add-ons relative to the main item take best, but that is practitioner consensus, not measured data.
- A digital add-on (e.g. a sleep guide PDF) has near-100% margin, so even 3-5% take at $4.99-$9.99 is about $0.15-$0.50 per order of nearly pure profit; small against a $28 unit profit and an ad cost near $45-50 per order.

### Gaps
- No published take-rate-by-price-band table for physical e-commerce order bumps was found.
- No measured take rate for a digital add-on attached to a physical product on Shopify was found; all digital bump figures come from course/info-product checkouts.
- The Candy Rack P25-P75 ranges and add-to-cart-vs-sold breakdown exist on the source page but did not come through; needs a manual read.

## 2. In-cart upsells, cart-drawer cross-sells, progress bars and free-gift thresholds

### Takeaway
Measured in-cart take rates are low at the median (about 1.4-1.9% of views in the Candy Rack dataset), while vendor case studies report AOV lifts of roughly 8-12% and marketing guides claim 10-20% for progress bars. No independent effect size for "spend more to unlock" bars exists, and controlled tests of free-gift thresholds are mixed (one showed no significant effect).

### Cited Findings
- Median take rates: cart pop-up 1.9%, slide-cart (drawer) offer 1.4%. [VENDOR-DATA, 2026, n=3,199 stores] — [Digismoothie / Candy Rack](https://www.digismoothie.com/blog/upsell-benchmarks)
- Cart-drawer offers and pre-checkout pop-ups each returned more than $1,800 in upsell revenue per 1,000 offer views; only 2,433 of 16,600 stores ran a cart-drawer offer. [VENDOR-DATA, 2026] — [Zipify study press release](https://www.einnews.com/pr_news/957117063/zipify-study-of-123-9m-upsell-offers-finds-pre-checkout-pop-ups-convert-best-yet-half-as-many-shopify-stores-use-them)
- Progress-bar / threshold AOV lift claims, none backed by published test data: about 9% "typical" ([CustomFit playbook](https://customfit.ai/playbooks/aov-lift)); 12-18% for a threshold reward alone ([Cartylabs](https://cartylabs.com/blog/shopify-free-shipping-bar-strategy/)); 10-20% from a cart-drawer progress bar ([GrowthSuite](https://www.growthsuite.net/resources/shopify-upsell-cross-sell/cart-drawer-upsell/free-shipping-progress-bar)); 8-15% "expected" in a test template ([Intempt](https://intempt.com/recipes/free-shipping-progress-bar-test)). [VENDOR/BLOG, 2025-2026]
- A product-page bundle is estimated at roughly 5-9% AOV lift; the same playbook says to judge on revenue per session so an AOV gain is not confused with a conversion loss. [VENDOR/BLOG] — [CustomFit](https://customfit.ai/playbooks/aov-lift)
- Threshold guidance: set the threshold 20-30% (or 25-35%) above current AOV; one variable at a time, 2-4 weeks, 500+ cart views. [VENDOR/BLOG] — [GrowthSuite progress bar guide](https://www.growthsuite.net/resources/shopify-discount/progress-bar-aov-boost-strategy-guide)
- A threshold set too high can reduce checkout completions. [AGENCY opinion] — [MakeBeCool](https://www.makebecool.com/blog/the-fastest-way-to-increase-aov-on-shopify-without-changing-your-products-or-prices)
- Rebuy Smart Cart case studies (AOV lift on vendor-attributed orders): WMP Eyewear +9.5% over 90 days; Luno +10.17%; Fanjoy +11.7%; Cabau Lifestyle +27%; Ascent Nutrition +50%. These use "influenced/assisted" attribution, not incremental lift, and none reports a take rate. [VENDOR case studies] — [Rebuy: WMP Eyewear](https://www.rebuyengine.com/case-studies/wmp-eyewear); [Luno](https://www.rebuyengine.com/case-studies/luno); [Cabau](https://www.rebuyengine.com/case-studies/cabau-lifestyle); [Ascent Nutrition](https://www.rebuyengine.com/case-studies/ascent-nutrition); [Rebuy Smart Cart blog](https://www.rebuyengine.com/blog/rebuy-smart-cart)
- An agency reports an 8-figure fashion brand gained about 10% AOV with Rebuy's Smart Cart. [AGENCY, single test] — [Blue Stout](https://bluestout.com/cro-wins/how-an-8-figure-fashion-brand-boosted-aov-by-10-percent-with-rebuys-smart-cart/)
- Enlightened Equipment reportedly raised AOV 38.7% with a "smart up-sell bar". [VENDOR case study, outlier] — [Maestra](https://maestra.io/blog/cases/enlightened-equipment-up-sell/)
- A cart-drawer A/B test for a clothing brand reportedly added $0.62 revenue per visitor ($126k/month). [NEWSLETTER, single self-reported case] — [DTC Pages](https://dtc-pages.beehiiv.com/p/turn-your-cart-into-an-aov-boosting-machine)
- Practitioner view: a drawer with only line items and a checkout button performs like a cart page; any gain comes from the progress bar plus one relevant upsell. [COMMUNITY opinion] — [Shopify Community thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/do-cart-drawer-actually-moves-the-aov-needle/639628)
- Free gift with purchase, mixed evidence: a targeted free-gift offer with a $299 minimum lifted conversion "nearly 30%" and AOV "nearly 15%" ([Spork Marketing](https://sporkmarketing.com/case-studies/free-gift-conversion-boost/), AGENCY); Trekell reported +38% AOV in 3 months with tiered gifts, a before/after comparison not a split test ([AOV.ai](https://aov.ai/case-study/from-50-to-1000-how-trekells-graduated-gift-thresholds-created-a-canvas-for-success), VENDOR); Kuddly's experiment found free gifts "did not significantly boost AOV or conversion rates" ([Sculpt Digital](https://sculpt.digital/case-study/impact-of-free-gift-promotions-on-aov/), AGENCY; attribution uncertain).
- Judge gift offers on contribution margin per order after gift cost, not AOV. [VENDOR guidance] — [Intelligems](https://intelligems.io/resources/blog/should-i-offer-gift-with-purchase)
- Baymard (usability research, not conversion data): 52% of benchmarked desktop sites show cart cross-sells that are irrelevant or based only on "others bought"; one questionable recommendation was observed to make users ignore all suggestions; highly relevant compatible accessories are seen as "good service". [INDEPENDENT] — [Baymard: product recommendations in cart](https://baymard.com/blog/product-recommendations-cart)

### Inferences
- With a single SKU at $39.99 and existing 2-pack/3-pack prices of $64.99/$89.99, a "spend more to unlock" bar has only one natural step (to the 2-pack). A threshold 20-35% above a roughly $40 AOV is $48-$54, which nothing in the catalogue hits except the 2-pack; the bar is in effect another nudge to the 2-pack, not an independent lever.
- A realistic in-cart cross-sell expectation for a small store is 1.5-5% of cart viewers taking the offer; double-digit AOV lifts are case-study outliers with attribution bias.

### Gaps
- No independent A/B-tested effect size for progress bars or free-gift thresholds was found; every lift figure is vendor or agency marketing.
- Dynamic Yield and Shopify first-party benchmark figures for cart cross-sell take rate were not surfaced in this session.

## 3. Post-purchase one-click upsells (after payment)

### Takeaway
Reported acceptance rates span 3% to 25% depending on source. The two largest measured datasets give 4.7% (ReConvert, 2023) and 8.7% (Zipify, Nov 2025-Oct 2026) of offer views; Zipify also markets a 16.2% "average" elsewhere, which conflicts with its own study. Average AOV lift in the one dataset that reports it is 5.6%.

### Cited Findings
- Post-purchase offers converted 8.7% of views across 13,614 stores (of 16,600 in the study, 123.9M offer views total, $266.7M upsell revenue), Nov 2025-Oct 2026. The report itself notes that acceptance rates "in circulation" range from 3% to 25%. [VENDOR-DATA, 2026] — [Zipify study via EIN Presswire](https://www.einnews.com/pr_news/957117063/zipify-study-of-123-9m-upsell-offers-finds-pre-checkout-pop-ups-convert-best-yet-half-as-many-shopify-stores-use-them)
- Zipify's marketing pages state OCU merchants average a 16.2% take rate on post-purchase offers, citing "15,000+ stores" on one page and "50,000+ Shopify stores" on another. This conflicts with the 8.7% in Zipify's own November 2026 study and the store counts conflict with each other. [VENDOR marketing claim, 2026] — [Zipify 2026 playbook](https://zipify.com/blog-how-to-upsell-in-2026/); [Zipify Plus app comparison](https://zipify.com/blog-best-shopify-plus-upsell-app-in-2026/)
- ReConvert 2023 report: average one-click post-purchase upsell conversion about 4.7%; thank-you-page upsells 1.7%; post-purchase upselling lifted AOV by 5.6% on average; top 5% of users averaged 28.3% conversion; top stores earned 12.7% of revenue from post-purchase upsells. Sample described as "40,000+ merchants"/"tens of thousands of brands". The same vendor elsewhere cites 6.7% as the average rate. [VENDOR-DATA, 2023] — [ReConvert report](https://reconvert.io/blog/the-impact-of-post-purchase-upselling-2023); mirror: [Upsell.com](https://upsell.com/blog/the-impact-of-post-purchase-upselling-2023)
- Thank-you page offers: median take rate 0.7%. [VENDOR-DATA, 2026, n=3,199 stores] — [Digismoothie / Candy Rack](https://www.digismoothie.com/blog/upsell-benchmarks)
- "Healthy" post-purchase acceptance 3-8%; own dataset averages about 6% across 250 stores; tuned stores 8-10%; relevant offers 4-8% vs generic 1-3% (relevant converts 2-4x generic). [VENDOR/BLOG, 2026, n=250 stores claimed] — [GrowthSuite benchmarks](https://www.growthsuite.net/resources/shopify-upsell-cross-sell/complete-guide/benchmarks)
- Typical post-purchase upsell conversion "about 4%". [VENDOR] — [CartHook](https://carthook.com/blogs/wiki/what-is-the-conversion-rate-for-post-purchase-upsell)
- Higher claims: 14.6% for physical-goods post-purchase offers (attributed to Focus Digital's 2025 study by a third party; a different third party attributes 9.9% to the same study, and neither figure was found on Focus Digital's own pages); 10-35% range in a general guide. [AGENCY/BLOG, unverified] — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/post-purchase-upsell-thank-you-page-2026-ecommerce-playbook); [Upsella calculator](https://upsella-com.pages.dev/free-shopify-tools/upsell-calculator); [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/ecommerce-growth/post-purchase-upsells)
- What offer works: a same-product "buy one, get one" post-purchase offer gave one merchant a 16% lift in post-purchase conversion and $6.81 revenue per visitor on its most-viewed funnel. Single case, no control described. [VENDOR case study, 2026] — [Zipify 2026 playbook](https://zipify.com/blog-how-to-upsell-in-2026/)
- Stores running offers in three or more placements earn 3.6x the upsell revenue of post-purchase-only stores (correlational; larger stores run more placements). [VENDOR-DATA] — [Zipify](https://zipify.com/blog-how-to-upsell-in-2026/)
- Olivia Jewelry reported an extra $138,678 from post-purchase upsells. [VENDOR case study] — [ReConvert case study](https://www.reconvert.io/case-study/post-purchase-upsell-case-study)
- Recommended validation: hold out half of post-purchase traffic with no offer and compare. [BLOG guidance; attribution uncertain] — [Loop](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/shopify-post-purchase-upsell-boost-aov)

### Inferences
- Plan on 4-9% acceptance for a one-click post-purchase offer, with 15%+ as an optimistic case for a tightly matched same-product discount. The 16.2% Zipify average should be treated as marketing; Zipify's own measured study says 8.7%.
- Client arithmetic (inference from the figures above plus the client's stated margins): offering a second wristband post-purchase at about $25 (the existing 2-pack step-up) with roughly $17 incremental profit per acceptance gives about $0.70-$1.55 profit per single-unit order at 4-9% acceptance. Post-purchase offers cannot reduce the conversion of the original order, since payment is already complete, which is their main structural advantage.
- For a one-product store, "same product at a discount" is the only naturally relevant post-purchase offer; the relevance data (4-8% relevant vs 1-3% generic) supports that over an unrelated accessory.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head test of same-product-discount vs accessory vs upgrade post-purchase offers was found.
- No AfterSell-published platform benchmark (acceptance or revenue per order) was found.
- Revenue per order from post-purchase offers is rarely published; only the ReConvert 5.6% AOV lift and Zipify's per-1,000-views revenue are available.

## 4. Quantity breaks and bundles (buy 2 / buy 3)

### Takeaway
No reliable industry benchmark exists for the share of orders that choose a multi-unit tier; sources say to read it from the store's own order-quantity distribution. The only quantified default/pre-selection test found reported +26% revenue per visitor and +18% units per order, but it changed two things at once. No test isolating a "most popular" or "best value" badge on quantity breaks was found.

### Cited Findings
- Explicit absence of a benchmark: guides instruct merchants to export 90 days of orders and pivot line-item quantity; their percentages (e.g. "70% single unit, 20% two units") are illustrative only. [VENDOR/BLOG, 2026] — [Ninety9](https://ninety9.dev/blog/quantity-breaks-vs-volume-discounts.html); [Pumper](https://pumper.run/blog/quantity-break-pricing-guide/)
- Intelligems customer story: a home-essentials brand on pre-order tested a page offering single unit plus bundle (control) against a bundle-only page with the bundle pre-selected and the single-unit path removed. Variant: revenue per visitor +26%, units per order +18%, conversion +26%, AOV roughly flat, headline 22% profit lift. No sample size, duration or significance given; the test confounds pre-selection with removing the single option. [VENDOR case study, 2025-2026] — [Intelligems](https://www.intelligems.io/resources/customer-stories/how-a-home-essentials-brand-turned-a-bundle-only-offer-into-a-22-profit-lift)
- Bundles vs bundles plus cross-sells test: 3,019 users over 24 days; variant +17.4% revenue per visitor and +28.6% conversion rate. Small sample for those effect sizes. [AGENCY, stated sample] — [Process Creative](https://www.processcreative.com.au/blog/a-b-testing-bundles-vs-bundles-cross-sells-what-actually-drives-more-revenue)
- Kaching Bundles case studies: Axzenia +23% AOV and $109,800 additional revenue; Flush Packaging +23% AOV; TrailSurvivor "doubles AOV"; HELLO +450% AOV and $609k additional sales. None gives share of orders choosing each tier or a control group. [VENDOR case studies] — [Axzenia](https://www.casestudies.com/company/kaching-appz/case-study/how-axzenia-boosted-their-aov-by-23-and-generated-109800-in-additional-revenue); [Flush Packaging](https://www.casestudies.com/company/kaching-appz/case-study/flush-packaging-boosts-aov-by-23-and-makes-shopping-more-efficient-with-kaching-appz); [TrailSurvivor](https://www.casestudies.com/company/kaching-appz/case-study/trailsurvivor-doubles-aov-with-kaching-bundle-quantity-breaks); [HELLO](https://www.casestudies.com/company/kaching-appz/case-study/hello-does-609000-in-additional-sales-using-kaching-bundle-quantity-breaks)
- HiSmile reportedly had 80% of orders include bundles. [BLOG, third-party, unverified, large brand] — [eCommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/unlocking-sales-potential-with-shopify-bundle-builders/)
- Switching quantity-button apps (not adding tiers) gave one store +8% revenue per visitor ($2.19 vs $2.04), +8% gross profit per visitor, +5% conversion. [VENDOR test] — [Intelligems blog](https://www.intelligems.io/resources/blog/do-you-need-a-separate-app-for-quantity-breaks-if-you-use-intelligems)
- Counter-example: a $50 charger brand tested its volume discount (10% off 2+, 15% off 3+, 20% off 4+) against a $10 lower base price; it rolled out the lower price and removed the volume discount; headline says conversion rate and revenue per visitor about 2x. [VENDOR case study] — [Intelligems Clutch case study](https://intelligems.io/clutchchargercasestudy)
- Test metrics and duration: judge quantity-discount tests on units per order, AOV, revenue per visitor and profit per visitor; significance typically takes 2-4 weeks "depending on traffic"; a high add-to-cart rate with low multi-unit share means the discount is too shallow or the tiers not visible. [VENDOR docs] — [Intelligems docs](https://docs.intelligems.io/getting-started/ready-to-run-tests/make-more-per-order-with-volume-discounts)
- Fit: quantity breaks work best for consumable or replenishable goods or multi-user purchases; more tiers add complexity; "two tiers, not five" as a start. [VENDOR/BLOG] — [Personizely](https://help.personizely.net/en/article/inspiration-test-whether-inline-quantity-break-toggles-lift-units-per-order-and-aov-4fvznf/); [Intelligems](https://intelligems.io/resources/blog/tiered-discounts-or-flat-discounts)
- Default and badge evidence is indirect: shoppers tend to read a pre-selected option as the most popular one ([Taplytics](https://taplytics.com/blog/a-b-test-idea-use-the-principle-of-default-bias-in-your-pricing-pages), BLOG); Azuna Fresh tested "Popular" vs "Recommended" badges on subscription options and rolled out "Popular" ([Blend Commerce](https://blendcommerce.com/blogs/ab-tests-shopify/popular-subscription-badges-increase-pdp-revenue), AGENCY; subscription context, result size not captured).
- A subscription-context pre-selection test reported subscription share rising from about 3% to about 21% of orders when Subscribe & Save was placed first and pre-selected. [VENDOR/AGENCY, attribution uncertain between the two pages] — [Skio](https://skio.com/blog/smart-upsells-subscription-aov-lift) / [Convertibles](https://convertibles.dev/blogs/optimization/increase-subscription-rate-shopify)

### Inferences
- Client arithmetic: each buyer who moves from 1 unit ($39.99, about $28 profit) to the 2-pack ($64.99, about $45 profit) adds $25 revenue and about $17 profit. Every 10 percentage points of orders shifted to the 2-pack adds about $2.50 to AOV (about 6%) and about $1.70 profit per order. This is the only lever in this document whose plausible size (tens of percentage points of mix) is comparable to the gap between unit profit ($28) and ad cost per order (about £36).
- A silent-alarm wristband is not a consumable; the multi-unit case rests on couples, gifts and spares, so the multi-unit share is likely below that of consumable stores quoting 23%+ AOV lifts.
- Pre-selecting the 2-pack is a default on the main product choice, which is different in kind from pre-ticking a paid add-on (see section 5), but the Shopify consent rule and UK/EU pre-ticked-box rules mean the shopper must clearly see the selected option and price before adding to cart.

### Gaps
- No dataset reports the share of orders taking 1 vs 2 vs 3 units for single-product stores.
- No controlled test isolating "pre-select middle option" or a "most popular"/"best value" label on quantity breaks was found; Kaching does not publish tier-mix data.

## 5. Shipping protection and package insurance (unticked vs pre-ticked; pushback)

### Takeaway
Vendor figures put unticked opt-in at roughly 20-45% and pre-ticked at 40-80%, but the one before/after case with stated numbers shows unticked rates starting at 2-3% and reaching 8-19% after optimisation. Since 18 February 2025 Shopify requires explicit buyer consent and bars apps from pre-selecting optional paid items, and UK and EU law prohibit pre-ticked paid extras; one first-party A/B test also showed a 26% conversion drop from adding a protection widget.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify platform rule: changelog posted 16 Jan 2025; as of 18 Feb 2025 all published apps must display costs and obtain explicit buyer consent before adding any optional paid items to Storefront, Cart or Checkout. App Store requirements state apps cannot automatically add or pre-select optional charges that increase the checkout total. [INDEPENDENT / platform policy, 2025] — [Shopify dev changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/new-buyer-consent-requirement); [Shopify App Store requirements](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/app-store-requirements.md)

[all VENDOR, 2025-2026]

- Conversion harm: in an A/B test against no widget, a food brand's first-time conversion rate fell from 1.30% to 0.96% (-26%) after adding a shipping protection app; the author argues protection apps report attach rate and fee revenue, hiding lost orders, and recommends opt-in, cart placement and plain labelling, judged on revenue per visitor. [Analytics vendor (not a protection seller), first-party single-store test, Oct 2026] — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/growth/shipping-protection-conversion-rate)
- Headline of an agency case study: "+24% conversion with the fee off by default"; the search summary also described "no clear winner", so the result needs a direct read. [AGENCY, single test] — [Convertibles](https://convertibles.dev/blogs/case-studies/shipping-protection-toggle-default-off-case-study)
- "Pre-ticked boxes convert once, then create chargebacks and tickets." [BLOG opinion] — [Ninety9](https://ninety9.dev/blog/shipping-protection-in-the-cart.html)
- Vendor compliance guidance: protection should not be pre-checked and a clear way to proceed without it should be offered. [VENDOR] — [Corso](https://help.corso.com/getting-started/ensure-customer-transparency/)
- A vendor claims many brands see strong opt-in "without noticeable conversion loss". [VENDOR claim, contradicts Lebesgue] — [U-PIC](https://u-pic.com/shipping-protection-at-checkout)
- UK: under the DMCC Act regime, traders cannot use pre-ticked boxes or automatic opt-in for optional extras the customer must pay for unless they opt out; drip pricing is prohibited; the CMA opened investigations and sent advisory letters to about 100 firms, citing default opt-ins for additional services among findings. [INDEPENDENT law-firm commentary on regulator action, Nov 2025; attribution across the two firms uncertain] — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/11/18/breaking-news-the-uks-consumer-law-revolution-starts-here-cma-fires-starting-p-102luuz); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/cma-clamps-down-on-checkout-tricks-as-consumer-enforcement-pipeline-accelerates)
- EU: Article 22 of the Consumer Rights Directive prohibits pre-ticked boxes for additional payments. [INDEPENDENT] — [Deceptive Patterns, ch. 27](https://bunny.deceptive.design/book/contents/chapter-27)
- US: the FTC junk-fees rule (16 CFR Part 464) covers only live-event tickets and short-term lodging; pre-checked add-ons elsewhere are pursued under FTC Act Section 5; California SB 478 bans drip pricing of mandatory fees economy-wide (optional add-ons and reasonable shipping are outside it). A secondary source reports a $35 million FTC settlement with a travel app over pre-ticked add-ons and misleading totals; not verified against an FTC release. [BLOG/secondary legal commentary, 2026; attribution uncertain] — [Audit Socials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/dark-patterns-ad-funnel-2026-eu-digital-fairness-act-dsa-article-25-ftc-junk-fees-countdown-scarcity-drip-pricing); [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/19/hopper-ftc-35-million-hidden-fees-add-on-upsell-compliance-guide)
- Baymard lists sneaking/pre-selected extras among deceptive patterns that cost conversion and trust. [INDEPENDENT] — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/dark-deceptive-patterns-ecommerce-ux)

### Inferences
- Pre-ticking the $2.99 protection is not a usable option: Shopify app rules bar it, UK law (relevant because the operator appears UK-based) and EU law prohibit it, and US enforcement risk exists under Section 5. Plan on unticked opt-in only.
- Realistic unticked attach for a $40 single-item order: 5-20% as a cautious range (Felix Gray 8-19%, Voro 16%), with vendor claims of 25-45% as the optimistic case. At $2.99 that is about $0.15-$0.60 per order cautious, up to $1.35 optimistic, before the cost of honouring claims on own-brand protection.
- The downside is asymmetric: the one controlled test found a 26% conversion drop on a first-order funnel. A 26% loss of orders at $28 profit each far exceeds a $0.60 per-order gain. The current $2.99 protection should be judged on revenue (or profit) per visitor with and without the widget, not on attach rate.

### Gaps
- Neither Route nor Navidium publishes an opt-in rate; no neutral multi-store dataset of unticked attach rates after February 2025 was found.
- The Lebesgue test is one food brand; no replication across categories was found.
- Whether Shopify's rule binds a merchant's own hand-coded theme toggle (as opposed to an app) was not confirmed from Shopify's text.

## 6. Extended warranties / protection plans on low-priced electronics

### Takeaway
Typical e-commerce warranty attach is about 4% by one vendor's own baseline, with vendor case studies at 9-22% on higher-priced goods. Attach rates rise with price (notably above $100) and plans for sub-$50 items are priced poorly relative to the product, so a third-party plan on a $39.99 gadget is likely worth very little.

### Cited Findings
- "A typical e-commerce attach rate for consumer brands is 4%", varying by vertical. [VENDOR, Clyde, about 2021-2022] — [Clyde: the metrics that matter](https://joinclyde.com/blog/the-metrics-that-matter)
- Clyde's reported platform average attach rate: 18% (TechCrunch, 2020) vs 14% (partner page). [VENDOR/press, 2020; older] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/05/11/clyde-raises-14-million-series-a-to-help-e-commerce-businesses-offer-extended-warranty-plans); [Object Edge](https://objectedge.com/technology-partners/clyde)
- Clyde case studies: Senville 12% overall; EVO Gimbals 22% on cameras; Liteboxer 38.6% after optimisation (high-priced fitness hardware). [VENDOR case studies] — [Senville](https://joinclyde.com/case-study/senville); [EVO Gimbals](https://joinclyde.com/case-study/evo-gimbals); [Liteboxer](https://joinclyde.com/case-study/liteboxer)
- Extend case studies: Baby Brezza 8.8% overall attach; BlendJet 11.4% (a roughly $50 product, the closest price analogue found); Boogie Board 13.5%; Dufresne (furniture) 40% in top categories. [VENDOR case studies] — [Baby Brezza](https://www.extend.com/merchant-case-studies/baby-brezza); [BlendJet](https://www.casestudies.com/company/extend/case-study/blendjet-increases-sitewide-conversion-rate-with-500k-in-extend-product-protection-sales); [Boogie Board](https://www.extend.com/merchant-case-studies/boogie-board); [Dufresne](https://extend.com/merchant-case-studies/the-dufresne-group-canada)
- Extend's co-founder: higher-priced items have higher attach rates, a trend that "kicks in for prices above $100". [VENDOR interview via analyst firm] — [Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/extend-ramps-retailers-eye-extended-warranty-shipping-protection-plans)
- Pricing: electronics plans typically 8-15% of retail price depending on term and accidental-damage cover. [VENDOR] — [Corso](https://www.corso.com/post-purchase-resource-center/product-protection-plans-by-merchandise-category)
- Pricing on cheap items: a Warranty Week price survey found computer-accessory plans averaged 26% of product price, with an outlier of a $43 two-year plan on a $10 mouse (410%). [INDEPENDENT trade publication, Feb 2025] — [Warranty Week](https://warrantyweek.com/archive/ww20250206.html)
- Big-box retailers sell Allstate plans in price bands, including a 3-year electronics band for $0-$50 items and bands such as $50-$74.99 and $50-$99.99. [Retailer listings] — [QVC](https://www.qvc.com/allstate-protection-plan-3-year-electronics%240-%2450.product.E210768.html); [Target](https://www.target.com/p/allstate-2-year-electronics-protection-plan/-/A-51514010); [Sam's Club](https://www.samsclub.com/ip/Allstate-3-Year-Protection-Plan-General-Merchandise-50-to-99-99/13866424775)
- Claims: average US manufacturer warranty claims rate was 1.30% of product revenue in 2025 (accrual rate 1.43%), all product categories; this is manufacturer warranty cost as a share of sales, not the share of extended-warranty buyers who claim. [INDEPENDENT, Apr 2026] — [Warranty Week](https://www.warrantyweek.com/archive/ww20260416.html)
- Consumer electronics makers historically spend about 0.5% of product revenue on warranty; computer makers 2-3%. [INDEPENDENT, 2013; old] — [Warranty Week](https://warrantyweek.com/archive/ww20130718.html)
- A blog cites e-commerce warranty claim rates of 1-3% for electronics up to 5-10% for general consumer goods. [BLOG, weak sourcing] — [Opensend](https://www.opensend.com/post/warranty-claim-rate-statistics-ecommerce)
- Attach rates track ticket price, perceived failure risk and repair inconvenience; one guide advises offering plans on phones, computers, furniture and fitness gear, "not on disposable items". [BLOG/VENDOR] — [Shopappy](https://shopappy.com/retail/protection-plans-guide)

### Inferences
- At $39.99, a plan priced at the typical 8-15% would be $3-$6; at the 26% accessory average, about $10. With attach likely at or below the 4% baseline for a sub-$100 item, the expected value is roughly $0.10-$0.40 per order, before any revenue share to a provider. An own-brand "extended warranty" at $4.99 keeps the full fee but creates a real liability.
- The existing $2.99 shipping protection and a product warranty compete for the same "peace of mind" budget and the same checkbox attention; running both on a $40 item is likely to dilute each.

### Gaps
- No attach-rate data specific to sub-$50 electronics was found, and no Mulberry figures at all.
- Extend, Clyde/Cover Genius and Mulberry minimum price or volume thresholds were not found; a 2-order store may not qualify for these programmes.
- No published claim rate (share of plan buyers who claim) for cheap electronics plans was found.

## 8. Do product-page upsells lower base conversion, and how many offers is too many?

### Takeaway
The evidence is a handful of agency tests that point both ways: upsells placed above the buy button or as forced steps have cost 9-10% of conversions, while removing them can cut AOV by more than the conversion gained. The consistent guidance is one relevant offer per stage, placed below the add-to-cart button or moved to cart or post-purchase, judged on revenue per visitor.

### Cited Findings
- Removing a priority-processing upsell above add-to-cart: +9% conversion. [AGENCY, single test] — [Blue Stout](https://bluestout.com/cro-wins/could-your-upsell-be-causing-confusion-try-this/)
- Moving product-page upsells below the add-to-cart button: +16.3% conversions and +53.3% revenue per session. [AGENCY, single test; attribution uncertain] — [Blue Stout: use your PDP upsells wisely](https://bluestout.com/cro-wins/use-your-pdp-upsells-wisely/)
- Streamlined product page with cross-sell and upsell sections removed: conversion +10.4%, AOV -11.9%, revenue per visitor -2.7%. That is, conversion rose but the store earned less. [AGENCY, single test; the figures could not be re-confirmed in a second search] — [Clean Commit](https://cleancommit.io/ab-tests/streamlined-product-page-for-conversion/)
- Adding cross-sells to a bundle page raised conversion 28.6% and revenue per visitor 17.4% (3,019 users, 24 days); the agency concludes the idea that more offers hurt conversion is "outdated" and that relevance is the driver. [AGENCY, small sample] — [Process Creative](https://www.processcreative.com.au/blog/a-b-testing-bundles-vs-bundles-cross-sells-what-actually-drives-more-revenue)
- Adding a shipping-protection widget: first-time conversion -26% (1.30% to 0.96%) in one A/B test. [Analytics vendor, single store, 2026] — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/growth/shipping-protection-conversion-rate)
- Baymard usability testing: 66% of users who had to pass through a separate cross-sell step during Amazon's checkout showed extreme frustration; forced accept/reject cross-sells are perceived as highly aggressive; carts cluttered with offers feel "pushy" and distract from checkout. [INDEPENDENT, qualitative usability research; the Amazon test is several years old] — [Baymard cross-sell step benchmark](https://baymard.com/checkout-usability/benchmark/step-type/cross-sell); [Baymard cart recommendations](https://baymard.com/blog/product-recommendations-cart)
- Most Shopify stores in Zipify's dataset run upsells in only 1 or 2 placements. [VENDOR-DATA, 2026] — [Zipify](https://zipify.com/blog-how-to-upsell-in-2026/)
- Most common failure mode: AOV rises while conversion drops enough to lower total revenue; split by visitor, not page view, and measure revenue per visitor. [BLOG] — [Ninety9](https://ninety9.dev/blog/ab-test-shopify-popups.html)

### Inferences
- No source gives a numeric "maximum number of offers". The practical reading of the tests: at most one optional offer near the buy button (and for this store the quantity selector already is that offer), one in the cart, one after payment. The store already shows bundles plus shipping protection before purchase, so any further pre-purchase add-on is the third ask.
- For a store paying about £36 per order in ads against $28 unit profit, a conversion loss is far more expensive than any add-on gain: a 9-10% conversion drop raises effective ad cost per order by about 10-11%, roughly £3.50-£4, which exceeds the per-order value of every add-on in sections 1, 5, 6 and 7.
- Post-purchase offers are the only placement with zero risk to conversion of the original order.

### Gaps
- No large-sample or independent study quantifies conversion loss as a function of the number of upsell offers.
- All tests found are single-store agency reports without confidence intervals.

## 9. Sample size needed before a small store can tell whether an upsell is working

### Takeaway
Standard guidance needs hundreds of conversions per variant (200-1,000), which this store will not reach for months; with 2 orders, nothing can be concluded. At low volume the workable approach is to track each add-on's attach rate with a confidence interval, use roughly 100 orders as the first read on attach rates, and accept that conversion-rate effects of an upsell cannot be measured at all without thousands of visitors per arm.

### Cited Findings
- Conversions-per-variant rules of thumb: CXL about 350+; others 200+; about 400 when the expected lift is around 20%; about 1,000+ as a "gold standard"; Bayesian approaches about 100-500; directional/bandit-style reads about 50-100. [BLOG/VENDOR guides, 2025-2026; attribution across pages uncertain] — [Fudge sample-size guide](https://www.fudge.ai/guides/shopify-ab-test-sample-size/); [Mida](https://mida.so/blog/how-much-traffic-ab-testing); [Clean Commit](https://cleancommit.io/blog/how-to-ab-test-low-traffic-store/); [Tailor](https://tailorhq.ai/guides/traffic-thresholds)
- Worked example: at a 3% baseline conversion rate, detecting a 20% relative lift needs about 13,911 visitors per variant; a 5% lift needs about 207,936 per variant. [VENDOR calculator example; attribution uncertain] — [Fudge](https://www.fudge.ai/guides/shopify-ab-test-sample-size/)
- If the required sample would take more than about eight weeks of eligible traffic, the test is not worth running as a randomised test. [BLOG guidance; attribution uncertain] — [Tailor](https://tailorhq.ai/guides/traffic-thresholds)
- Keep to two variants, make bigger changes, and do not stop early; underpowered tests produce false positives. [BLOG] — [Clean Commit](https://cleancommit.io/blog/how-to-ab-test-low-traffic-store/)
- Vendor test guidance: 2-4 weeks to significance "depending on traffic"; 500+ cart views per variant for cart tests. [VENDOR] — [Intelligems docs](https://docs.intelligems.io/getting-started/ready-to-run-tests/make-more-per-order-with-volume-discounts); [GrowthSuite](https://www.growthsuite.net/resources/shopify-upsell-cross-sell/cart-drawer-upsell/free-shipping-progress-bar)
- Bundle-specific sample-size and duration calculator exists. [VENDOR tool] — [ShopSidek](https://shopsidek.com/shopify-bundle-ab-test-sample-size/)

### Inferences
(Calculated for this brief with standard formulas: Wilson 95% interval for a single proportion; two-proportion test at 5% significance and 80% power. These are my calculations, not sourced figures.)
- How uncertain an observed attach rate is: 0 of 10 orders still allows a true rate up to about 28%; 3 of 10 means anywhere from 11% to 60%; 0 of 30 allows up to about 11%; 3 of 30 means 4-26%; 9 of 30 means 17-48%; 10 of 100 means 5.5-17%; 30 of 100 means 22-40%; 20 of 200 means 7-15%; 50 of 500 means 8-13%.
- Practical reading: about 30 orders tells you only whether an add-on is "near zero" or "clearly popular"; about 100 orders pins an attach rate to within roughly plus or minus 6-9 points, enough to rank offers and to price the offer's value per order; 300-500 orders are needed for plus or minus 3 points.
- Orders per arm needed to compare two versions of an offer: 5% vs 10% take needs about 430 orders per arm; 10% vs 20% about 200; 20% vs 30% about 290; 20% vs 40% about 80; 30% vs 45% (e.g. 2-pack share with and without pre-selection) about 160. Only large differences are detectable on a small store.
- Whether an upsell hurts base conversion is far harder: at a 2% conversion rate, detecting a relative change of a fifth (2.0% vs 2.4%) needs about 21,000 visitors per arm; a much larger change (2% vs 3%) still needs about 3,800 per arm. At roughly £36 per order, a 21,000-visitor arm is unaffordable, so conversion-side effects must be taken from external evidence (section 8) rather than measured in-store.
- With zero observed events in n orders, the 95% upper bound on the true rate is roughly 3/n (the "rule of three"): no takers in 30 orders means the true rate is probably under 10%.
- Sequencing matters at low volume: change one offer at a time, run each for a fixed order count (e.g. 50-100 orders) and compare before/after profit per order and per visitor, accepting that before/after reads are confounded by ad-creative and seasonality changes.
- At 2 orders, the store has no usable data on any upsell; the priority is base conversion and ad cost, and the choice of upsells should rest on the external ranges above until at least 30-50 orders exist.

### Gaps
- No source addresses upsell testing specifically for stores with fewer than 100 orders; all published guidance assumes thousands of sessions per week.
- The sources for the rule-of-thumb thresholds are vendor blogs restating CXL and standard calculators; the original CXL article was not opened.
