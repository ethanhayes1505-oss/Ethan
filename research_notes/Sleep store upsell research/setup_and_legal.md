## 1. Digital products on Shopify (sleep guide PDF/audio): delivery apps, costs, mixed carts, DSers, US sales tax

### Takeaway
Shopify's own Digital Downloads app is the default, low-cost route for a single PDF/audio file and supports a digital item in the same order as a physical one; the digital product must be set as not-a-physical-product so it adds no shipping and can be auto-fulfilled. US sales tax on a $6.99 digital guide is a real but, at 2 orders, almost certainly not-yet-triggered issue: economic-nexus thresholds are typically $100,000 of sales into a state, but digital goods are taxable in roughly 30 states and all sales count towards the thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify's Digital Downloads app can "host and deliver files up to 5GB", and Shopify says a single product can combine physical and digital components (e.g. a record with an MP3 download) — [Shopify digital products page](https://www.shopify.com/digital-products). Third-party write-ups repeat the 5 GB per-file cap and say bandwidth is not capped and folders must be zipped — [Cartmango](https://cartmango.com/sell-digital-products-on-shopify/).
- Shopify Help Center (Digital Downloads): download limits are set per product in the app ("To remove a download limit, set the limit to 0"); a delivery email can be re-sent from the app's Orders menu ("a button will allow you to resend the fulfillment email"); digital-only items skip shipping and the physical-product setting must be turned off for them — [Shopify Help Center – Digital Downloads](https://help.shopify.com/manual/apps/apps-by-shopify/digital-downloads) (search extract only; page not opened).
- Users report an undocumented cap of 50 files per product in Digital Downloads; workaround is a single ZIP — [Shopify Community thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/digital-downloads-50-file-upload-limitation/298215) (anecdotal).
- Mixed carts: un-ticking "This is a physical product" on the digital item keeps it out of the shipping calculation ("the shipping payload sent by Shopify at checkout excludes any products that are unchecked as a physical product"), while the physical line still ships normally — [Shopify Community – shipping rates for digital & physical products](https://community.shopify.com/t/shipping-rates-for-digital-physical-products/166135) (community answer, not official doc).
- Mixed-order fulfilment: digital lines should be auto-fulfilled (app setting or a Shopify Flow workflow that marks non-shipping line items fulfilled) while the physical line stays unfulfilled for the fulfilment service — [MESA guide](https://www.getmesa.com/blog/shopify-auto-fulfill-digital-products). A Shopify community thread reports the Flow template "Fulfill any digital items in an order" correctly fulfilling digital lines in mixed orders, but in that case physical items then failed to sync to the merchant's 3PL; a suggested fix was a ~10-minute Flow "Wait" step before digital fulfilment — [Shopify Community – Flow template thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/flow-template-fulfill-any-digital-items-in-an-order-and-orders-with-physical-digital-products/389073); [second thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-to-input-a-time-delay-on-fulfillment-status-for-orders-with-a-mix-of-physical-and-digital-products/563694).
- Sky Pilot: the Shopify App Store listing shows a free plan with "100MB files Storage" — [Sky Pilot listing](https://apps.shopify.com/sky-pilot?locale=en) (search extract). A directory records a free plan as of 7 May 2026 and a 4.9 rating from 537 reviews — [Shoplist](https://shoplist.co/shopify-apps/sky-pilot-digital-downloads). Paid tiers not confirmed.
- Fileflare: App Store listing says "Free plan available" and a free trial — [Fileflare listing](https://apps.shopify.com/digital-assets) (search extract). Free-plan storage cap and paid prices not confirmed.
- SendOwl: a comparison blog says it "Starts at $4.99/month for 10 digital products" — [Tevello comparison](https://tevello.com/blogs/comparisons/sky-pilot-digital-downloads-vs-sendowl-an-in-depth-comparison) (third-party; NOT confirmed on SendOwl's pricing page). The many `conversionproplus.com` comparison pages contradict each other on prices and are a competitor's SEO content; treat as unreliable.
- Post-purchase offers and digital items: Shopify's developer docs note that if a checkout produces an order without a shipping address (e.g. digital-only), a subscription cannot be added through post-purchase; and orders must be $0.50 or more to qualify for post-purchase offers — [shopify.dev – About product offers](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/checkout/product-offers).
- US sales tax, nexus: the common economic-nexus benchmark is $100,000 of gross sales into a state in the current or prior calendar year; thresholds are set state by state; Illinois dropped its 200-transaction test from 1 Jan 2026; California and Texas use $500,000 — [Kintsugi economic nexus guide 2026](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus) (vendor guide).
- US sales tax, digital goods: counts vary by source — "approximately 30 states tax digital downloads as tangible personal property, and 15 states exempt them" vs "28 + D.C." taxing at least some digital products — [Dodo Payments 2026 guide](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/sales-tax-digital-goods-by-state); [VATCalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/united-states/us-sales-tax-on-digital-services/). Thresholds count all sales, not only taxable ones, so exempt digital sales still count towards nexus — [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/sales-tax/sales-tax-on-digital-goods-by-state/) (vendor blogs; treat counts as approximate).

### Inferences
- For one ~$6.99 PDF/audio file, Shopify's free first-party Digital Downloads app is sufficient (5 GB/file is far above need). Third-party apps (Sky Pilot, Fileflare, SendOwl) add things like streaming, PDF stamping or licence keys that this product does not need. (Inference; app feature claims not verified on listings.)
- Setup pattern for the mixed cart: create the guide as a separate product, un-tick physical product/shipping, attach the file in Digital Downloads, set automatic fulfilment for the digital item. The wristband line stays unfulfilled until DSers fulfils it. The guide must NOT be mapped to an AliExpress supplier in DSers.
- The one practical risk in the mixed order is fulfilment-status interference: the community threads show an order going "partially fulfilled" immediately (digital line) can confuse a downstream fulfilment sync. Test one mixed order end-to-end with DSers before promoting the guide.
- Sales tax: with US revenue in the low hundreds of dollars, the store is far below every state's economic-nexus threshold cited, so no state registration is likely triggered yet by sales volume alone. This is an inference from the thresholds, not tax advice; a foreign seller with no US presence is treated as a remote seller under the same economic-nexus tests (see Gaps).
- If the guide is sold bundled inside the wristband price (free bonus) rather than as a separate line, there is no separate digital-good line to classify; but bundling rules vary by state.

### Gaps
- Could not open the Shopify Help Center page, so not confirmed first-hand: that the app is free, exactly how the download link is delivered (email plus thank-you/order status page link), and auto- vs manual-fulfilment settings. My background understanding is that the app is free and delivers by email and a checkout download link, but this was not verified in this session.
- No source found on how DSers treats an unmapped digital line item in a mixed order (whether it shows as "unmapped/to-do" noise or is ignored). Needs a live test or DSers support.
- Paid pricing for Sky Pilot, Fileflare and SendOwl not confirmed from vendor pages.
- No authoritative source found on foreign-seller (UK sole trader) specifics for US state sales tax registration, or on how Shopify Tax classifies a digital guide per state. A US sales-tax adviser is needed once volume grows; Shopify Tax's product-category setting for digital goods should be checked in admin.
- Whether a PDF guide with an audio file is an "ebook", "digital audio work" or "other digital good" under each state's definitions was not researched state by state.

## 2. Post-purchase and checkout upsells on a non-Plus store in 2026 (what Shopify allows; AfterSell, ReConvert, Zipify OCU; Shop Pay / Installments)

### Takeaway
On a standard (non-Plus) plan the store cannot place app blocks inside the information/shipping/payment steps, but it CAN run a one-click post-purchase offer page (between payment and thank-you) and thank-you/order-status page extensions via an app. The post-purchase page is not shown for wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay, Amazon Pay) or instalment methods (Klarna, Affirm, Afterpay; app vendors say Shop Pay Installments too), so only a share of orders will ever see the offer. Shopify's rules for these offers forbid pre-selected paid add-ons and checkout countdown timers.

### Cited Findings
- Plan gating (official): "Checkout UI extensions that render on the information and shipping and payment steps in checkout are available only to stores on a Shopify Plus plan." Post-purchase extensions add UI to the post-purchase page; "Extensions that appear after a purchase has been completed will be available to all plans except Shopify Starter." — [shopify.dev – About checkout app extensions](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/checkout-extensions).
- Post-purchase page limitations (official, shopify.dev):
  - Not surfaced when "the customer chooses to check out with an installment service or a wallet service (such as Klarna, Affirm, AfterPay, Apple Pay, Amazon Pay, or Google Pay)" or when "the initial purchase was made with a gift card or any payment method other than a credit card."
  - Third-party gateways that need the CVV retained are unsupported.
  - Not surfaced on orders with duties or multiple currencies; not on local delivery; only on the Online Store sales channel.
  - Order must be $0.50 or more; a customer can accept a maximum of three post-purchase offers per checkout; only one post-purchase page; only one app can be selected for post-purchase offers (chosen in the admin checkout settings).
  - Third-party pixels (Google, Facebook etc.) "report only the purchase event and value for the initial purchase" — i.e. upsell revenue is under-reported to Meta.
  - Source: [shopify.dev – About product offers](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/checkout/product-offers).
- Shop Pay: Shopify says post-purchase UI components are "managed by Shopify to ensure that the checkout is fast, optimized for mobile, and integrated with Shop Pay" — [shopify.dev – Build a post-purchase offer](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/checkout/product-offers/build-a-post-purchase-offer). App vendors state that Shop Pay paid in full works but "the 4-installment payment method in Shop Pay is not supported for post-purchase offers. Only full-payment Shop Pay checkouts qualify", and that express/accelerated checkout buttons cause the page to be skipped — [AOV/Avada docs on Shopify limitations](https://docs.avada.io/aov-post-purchase-upsell/reference/shopify-limitations-and-considerations-for-post-purchase-checkout-extensions); [AfterSell payment methods doc](https://aftersell.mintlify.app/aftersell/payment_methods); [ReConvert help](https://help.reconvert.io/en/article/post-purchase-pages-not-displaying-shopify-12cy0rv) (vendor docs, consistent with each other). Sources conflict on PayPal (one says PayPal Express works only with reference transactions enabled; another lists PayPal as unsupported).
- Shopify App Store requirements that bind these apps (and state Shopify's expectations of merchants' checkout experiences):
  - 5.6.5: "Apps can't automatically add or pre-select optional charges to a buyer's cart that increase the total checkout price. Apps can only add optional charges … after displaying the additional cost in a manner that is clear to the buyer, and upon obtaining explicit buyer consent."
  - 5.6.6: "Checkout UI Extension apps must not add countdown timers to the checkout."
  - 5.6.4: checkout extensions must "display the same product name, image, and cost as the product in the merchant's store."
  - Source: [shopify.dev – App Store requirements](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/app-store-requirements).
- Post-purchase app best practice (Shopify): redirect to order confirmation after the customer responds; "limit consecutive requests to 3 and ensure customers can accept or decline offers"; pricing must be accurate and match the store price; "for limited-time offer details, clearly disclose them"; no third-party promotions — [shopify.dev – App Store best practices](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/best-practices).
- Buyer-consent rule date: "As of Tuesday, February 18, 2025, all published apps must display costs and obtain explicit buyer consent before adding any optional paid items to Storefront, Cart, or Checkout… These requirements apply to both packaged Checkout UI extensions and any downloadable code provided by the app or app partner across Storefront, Cart, and Checkout." — [Shopify developer changelog, 16 Jan 2025](https://shopify.dev/changelog/posts/new-buyer-consent-requirement).
- Thank-you / order-status page legacy code: additional scripts and checkout.liquid were sunset on those pages on 28 Aug 2025; script tags stopped on 28 Aug 2025 for Plus and "are sunset for non-Plus stores on August 26, 2026"; replacements are checkout/customer-account UI extensions (app blocks) and web pixels — [shopify.dev – Order status script tags](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/online-store/script-tag-deprecation/order-status); [shopify.dev – checkout.liquid](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/architecture/layouts/checkout-liquid). As of this research date that deadline has passed, so any thank-you-page upsell must be an app block/extension, not pasted script.
- AfterSell (now "Aftersell") pricing — NOT confirmed on the live listing; sources conflict:
  - App Store listing as extracted by search: free tier "only for development & partner stores"; paid tiers by monthly order band, 0–500 orders at $34.99/month, 501–1,000 at $54.99, 1,001–2,000 at $99.99; free trial (30 days per several sources) — [Aftersell App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/aftersell); [Flowium partner page](https://flowium.com/partners/aftersell/).
  - Vendor billing doc describes a newer model for new merchants: "a low base fee plus a small per-order fee on each order above 500 per month, with a hard monthly cap"; first 500 orders/month carry no per-order fee; orders counted over the trailing 30 days; existing merchants grandfathered; checkout functionality bundled into its Post-purchase and Complete plans — [Aftersell billing & pricing doc](https://aftersell.mintlify.app/aftersell/aftersell_billing_pricing). Dollar amounts were not in the extract.
  - One directory claims a free "Rokt Thanks" plan (ad-funded thank-you-page offers); another says there is no free plan — [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/aftersell/pricing); [Adtribute](https://www.adtribute.io/directory/aftersell). Unresolved.
- ReConvert pricing — NOT confirmed on the live listing; sources conflict: several 2026 comparison pages say a free tier exists for stores under roughly 49–50 orders/month with paid plans from $7.99/month (up to 99 orders); another says entry is $4.99 with up to 200 orders; another says no permanent free plan; a directory mirror says the free plan is for development stores — [app-compare ReConvert pricing](https://app-compare.pages.dev/pricing/reconvert/); [softwares.com](https://www.softwares.com/software/reconvert/pricing); [xpay directory](https://www.xpay.sh/directory/shopify/apps/reconvert-upsell-cross-sell/) (all third-party).
- Zipify OCU pricing — NOT confirmed: listing shows "a free plan and a free trial" but a directory mirror says the free plan is for development and partner stores; third-party figures range from $34.99/month (post-purchase plan) to tiers "starting at $9.95" by upsell revenue; Zipify's own page says "One plan. Every placement. Every feature." — [Zipify OCU listing](https://apps.shopify.com/zipify-oneclickupsell); [Zipify OCU page](https://zipify.com/apps/ocu/); [Zipify billing help](https://help.zipify.com/en/articles/4571658-plan-billing-for-your-subscription-ocu); [app-compare](https://app-compare.pages.dev/pricing/zipify/).
- Zipify (vendor, July 2026) states post-purchase offers run on any Shopify plan while in-checkout offers are Plus-only — [Zipify blog](https://zipify.com/blog-post-purchase-upsells-shopify-2026/).

### Inferences
- The "discounted second unit after checkout" is exactly what the post-purchase page is for: one-click accept, charged to the same card, added to the same order. For DSers this means the order's wristband quantity becomes 2 after acceptance; DSers should be checked to confirm it picks up the edited quantity (untested).
- Because wallets, instalments and non-card payments skip the page, expect well under 100% of buyers to see the offer. A thank-you-page block (available on all plans except Starter) catches the rest but requires re-entering payment (a new order), so conversion will be lower.
- At 2 orders total, a $35/month post-purchase app (if the $34.99 entry price is right) costs more than the store's gross margin to date. Cheaper routes to the same "second unit" outcome with no app: a quantity-break on the product page (see Q3), or a discount code for a second unit in the order-confirmation email. Whether a genuinely free tier exists for live stores (ReConvert <50 orders; Aftersell "Rokt Thanks") is unconfirmed and should be checked on the listings.
- Any "limited-time" framing on the post-purchase page must be true (Shopify requires limited-time details to be clearly disclosed; FTC treats fake timers as deceptive, see Q5). Countdown timers are banned inside checkout extensions; the post-purchase page rule is "clearly disclose", not an express ban, but a timer that resets is deceptive regardless.
- A digital guide can be offered post-purchase, but the main order must have been paid by credit card/Shop Pay in full.

### Gaps
- Live App Store pricing for Aftersell, ReConvert and Zipify OCU could not be opened; all figures above are second-hand and inconsistent. Must be read off the listings on the day of install (record the date).
- Shopify's own Help Center page on the post-purchase page (`help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/post-purchase`) could not be opened; the Shop Pay Installments exclusion is confirmed only by multiple app vendors plus Shopify's generic "installment service" wording.
- Not confirmed whether DSers handles a post-purchase order edit (added line/quantity) cleanly.
- Whether Shopify's native thank-you-page customisation (checkout editor) lets a non-Plus merchant add a product-offer block without any app was not confirmed.

## 3. Quantity-break and add-on apps versus custom theme code (Kaching Bundles and similar)

### Takeaway
Kaching Bundles appears to have no free plan for live stores (free for development stores only) and is reported at roughly $10–$20/month at entry, but the figure differs by source and was not confirmed. Product-page tick-box add-ons and simple quantity pricing can be built in theme code or with Shopify's native discount functions at zero app cost, provided add-ons are unticked by default and itemised.

### Cited Findings
- Kaching Bundles: comparison sources say the free plan "covers Shopify development stores only" and a live store needs a paid plan after a trial (7 days per one source, 14 days per another) — [Qikify comparison](https://qikify.com/blogs/all-articles/qikify-vs-kaching-bundles); [Instant.so](https://instant.so/blog/free-versus-paid-shopify-bundle-apps) (third-party; one is a competitor).
- Kaching paid price is reported inconsistently: "$14.99/mo"; "Basic Yearly at $9.99/month and Basic Monthly at $14.99/month after a 14-day trial"; and "paid plans from $19.99 to $59.99/month" — [Qikify](https://qikify.com/blogs/all-articles/qikify-vs-kaching-bundles); [Craftshift guide](https://craftshift.com/kaching-bundles-shopify-complete-guide-2026/); [Skailama](https://www.skailama.com/blog/kaching-bundle-alternatives) (attribution of each figure to each page is approximate; none is the vendor listing). Official listing: [Kaching Bundles on the App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/bundle-deals) (not opened).
- Alternatives with revenue-capped free plans are reported, e.g. Pumper Bundles "free plan is available until you generate $300 in bundle revenue", then $9.99/month — [Pumper blog](https://pumper.run/blog/kaching-alternatives-for-shopify-upsell-and-cross-sell/) (vendor's own blog).
- Shopify's design rules apply to theme code and app snippets alike: optional charges "should be turned off by default", "clearly disclosed and itemized… on the storefront, in the cart, and at checkout"; an auto-added item the buyer must opt out of is "unacceptable"; "these requirements apply to both packaged Checkout UI extensions and any downloadable code provided by the app or app partner across Storefront, Cart, and Checkout" — [shopify.dev – App Store best practices](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/best-practices); [Shopify changelog 16 Jan 2025](https://shopify.dev/changelog/posts/new-buyer-consent-requirement).

### Inferences
- For a one-product store, tick-box add-ons on the product page (guide, gift option, shipping protection) can be done with a small theme change that adds extra variant IDs to the add-to-cart form; this costs nothing monthly and avoids an app whose fee exceeds current revenue. The trade-off is maintenance and no built-in analytics.
- A "buy 2, save X%" quantity break can be done without an app using Shopify's native automatic discounts (quantity-based "Buy X get Y"/amount-off rules) plus theme markup to display the tiers. (Background knowledge of Shopify's native discount types; not sourced this session.)
- Whatever the method, each add-on must be unticked by default, show its price next to the tick-box, and appear as its own cart line. That satisfies both Shopify's rule 5.6.5 and the FTC/state rules in Q5.
- Any "compare-at"/"was" price shown for a bundle must be a genuine former price; fictitious reference prices are a separate deception risk (not researched in depth here).

### Gaps
- Kaching Bundles' current plan names, prices and any revenue/order thresholds could not be read from the App Store listing. Do not rely on the figures above.
- Did not research Shopify's free first-party "Shopify Bundles" app or other bundle apps' free tiers in detail.
- No source reviewed on how quantity-break discounts interact with post-purchase offers or with Shop Pay Installments eligibility thresholds.

## 4. Merchant-sold extended warranty / "replacement cover": Magnuson-Moss, state service-contract laws, third-party providers, lower-risk alternatives

### Takeaway
A separately priced promise to replace the product if it fails ("2-year replacement cover" for an extra fee) is, under federal definitions, a service contract rather than a warranty, and under most state service-contract statutes that separate charge is precisely what moves it out of the unregulated "warranty" category into a regulated product that needs registration/licensing and financial backing in states including California, Florida, Texas and Washington (New York is more nuanced). The same promise included in the product price at no extra charge is a warranty, exempt from those state regimes but subject to the federal Magnuson-Moss disclosure rules. For a 2-order UK dropshipper the paid plan is high-risk; a free included limited warranty (priced into the product) is the lower-risk route.

### Cited Findings
- Federal line between warranty and service contract: 16 C.F.R. § 700.11 — "The Act recognizes two types of agreements which may provide similar coverage of consumer products, the written warranty, and the service contract"; agreements regulated by a state as insurance are subject to Magnuson-Moss "only to the extent they are not regulated in a particular state as the business of insurance" — [16 CFR 700.11 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/700.11).
- A written warranty must be part of the basis of the bargain at the time of sale with no payment beyond the purchase price; a plan bought for an extra charge is a service contract. In Ware v. Best Buy (N.D. Ill. 2019) a separately purchased 5-year protection plan ($519 on a $3,119 TV) was held to be a service contract, not a warranty — [Ware v. Best Buy opinion (govinfo)](https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ilnd-1_18-cv-00886/pdf/USCOURTS-ilnd-1_18-cv-00886-0.pdf).
- Commentary summary: "A warranty is included in the sale price of the product, whereas a service contract is an additional (optional) charge" — [IRMI expert commentary](https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/warranty-service-contract-and-insurance).
- Magnuson-Moss basics for a warrantor: written warranties on consumer products must be titled "full" or "limited"; a limited warranty may limit implied warranties to the warranty's duration but a warrantor cannot disclaim them outright; warranty terms must be available for review before purchase (15 U.S.C. § 2302) — [FTC Businessperson's Guide to Federal Warranty Law](https://www.ftc.gov/node/60376) (search extract); [Foster Swift – Full or Limited](https://www.fosterswift.com/communications-Warranty-Full-or-Limited.html); [Foster Swift – Does your warranty comply](https://www.fosterswift.com/communications-Does-Your-Warranty-Comply-Federal-Law.html).
- Texas: Occupations Code ch. 1304 regulates service contract providers/administrators (Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation). The chapter exempts a "warranty", defined as an undertaking made solely by the manufacturer, importer or seller that is provided without additional consideration, is not negotiated or separated from the sale, and is incidental to the sale — [Tex. Occ. Code § 1304.004](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_1304.004).
- Washington: RCW 48.110.020 defines "warranty" as one "made solely by the manufacturer, importer, or seller of property or services without consideration"; a "service contract provider" is the obligor; "Solicitation or issuance of a service contract to a resident of Washington without registration as a service contract provider is an illegal practice" — [RCW 48.110.020 (PDF)](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/RCWPDF/RCW%20%2048%20%20TITLE/RCW%20%2048%20.110%20%20CHAPTER/RCW%20%2048%20.110%20.020.pdf); [Washington OIC service contract provider guidance](https://insurance.wa.gov/media/7062).
- California: the Bureau of Household Goods and Services (BHGS) requires registration of "persons who engage in selling, issuing, renewing or administering service contracts"; service contract seller registration fee listed at $95 per location (a second FAQ table shows $47.50; unclear which applies); "obligor" is "the entity financially and legally obligated under the terms of a service contract"; a seller "shall not issue, sell, or offer for sale a service contract unless the obligor under the service contract has complied with one of" several financial-backing options; a 2017 amendment (SB 1483) broadened covered products to "all consumer goods… used for personal, family, or household purposes" — [BHGS service contract application](https://www.bhgs.dca.ca.gov/forms_pubs/app_sc.pdf); [BHGS industry FAQ](https://www.bhgs.dca.ca.gov/licensee/bear_faqs.shtml); [BHGS flyer](https://www.bhgs.dca.ca.gov/forms_pubs/19-013_scflyer.pdf); [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 9855.2](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_9855.2); [SB 1483 text](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB1483/2017).
- Florida: ch. 634 Part III (service warranty associations) — "No person in this state may provide or offer service warranties without a subsisting license issued by the office" (Office of Insurance Regulation); annual licence continuance; sales representatives must also be licensed/appointed unless selling the association's own product — [Fla. Stat. ch. 634 Part III (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/florida/2017/title-xxxvii/chapter-634/part-iii/); [Fla. Admin. Code R. 69O-136.051](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-69O-136-051).
- New York: "a service contract provider, whether domiciled in New York State or not, must register with the Superintendent in order to do a service contract business in this State" (Insurance Law Art. 79) — [NY DFS OGC Opinion 05-05-31](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/insurance/ogco2005/rg050531.htm). However, DFS opinions since a July 2001 reconsideration say "the mere charging of a fee does not convert a warranty into a service contract" if the contract is a true warranty and the warrantor makes warranties "merely incidental to any other legitimate business"; a 2009 opinion stresses the warrantor "must have a relationship to the product or service" that minimises the risk element; a 2000 opinion had said the opposite — [DFS OGC 04-05-07](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/insurance/ogco2004/rg040507.htm); [DFS OGC 03-07-06](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/insurance/ogco2003/rg030706.htm); [DFS OGC 09-09-01](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/insurance/ogco2009/rg090901.htm); [DFS OGC 00-05-17](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/insurance/ogco2000/rg005311.htm); statute text at [N.Y. Ins. Law § 7902](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._insurance_law_section_7902) (current wording of the definition not verified; it has been amended).
- General pattern: state regulation "is by no means uniform"; registration duties "are imposed on the dealer, not the manufacturer"; some states require providers to be licensed as insurers; manufacturers generally face a lighter burden than third-party providers — [National Law Review](https://www.natlawreview.com/article/regulatory-concerns-sale-extended-warranties-to-commercial-purchasers); [Foley – Service Contracts and Extended Warranties](https://www.foley.com/?p=50684); [NCOIL overview of warranty regulatory landscape](https://ncoil.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Introduction-and-Overview-of-Warranty-Legislative-Regulatory-Landscape.pdf).
- Labels do not control: regulators and courts look at substance (who bears what risk), and a law-firm note says service-contract statutes "typically require that the contract cover defects or mechanical failures — not theft or carrier loss" — [Revision Legal](https://revisionlegal.com/internet-law/e-commerce-package-protection-may-violation-insurance-law/); see also [Perkins Coie – Risks of offering damage protection to customers (2017)](https://www.perkinscoie.com/en/news-insights/risks-of-offering-damage-protection-to-customers.html).
- Payment-processor angle: a third-party FAQ lists "extended warranties" among business categories prohibited under the Shopify Payments Terms of Service, and a tracker of Stripe's restricted-businesses list flags US extended warranties as prohibited — [shopping-cart-migration FAQ](https://www.shopping-cart-migration.com/faq/22-shopify/prohibited-businesses-ip-infringement-regulated-or-illegal-products-and-services); [ConductAtlas Stripe provision record](https://conductatlas.com/platform/stripe/stripe-restricted-businesses-list/provision/CA-P-065682/us-extended-warranties-prohibited/) (both secondary; official list at [Shopify Payments eligibility](https://help.shopify.com/manual/payments/shopify-payments/eligibility) not opened). Shopify's payment-facilitator terms bar use of the service for businesses on the acquirer's prohibited/restricted list — [Shopify Payments Facilitator Terms](https://www.shopify.com/legal/terms-payfac).
- Third-party providers:
  - Extend: says merchants pay nothing to partner and receive a share of each plan sale; its Shopify app is listed as free — [Extend docs](https://docs.extend.com/docs/common-questions-or-concerns); [Extend App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/extend-protection?locale=th). No published minimum revenue found; a Shopify community post says Extend "does not work with smaller stores" (anecdote) — [Shopify Community thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/what-is-the-best-app-for-product-extended-warranty-plans/59769) (attribution to this thread vs [this one](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-can-i-add-an-in-house-warranty-program-to-my-online-store/127108) uncertain).

A community thread titled "Beware of Clyde warranty app" exists (not read) — [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/beware-of-clyde-warranty-app/628163).
  - Mulberry: described by a comparison blog as best for larger stores needing deep integrations — [ShipAid comparison](https://www.shipaid.com/blogs/shopify-app-comparisons/extend-postpurchase-solutions-vs-mulberry-product-protection-a-detailed-comparison) (competitor blog). No eligibility thresholds found.

### Inferences
- What a separately priced "2-year replacement cover" sold by the merchant itself would trigger (inference from the statutes above; lawyer needed):
  1. Federally it is a "service contract", not a warranty (extra consideration).
  2. In Texas and Washington the warranty exemption expressly requires no additional consideration, so a paid plan falls inside the service-contract statutes (registration with TDLR / OIC, financial-responsibility requirements).
  3. In California the store would be a service-contract "seller" (BHGS registration) and, as the party promising replacement, also the "obligor" who must meet financial-backing requirements, for any consumer good.
  4. In Florida it would be "offering service warranties", which requires a licence from the Office of Insurance Regulation.
  5. In New York there is an argument, from DFS opinions, that a seller's own fee-based warranty incidental to its business stays a warranty; but the opinions conflict over time, the statute has been amended, and a dropshipper that never handles or manufactures the product is a weak fit for the "relationship to the product that minimises risk" rationale.
  6. Selling to "US customers only" means all 50 states' rules apply to whoever buys; there is no de minimis exemption identified in the sources for tiny sellers.
  7. It may also conflict with Shopify Payments' prohibited-business list (unconfirmed, secondary sources).
- Practical exposure is also commercial: the product is an AliExpress-sourced electronic wristband with no manufacturer relationship, so every 2-year failure is replaced at the merchant's own cost.
- Lower-risk alternatives, in rough order:
  - (a) Include a free written limited warranty in the product price (e.g. "1-year limited warranty: if it stops working we replace it"), raising the base price if needed. No separate charge means it is a warranty, outside state service-contract regimes; but it must comply with Magnuson-Moss: titled "Limited Warranty", clear terms (who is covered, what is covered/excluded, duration, how to claim), available on the site before purchase, and no disclaimer of implied warranties (duration can be limited to the warranty term where state law allows).
  - (b) Sell a higher-priced variant/bundle whose price includes the longer warranty. Caution: if the only difference between two variants is the warranty length, a regulator could view the price difference as separate consideration for a service contract; this is a grey area (lawyer).
  - (c) Use a licensed third-party programme (Extend/Clyde/Mulberry) where the provider is the registered obligor. Realistically unavailable at 2 orders, and Clyde requires an existing 90-day limited warranty and 14-day return policy.
  - (d) Monetise the same customer motivation differently: the paid add-on is something that is clearly a product (spare band/charger, second unit, the digital guide), not a promise of future performance.
- A satisfaction/returns guarantee ("30-day money back") is a different thing from a warranty and is generally lower-risk, but must be honoured as advertised.

### Gaps
- Not verified this session (background knowledge only): the Magnuson-Moss dollar thresholds (the "full/limited" designation applying to consumer products costing more than $10; the Disclosure and Pre-Sale Availability Rules applying above $15), and the rule that a supplier who sells its own service contract at or within 90 days of sale may not disclaim implied warranties (15 U.S.C. § 2308). The FTC guide and 16 C.F.R. Parts 701–702 should be checked directly.
- Current text of N.Y. Ins. Law § 7902's "service contract"/"warranty" definitions, Florida's exemptions (if any) for sellers' own warranties, California's financial-backing options and any small-seller exemptions were not read in full.
- No official statement found of Extend's or Mulberry's minimum merchant size or product-category rules; Mulberry's current corporate status not confirmed.
- Shopify Payments' official prohibited-business list could not be opened; whether "extended warranties" is on the current UK-account list (the merchant's Shopify Payments account is UK-based, selling to the US) is unconfirmed.
- Whether a UK sole trader can even register as a service-contract provider in these states (many require a US entity, bond or reimbursement insurance policy) was not researched; in practice this alone may make the paid plan unworkable.
- State "lemon"/implied-warranty rules (e.g. states that prohibit disclaiming implied warranties on consumer goods at all) were not researched.

## 5. FTC and state rules on upsell presentation: pre-ticked boxes/negative option, drip pricing/junk fees, fake reviews, fake urgency, health-claim substantiation

### Takeaway
None of the planned upsells is prohibited in itself; the legal risk lies in presentation. As of Nov 2026 the FTC "click-to-cancel" rule is vacated (8th Cir., 8 July 2025) and a fresh rulemaking is only at the advance-notice stage, and the federal junk-fees rule covers only live-event tickets and short-term lodging; but the FTC Act §5, ROSCA, the in-force fake-reviews rule (16 CFR 465, effective 21 Oct 2024) and a growing set of state all-in-pricing laws (CA, MA, MN, VA, CO, OR, CT) do apply to an online store. The safe pattern is: add-ons unticked, itemised and clearly optional; no handling/"service" fees beyond real shipping; only real reviews; no fake timers or scarcity; and sleep/health claims limited to what the evidence supports.

### Cited Findings
**Negative option / "click-to-cancel" (status)**
- The Eighth Circuit vacated the FTC's amended Negative Option Rule ("click-to-cancel") in its entirety on 8 July 2025, days before its 14 July 2025 compliance date, because the FTC had not prepared the preliminary regulatory analysis required by FTC Act §22; the decision was procedural, not on the merits — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule); [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/publication/eighth-circuit-cancels-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule/); [Jones Day, May 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/it/insights/2026/05/ftc-revives-clicktocancel-rule-new-risks-for-subscription-businesses).
- 2026 developments: consumer groups (CFA, AELP) petitioned in November (2025) to reopen the rulemaking; in February 2026 the FTC formally restored the Negative Option Rule to its pre-2024 text; the FTC issued an advance notice of proposed rulemaking in March 2026 seeking comment before drafting a new rule — [Gibson Dunn, Feb 2026](https://www.gibsondunn.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues.pdf); [Goodwin, Feb 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/insights/publications/2026/02/alerts-practices-ba-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-gets-new-life); [Arnold & Porter, Feb 2026](https://arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/02/ftc-and-state-ags-continue-to-scrutinize-subscription-practices); [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-click-to-cancel-vacatur-signaling-subscription-rules-are-back-in-play). (Exact ANPRM publication date and comment deadline not confirmed; the Feb-dated alerts and the "March" ANPRM date in the search summary are slightly inconsistent.) No new final rule is in force.
- ROSCA (Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act) remains in force and is the FTC's main tool; civil penalties up to $53,088 per violation; state laws such as California's amended automatic-renewal law (AB 2863) remain in force — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/it/insights/2026/05/ftc-revives-clicktocancel-rule-new-risks-for-subscription-businesses); [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102ksm0/8th-circuit-vacates-ftcs-negative-option-rulebut-compliance-traps-remain).

**Pre-ticked boxes**
- The FTC's position, as cited in 2026 litigation, is that "a pre-checked box does not amount to affirmative consent" — [Mertson v. Route App complaint (N.D.N.Y. No. 3:26-cv-01172, filed 3 June 2026)](https://www.classaction.org/media/mertson-et-al-v-route-app-inc-complaint.pdf) (a plaintiff's characterisation of FTC statements, not an FTC document).
- Shopify independently bans pre-selected optional charges (App Store requirement 5.6.5, in force since 18 Feb 2025) — [shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/app-store-requirements).
- FTC staff report "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" (Sept 2022) identifies design practices that trick consumers into consent or purchases as an enforcement priority, while noting not all dark patterns are illegal per se — [FTC report page](https://www.ftc.gov/node/79647); [Venable summary](https://www.venable.com/insights/blogs/2022/09/the-ftc-brings-more-light-to-dark-patterns-in-new); [Hunton summary](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-releases-report-on-dark-patterns).

**Drip pricing / junk fees**
- Federal: the FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees took effect 12 May 2025 and applies only to live-event tickets and short-term lodging; it regulates how total price is displayed, not which fees may be charged — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/de/pubs/2025/01/ftc-issues-final-junk-fees-rule-to-crackdown-on-aggressive-pricing-practices); [BakerHostetler on FTC v. StubHub](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/ftc-v-stubhub-10-million-settlement-signals-aggressive-enforcement-of-new-fees-rule/) (StubHub $10 million settlement announced 9 April; year appears to be 2026 but was not confirmed). It does not cover a physical-goods store, but drip pricing remains challengeable as deception under FTC Act §5.
- States: "the unifying principle of these laws is that all mandatory fees, excepting taxes and shipping costs, must be included in the price first displayed to the consumer"; California, Minnesota and Massachusetts ban drip pricing generally; Colorado HB25-1090 effective 1 Jan 2026 (shipping that varies by location/rate/order size may sit outside the advertised price); Oregon SB 430 targets online sellers, effective 1 Jan 2026; Connecticut effective 1 July 2026; Virginia also has a law — [BakerHostetler 2025 year in review](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/2025-year-in-review-junk-fee-laws/); [Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/taking-out-the-trash-new-junk-fee-laws-impact-pricing-practices/); [Arnall Golden Gregory on Oregon](https://www.agg.com/news-insights/publications/oregon-joins-the-ranks-of-states-enacting-pricing-transparency-laws/); [Troutman, April 2026](https://www.troutman.com/insights/state-attorneys-general-and-continued-enforcement-against-junk-fees-in-2026/?pdf=display); [Kelley Drye summer 2026 roundup](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/junk-fees-summer-2026-roundup-part-2-state-ag-enforcement-on-fee-practices). (Attribution of each detail to each firm is approximate.)
- California SB 478 (effective 1 July 2024): the advertised price must include all mandatory fees; may exclude government taxes and "postage or carriage charges that will be reasonably and actually incurred to ship the physical good to the consumer"; the AG treats a "handling" charge as mandatory, so it must be in the price; the AG FAQ is guidance without force of law — [California AG SB 478 page](https://www.oag.ca.gov/hiddenfees); [AG FAQ (PDF)](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/SB%20478%20FAQ%20%28B%29.pdf); [Fenwick](https://fenwick.com/insights/publications/californias-hidden-fee-ban-faqs-and-guidance); [Holland & Knight](https://hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/06/california-law-bans-hidden-fees-for-goods-and-services-starting-july).
- Massachusetts 940 CMR 38.00 (effective 2 Sept 2025): applies to offers "targeted to or result[ing] in a sale in Massachusetts", including out-of-state sellers; where fees are optional or waivable the seller must clearly disclose that they are optional and how to avoid them; at the final price presentation there must be a clear and conspicuous total including shipping and government charges — [940 CMR 38.00 text](https://www.mass.gov/doc/junk-fee-regulations-940-cmr-3800-0/download); [Mass. AG guidance](https://www.mass.gov/doc/guidance-on-junk-fee-regulations-940-cmr-3800/download); [Hinshaw](https://www.hinshawlaw.com/en/insights/in-the-news/massachusetts-consumer-protection-regulations-junk-fees-deceptive-pricing).
- A consumer-law explainer on shipping-protection suits: such fees "aren't illegal, but the way they are charged can be"; pre-checked boxes or failing to disclose that a fee is optional "may violate the FTC Act and state-specific 'junk fee' laws" — [AllAboutLawyer](https://allaboutlawyer.com/shipping-protection-fee-investigation-unfair-charges-and-your-rights-2026/) (secondary, non-law-firm).

**Fake reviews and testimonials**
- The FTC Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 C.F.R. Part 465) took effect 21 October 2024 — [WilmerHale alert](https://wilmerhale.com/-/media/files/shared_content/editorial/publications/wh_publications/client_alert_pdfs/20241009-ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-reviews.pdf); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ftc-finalizes-rule-on-fake-reviews).
- It prohibits fake or false reviews and testimonials (including AI-generated ones and ones by people with no real experience of the product), buying or offering compensation for reviews conditioned on positive or negative sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews, and related practices; civil penalties were up to $51,744 per violation at launch and are cited as $53,088 per violation in 2026, available against knowing violators — [Crowell](https://crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/final-rule-announced-the-ftc-strengthens-its-enforcement-capacity-against-deceptive-reviews-and-testimonials); [KJK, Aug 2026](https://kjk.com/2026/08/06/ftc-consumer-review-rule-enforcement-what-businesses-need-to-know/); [FTC 2025 warning-letter template](https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2025-Fake-Review-Warning-Template.pdf).
- Enforcement posture: FTC sent warning letters to ten companies in late 2025 and commentators describe stepped-up enforcement in 2026 — [Inside Privacy (Covington)](https://insideprivacy.com/united-states/federal-trade-commission/ftc-issues-warning-letters-for-violations-of-consumer-reviews-rule/); [Benesch via JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-3594404/).

**Fake urgency / scarcity**
- The FTC dark-patterns report's examples include "a baseless countdown timer that pressures users to buy immediately, but the clock just goes away or resets when it times out", classed among design elements that induce false beliefs — [FTC report page](https://www.ftc.gov/node/79647); [Frankfurt Kurnit summary](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102hx82/ftc-releases-staff-report-on-dark-patterns-warning-marketers-that-these-traps).
- Shopify bans countdown timers in checkout extensions (5.6.6) and requires limited-time post-purchase offer details to be clearly disclosed — [shopify.dev requirements](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/app-store-requirements); [shopify.dev best practices](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/best-practices).

**Health claims (relevant to a paid "sleep guide" and to the wristband)**
- FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022) replaced the 1998 dietary-supplement guide and covers health-related products broadly, including health apps, equipment/devices and diagnostic tests; it is staff guidance, not itself law — [FTC guidance PDF](https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Health-Products-Compliance-Guidance.pdf); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2023/01/ftc-issues-new-guidance-on-health-related-claims-to-replace-the-dietary-supplements-advertising-guide); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/after-almost-25-years-ftc-issues-new-health-products-compliance-guidance/).
- Standard: objective health claims need "competent and reliable scientific evidence"; the FTC treats randomised, controlled human trials as the normal benchmark, expects control group/randomisation/blinding, sets no fixed number of studies but values replication; animal, in-vitro and anecdotal evidence generally do not suffice alone — [Manatt](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/icymi-a-new-year-and-new-guidance-from-ftc-on); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/after-almost-25-years-ftc-issues-new-health-products-compliance-guidance/).
- Hedging words such as "may help" do not protect a claim (consumers may read them as product attributes); where evidence is limited the ad must clearly say so; advertisers cannot use testimonials or expert endorsements to make claims they could not substantiate directly — same sources (law-firm summaries of the guidance; the FTC PDF itself was not opened).

### Inferences
- Tick-box add-ons (guide, shipping protection, gift option): lawful if unticked by default, priced next to the box, itemised in cart/checkout and described accurately. The store's existing $2.99 shipping protection, unticked by default, already matches this. One-off add-ons are not "negative option" features; ROSCA/negative-option rules only bite if something recurs or is charged by silence, which none of the planned upsells does. Do not turn the guide into a free-trial-then-billed product without legal review.
- All-in pricing: the $39.99 price shown must be the price the customer can actually pay for the product. Optional add-ons need not be in the headline price, but there must be no mandatory "handling", "processing" or "service" fee added at checkout (California's AG treats handling as mandatory). Shipping charged at actual/reasonable cost can stay separate in the states reviewed; Massachusetts additionally wants optional fees labelled optional with how to avoid them, and a clear final total before purchase (Shopify checkout provides the total).
- Reviews: importing AliExpress/supplier reviews and presenting them as reviews from this store's customers, writing reviews oneself, AI-generating reviews, or showing invented "X people bought this" counters would fall within conduct the FTC rule targets (fake reviews / reviews misrepresenting the reviewer's experience / fake social proof). With 2 orders, the store should show few or no reviews rather than borrowed ones. Review-for-discount offers must not be conditioned on a positive rating and should be disclosed. (Inference from rule summaries; the rule text was not read.)
- Urgency: a "sale ends in 10:00" timer that resets on refresh, "only 3 left" when stock is AliExpress-unlimited, or a post-purchase "this offer expires" banner when the same discount is always available are deceptive-practice risks under FTC Act §5 and state UDAP laws. A genuinely one-time post-purchase price (not obtainable elsewhere on the site) can truthfully be described as available only on that page.
- Sleep guide, acceptable vs risky statements (inference applying the FTC guidance; lawyer for edge cases):
  - Generally fine: describing what the guide contains ("a 20-page guide to building a wind-down routine", "audio relaxation track"), general sleep-hygiene education consistent with mainstream public-health advice, subjective puffery ("our favourite tips"), and describing how the wristband works (silent vibrating alarm that does not wake a partner).
  - Needs competent and reliable scientific evidence (usually RCT-level): "fall asleep in 10 minutes", "cures/treats insomnia", "clinically proven", "improves sleep quality by X%", "reduces anxiety", "doctor recommended", any disease claim, and the same claims made indirectly via testimonials or before/after stories. "May help you sleep better" is still an efficacy claim.
  - The content of an informational guide is expression; the FTC's concern is the advertising claims made to sell it and any objective outcome promises. Calling it a "sleep guide" is lower-risk than "insomnia treatment programme". Disease-treatment positioning could also raise FDA questions for the wristband itself (not researched).
- UK seller: the FTC Act and state UDAP laws apply to practices directed at US consumers regardless of where the seller sits; practical enforcement against a tiny foreign seller more likely comes via platform action (Shopify, Meta, payment processor) or chargebacks than via regulators, but class-action plaintiffs' firms are actively targeting checkout add-on fees (see Q6).

### Gaps
- FTC primary pages (ftc.gov) could not be opened; the fake-reviews rule's exact prohibitions (e.g. the precise treatment of reviews "repurposed" from another product/seller, and fake social-media indicators) and the Health Products Compliance Guidance examples are reported from law-firm summaries. The statute's "mandatory" language suggests genuinely optional add-ons are outside it, but read the FAQ directly.

- No FTC source specific to sleep products or to paid informational guides was found; the acceptable/risky list above is an application of general principles.

## 6. Merchant-sold shipping protection: insurance characterisation and how Route, Navidium and Seel structure it

### Takeaway
Shipping protection is the upsell with the most live legal heat in 2025-26: insurance regulators (via the NAIC and several state departments) have said protection sold without a licensed insurer/producer may be unlicensed insurance, and plaintiffs' firms are filing junk-fee class actions over pre-selected protection fees. Structures differ: Seel and Route are insurance-backed third-party programmes; Navidium is a tool for the merchant to run its own self-funded programme and keep the fee. An unticked, clearly optional, accurately described merchant-funded fee avoids the junk-fee/pre-selection problem but not the unresolved "is this insurance?" question.

### Cited Findings
- Regulators: the NAIC consumer alert says unlicensed entities "are not legally allowed to sell insurance products" and may label them warranties, guarantees or protection plans; Utah's Insurance Department (20 Mar 2024) warned that "fraudulent shipping protection is worse than no protection at all"; Oklahoma's department flagged unlicensed "package protection" sellers (Nov 2023) — [NAIC consumer alert](https://content.naic.org/index.php/article/consumer-alert-beware-package-protection-insurance-scams); [Utah Insurance Department release](https://insurance.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/PR-BewareOfFakeShippingInsurance-3-20-2024.pdf); [Oklahoma Insurance Department release](https://oid.ok.gov/release_112823).
- An NAIC committee presentation describes three models — licensed, policy-backed programmes; unlicensed third-party apps that assume risk; and merchant "DIY" self-insurance — and says the latter two fail to meet state insurance rules — [NAIC D Committee presentation on package protection](https://content.naic.org:443/sites/default/files/national_meeting/D%20Cmte%20Presentation%20-%20Package%20Protection%20.pdf). (The presentation's authorship was not confirmed; industry participants with licensed products, such as Route, have promoted this framing.)
- Route's own guide argues shipping insurance is "clearly an insurance product subject to state insurance regulation", that "no state entirely exempts shipping insurance from regulation", and that merchant-side apps assume risk "without the benefit of a licensed insurance producer, actual insurance policy, insurer or licensed adjusters" — [Route – Guide to Package Protection Offerings](https://go.route.com/hubfs/Route%20-%20Guide%20to%20Package%20Protection%20Offerings.pdf) (self-interested advocacy).
- Law-firm analysis: many package-protection products "may run afoul of state insurance law"; "at least a dozen states" have regulators with an expansive view of the "business of insurance"; one option is to structure as a service contract, but those statutes typically cover defects/mechanical failure, not theft or carrier loss — [Revision Legal](https://revisionlegal.com/internet-law/e-commerce-package-protection-may-violation-insurance-law/). Older analysis of "damage protection" plans discusses California's "principal object and purpose" test, under which a storage company's protection plan was held not to be insurance by the Court of Appeal (Heckart; Supreme Court review then pending) — [Perkins Coie, 2017](https://www.perkinscoie.com/en/news-insights/risks-of-offering-damage-protection-to-customers.html).
- Litigation: Wolf-Bond v. TA3 and Route (filed March 2025) alleges hidden shipping-protection charges at checkout; Periera v. International Surf Ventures (2025) targets a retailer's "Route Package Protection" fee; Mertson v. Route App (N.D.N.Y. No. 3:26-cv-01172, filed 3 June 2026) alleges Route's widget adds a hidden pre-selected toggle and cites Route's statement that 98% of merchants leave the box pre-checked — [ClassAction.org on TA3](https://www.classaction.org/news/ta3-customer-files-class-action-lawsuit-over-allegedly-hidden-route-shipping-protection-fee); [Periera complaint (TINA)](https://truthinadvertising.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Periera-v-International-Surf-Ventures-complaint.pdf); [Mertson complaint](https://www.classaction.org/media/mertson-et-al-v-route-app-inc-complaint.pdf); [ClassAction.org on Route](https://www.classaction.org/news/class-action-lawsuit-claims-route-app-enables-unlawful-junk-fees). These are allegations in proposed class actions; no rulings were found.
- Seel: its terms say "insurance benefits are provided by Seel Insurance Inc. or Arch Specialty Insurance Company" and policies are "offered and administered by Seel Insurance Services, Inc.", covering items lost, stolen or damaged in transit — [Seel shipping protection terms](https://www.seel.com/terms/learn-more-about-seel-shipping-protection); [Seel policy summary](https://seel.com/package-protection-policy-summary).
- Navidium: describes itself as a self-service platform for creating "your own shipping protection program"; merchants "keep 100% of the collected fees" and route premiums to themselves rather than a third-party insurer — [Navidium listing mirror (Open.Store)](https://open.store/shopify-apps/navidium-shipping-protection); [Navidium on BigCommerce](https://www.bigcommerce.com/apps/navidium-shipping-protection/).
- Shopify platform rule: since 18 Feb 2025 apps cannot auto-add or pre-select optional paid items; several protection apps removed "accept by default" features citing Shopify's requirements; Shopify's own example of an acceptable design is two clearly labelled checkout buttons, one with the itemised protection fee and one without — [Shopify changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/posts/new-buyer-consent-requirement); [shopify.dev best practices](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/best-practices); [UpCart doc on removed feature](https://aftersell.mintlify.app/upcart/add_ons_shipping_protection); [Lucent Innovation note](https://lucentinnovation.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/16000212698-why-auto-opt-in-shipping-protection-was-removed).

### Inferences
- The store's current setup ($2.99, unticked by default) is compliant with Shopify's consent rule and avoids the specific allegation (pre-selected, hidden fee) driving the class actions. It should also be itemised as its own cart line and labelled optional.
- If the $2.99 is collected and kept by the merchant with no insurer behind it, it is the "merchant DIY" model that the NAIC materials and Route characterise as non-compliant with insurance law. No court ruling or formal state rule settling this was found, so the status is genuinely uncertain (lawyer). The risk is reduced, not eliminated, by: not calling it "insurance"; describing it as the store's own enhanced reship/refund service rather than indemnity; and not denying help to customers who decline it for losses the store is in any case responsible for.
- A related fairness point: a seller is generally responsible for getting goods to the buyer (card-network chargeback rules and "item not received" disputes apply regardless of whether protection was bought). Charging for "protection" against non-delivery that the store must remedy anyway could itself be attacked as a deceptive or valueless fee. This argument appears in the class-action framing but is my inference, not a cited holding.
- Third-party insured programmes (Seel, Route) move the insurance-licensing question to the provider but historically defaulted to pre-selection and take the fee themselves; with any provider the merchant should confirm the toggle is off by default.
- Lowest-risk alternative: include free replacement of lost/damaged-in-transit orders in the standard shipping policy and drop the paid add-on, or keep it clearly framed as optional priority handling/reship service.

### Gaps
- No court decision or state regulation directly resolving whether merchant-funded shipping protection is insurance was found; regulator statements are consumer alerts, not rules.
- The current status/outcome of the Route-related class actions (motions to dismiss, settlements) was not found.
- Seel's, Route's and Navidium's current merchant pricing and minimum-volume requirements were not researched.
- Which app/provider the store's existing $2.99 protection uses was not provided, so its exact structure could not be assessed.

## 7. Shopify policies (AUP, Shopify Payments terms) and Meta advertising policy points

### Takeaway
Shopify's enforceable rules most relevant here are: no deceptive practices (AUP), no auto-added or pre-selected paid add-ons and no checkout countdown timers (App Store requirements, which Shopify applies to storefront, cart and checkout code), and the Shopify Payments prohibited-business list, which secondary sources say includes "extended warranties". On Meta, the main traps for a sleep product are the Personal Attributes rule (no "Do you have insomnia?" style copy implying the viewer's health condition), unsubstantiated health claims and landing-page claims; Meta's Health and Wellness standards were reportedly rewritten on 22 July 2026 and should be read directly.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify AUP: forum posts quoting the AUP cite its fraud section ("scamming customers, using deceptive practices, or engaging in other fraudulent behavior") and a prohibition on "deceptive commercial practices or any other illegal or deceptive activities"; Shopify may remove products or suspend stores "in our sole discretion", and Trust & Safety may not give detailed reasons — [Shopify Community thread on AUP block](https://community.shopify.com/c/shopify-discussions/why-did-shopify-block-my-store-due-to-aup-policy/td-p/2094981); [Shopify Community thread on AUP product removal](https://community.shopify.com/c/shopify-discussions/product-s-in-your-store-have-been-identified-as-violating/td-p/799245) (second-hand quotations; the AUP page itself was not opened).
- Shopify buyer-consent and checkout rules (official): no automatically added or pre-selected optional charges; explicit consent before anything that changes the order total; no countdown timers in checkout; checkout/post-purchase offers must show the same name, image and price as the store; max three consecutive post-purchase requests — [shopify.dev – App Store requirements](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/app-store-requirements); [shopify.dev – best practices](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/best-practices); [changelog 16 Jan 2025](https://shopify.dev/changelog/posts/new-buyer-consent-requirement).
- Shopify Payments: merchants may not use the service for businesses on the acquirer's prohibited or restricted lists or barred by card-network rules — [Shopify Payments Facilitator Terms](https://www.shopify.com/legal/terms-payfac). Shopify's eligibility page lists high-level prohibited product/service categories — [Shopify Payments eligibility](https://help.shopify.com/manual/payments/shopify-payments/eligibility) (not opened). Secondary sources list "extended warranties" among prohibited categories — [shopping-cart-migration FAQ](https://www.shopping-cart-migration.com/faq/22-shopify/prohibited-businesses-ip-infringement-regulated-or-illegal-products-and-services); [eCommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/what-you-need-to-know-about-shopifys-prohibited-items/).
- Digital goods: Shopify actively supports selling digital products (first-party Digital Downloads app; dedicated digital-products page) — [Shopify digital products](https://www.shopify.com/digital-products). No Shopify restriction on a PDF/audio guide was found in the sources reviewed.
- Meta – Personal Attributes: "ads must not assert or imply personal attributes" of the audience; third-party guides list physical or mental health and medical conditions among covered attributes and give "Do you have diabetes?" as a flagged pattern; a 2026 guide says enforcement now reaches indirect implications and suggests, for sleep products, replacing a symptom hook with a feature-focused claim — [Meta Transparency Center – Personal Attributes](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/personal-attributes); [AuditSocials 2026 guide](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-ad-misleading-claims-personal-attributes-prohibited-content-policy-2026) (third-party).
- Meta – Health and Wellness: third-party guides say the standard bars ads that create "negative self-perception" to promote health products and restricts before/after imagery (at least for weight-loss and some cosmetic categories); one tracker reports Meta rewrote its Health and Wellness standards on 22 July 2026, moving from category-based rejection towards claim-by-claim review and removing "Weight Loss and Cosmetic Products and Procedures" as a separate category — [Meta Transparency Center – Health and Wellness](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness); [Accelerated Digital Media 2026 guide](https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/guide-to-social-media-health-ad-restrictions-2026/); [ConductAtlas change record, 23 July 2026](https://conductatlas.com/change/2026-07-23-meta-ads-meta-advertising-policies-3936/) (secondary sources disagree on structure; verify on Meta's live page).
- Meta – landing pages: several guides say a compliant ad can still be rejected if its landing page makes non-compliant claims — [OuterBox](https://www.outerboxdesign.com/articles/digital-marketing/how-health-and-wellness-brands-can-keep-their-ads-compliant-on-meta-and-other-social-media-platforms/); [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/meta-ads-policy) (third-party).
- Measurement note: third-party pixels receive only the initial-purchase event and value when a post-purchase upsell is accepted — [shopify.dev – product offers](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/checkout/product-offers).

### Inferences
- Selling a PDF/audio sleep guide is squarely within Shopify's supported use; the only platform exposure is what is claimed about it (deceptive-practice language in the AUP) and how it is added to the cart (consent rule).
- A merchant-run paid "extended warranty/replacement cover" is the one planned upsell that may breach Shopify Payments terms as well as state law. Because the merchant's Shopify Payments account is UK-based, the UK prohibited list applies to the account; it could not be checked here. A free included warranty is not a separately sold financial product and is not within that category on any reading of the secondary sources.
- For Meta ads: write to the product, not the person. "A silent vibrating alarm that wakes you without waking your partner" is feature-led; "Struggling with insomnia?", "Tired of feeling exhausted every morning?" or "You're not sleeping enough" imply the viewer's health state. If the guide is mentioned in ads or on the landing page, the same FTC substantiation limits (Q5) effectively define what Meta will tolerate, since landing pages are reviewed too.
- Meta's ROAS for campaigns will understate order value where post-purchase upsells are accepted, because the pixel only reports the initial purchase; use Shopify's own order data to judge upsell performance.
- Shopify's enforcement is discretionary and often unexplained; for a new low-volume dropshipping store already subject to higher scrutiny (payout holds, chargebacks), fake urgency widgets, borrowed reviews and unsupported health claims raise account risk well before any regulator would be involved.

### Gaps
- Shopify's AUP and the Shopify Payments prohibited-business list (UK and US versions) could not be opened; the "extended warranties" entry is confirmed only by secondary sources and by analogy with Stripe's list.
- Meta's current Health and Wellness and Personal Attributes policy texts were not read in full; the July 2026 rewrite is reported by one tracker. No source found on whether sleep aids/devices are treated as a restricted health category, or on Meta's health-related data-sharing (pixel event) restrictions for a consumer sleep gadget.
- Shop Pay Installments terms regarding digital goods or warranties in the cart were not researched.
- Gift options (gift wrap/gift message as a paid add-on) were not separately researched; as an optional paid add-on they fall under the same consent/itemisation rules in Q2, Q3 and Q5, and a paid "gift wrap" must actually be deliverable through the AliExpress/DSers supply chain (unverified).
