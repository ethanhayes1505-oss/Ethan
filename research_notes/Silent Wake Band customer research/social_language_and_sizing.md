## Q1. What do people actually say on X, TikTok, Threads, YouTube and Instagram about partners' alarms, snoozing, oversleeping, silent/vibrating alarms, sleep divorce and phone-free bedrooms? (Verbatim bank)

### Takeaway
The strongest repeatable language is **resentment at being woken by someone else's alarm while they sleep on**. Threads has the best examples: "a level of disrespect I can't explain", and "wake up 27 times in eight-minute intervals to glare at you". The second strongest is **heavy-sleeper desperation**: "I set 10 alarms and somehow sleep through all of them". TikTok is full of competing wrist-vibration-alarm sellers (Onyx/HiStrips, FitSleeps), already pitched to "deep sleepers, students, nurses". The category is validated, but the creative space is crowded. "Sleep divorce" is a well-covered TikTok/media trend, but most of the viral numbers date from 2023–24.

### Inferences
- **The most potent hook copy is written in the voice of the woken partner.** Phrases to mirror: "someone else's alarm", "while they stay asleep", "the warm-up alarm isn't for me", "27 times in eight-minute intervals", "on the one day we don't have to be up". On the X/Threads examples found, the woken partner is more often a woman complaining about a man's early or snoozed alarm. That matches AASM's finding that men are twice as likely to already use a silent alarm (see Q2). Likely ad structure: the woman is the emotional audience or gifter, and the man is the wearer.
- **The "gift for him from her" dynamic shows up organically** (the "My wife bought me a watch that shocks me" post). That supports Christmas and Valentine's gifting creative.
- Competitors already own "deep sleepers, students, nurses" on TikTok. SleepLabs' "Let them sleep" couples framing is less crowded than the heavy-sleeper framing.
- Objections to pre-empt: "vibration isn't strong enough for heavy sleepers", battery death, and comparisons with Apple Watch/Fitbit, which already do silent alarms.

## Q2. Hard sizing numbers

### Takeaway
The single most useful number is from AASM's 2025 survey: **15% of US adults already use a silent alarm to accommodate a bed partner**, with men at 20% and women at 10% (2024 figure: 11%). That is direct, current proof of the behaviour the band serves. Around it: about a third of US adults practise some form of "sleep divorce" (AASM 2023: 35%; 2025: 31%); **~16% of US wage and salary workers (~23.7M) work non-daytime shifts** (BLS 2024–25); and snoozing is the majority behaviour (snooze pressed on 56% of nights in a 2025 study of 21K sleep-app users).

### Inferences
- **Silent-alarm users:** 15% of US adults. Applying that to an assumed ~260M US adults (my assumption, not sourced in this research) gives roughly **35–40M** people who already use a silent alarm for a partner. Most likely use a phone on vibrate or a smartwatch. This is the warmest "switch" audience, with men over-indexed.
- **Couples in a sleep conflict:** roughly 31–35% of adults. These are people already sleeping apart. The band's pitch to them is "move back in", or "a fix short of sleep divorce" for couples not there yet. Millennials (43% in 2023) are the core age band, which maps well to Facebook/Instagram demographics.
- **Shift workers:** 16.4% × 144.3M ≈ **23.7M** non-daytime wage and salary workers (my calculation from the BLS snippet). Nurses (3.28M) and truckers (2.07M) are well-defined, highly motivated niches. Truckers in sleeper cabs with a co-driver are a plausible micro-niche (unverified).
- **Habitual snoozers:** ~25% of US adults (YouGov "often" + "always"). Snoozing multiplies the partner disruption, but snoozers themselves are less motivated to buy a "let them sleep" device than their partners are.
- **Phone in the bedroom (87%):** huge but low-intent. Phone-free-bedroom is a secondary, aspirational message, not a primary segment.
- **ADHD adults (15.5M) and hearing loss (37.5M):** large, but motivation is mixed. Hearing loss strongly favours vibration, though the "deaf alarm" category is established (bed shakers). ADHD links to heavy sleeping and sound aversion, but this is not quantified here.
- **New parents (3.6M births per year):** strongly motivated to protect a sleeping baby or partner, but small and time-limited.
- **Dorms (2.8M):** a roommate version of "let them sleep", with an August back-to-school spike. Younger users skew to TikTok more than Meta.

## Q4. Seasonal hooks

### Takeaway
The best-supported seasonal hooks are **daylight-saving transitions**, since AASM documents widespread post-change tiredness, and **New Year sleep/health resolutions**. **Christmas gifting for a partner** is supported only indirectly (organic "my wife bought me…" posts, plus the gender gap in silent-alarm use). **Back-to-school/college** is plausible given dorm counts and competitor targeting of "students", but I found no direct data.

### Cited Findings
- AASM: **55% of US adults** felt extremely or somewhat tired after the spring DST transition. When DST ends (fall), **41% plan to "fall back" to sleep**, i.e. use the extra hour to sleep — [AASM spring](https://aasm.org/survey-results-us-adults-feel-tired-spring-daylight-saving-time-transition/); [AASM fall](https://aasm.org/when-daylight-saving-time-ends-people-plan-to-sleep/) [snippet; ~2019 survey data, older]
- AASM advocates permanent standard time, so DST is an annual news hook — [AASM position](https://aasm.org/new-position-statement-supports-permanent-standard-time/) [snippet]
- Sleep Foundation, Nov 2023 survey (n=1,000 US): 63% had tried making resolutions and **54% hoped better sleep habits would improve their health next year**; keeping a regular bedtime was the top approach (42%) — [Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-news/new-years-resolutions-that-you-can-keep-for-better-sleep) [snippet]
- YouGov Dec 2025 (n=1,104 US): 31% planned a 2026 resolution; "exercising more" was most common (25%) — [YouGov](https://today.yougov.com/society/articles/53789-americans-new-years-resolutions-2026-poll) [snippet]
- Snoozing peaks on weekdays, highest on Wednesdays (Robbins et al. 2025) — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/05/250519131537.htm) [snippet]. Possibly useful for day-parting, but this is behavioural, not seasonal.
- Partner-as-gift-buyer evidence: "My wife bought me a watch that shocks me like a dog cos I sleep through all my alarms…" — [X @Strippin](https://x.com/Strippin/status/1765072517407121413) [snippet]
- Sleep divorce Google interest: a Marie Claire piece cites a 478% weekly spike (undated); Google Trends interest rose from 17 to 33 between 2021 and 2024 per Daily News SA — [Marie Claire UK](https://marieclaire.co.uk/opinion/sleep-divorce-tiktok-wellness-trends-burnout); [Daily News SA](https://dailynews.co.za/lifestyle/love-sex/relationships/2024-11-28-why-couples-are-embracing-sleep-divorce-for-better-relationships) [snippet; non-US outlets]

### Inferences
- **Now (early November 2026)** sits just after the US fall-back on Nov 1, 2026 (the first Sunday in November, by the standard US rule). Early-morning darkness returns, which makes the "early riser, sleeping partner" scenario more vivid. Run Christmas creative from mid-November ("the gift that lets them sleep in"), New Year "5am club / early-riser resolution" creative from Dec 26 to mid-January, and DST spring-forward creative in early March (second Sunday of March). Valentine's Day is a plausible couples hook, but I found no data on it.
- Back-to-school (August) suits a roommate/dorm creative variant, though the audience skews to TikTok.

### Gaps
- No US survey found on the % who resolve to "wake up earlier".
- No seasonal Google Trends or sales data for "vibrating alarm" or "silent alarm". A direct Google Trends pull would answer this.
- No data on alarm/sleep gadgets as Christmas gifts for partners.
