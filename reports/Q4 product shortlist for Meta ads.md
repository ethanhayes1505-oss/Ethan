# Skip the Christmas rush, launch 26 December

> **Read this first: how far you can trust this report.** The researchers could not open most web pages, because the research session's network settings blocked them. Reddit could not be read at all, so nothing here is first-hand Reddit evidence. Most figures come from search-result summaries of pages, not from the pages themselves. Only two kinds of fact were seen directly: Meta Ad Library results on 23 November 2026 (page names, ad headlines, start dates and estimated ad counts, but not the videos, prices or spend) and Meta's own Help Center articles. **This shortlist is a set of leads to check, not a list of proven winners.**
>
> Every claim carries a label. **[V]** = verified: seen directly, as described above. **[U]** = unverified: taken from a search summary of a page nobody opened. **[E]** = our own estimate, sum or judgement, also unverified. Figures about your own test are yours and are marked "your figure".

Do not launch a new product for Christmas: it is too late to ship from China and this is the most expensive ad week of the year **[U/E]**. Spend the next four weeks getting supplier quotes and samples, then start ads on or just after **26 December**, when ad prices are at their lowest and people buy for themselves **[U]**. The three leads most worth testing first are a **Bluetooth sleep headband set at about $45**, a **resistance-band set sold as "stronger at any age" in a $40–$50 bundle**, and a **beginner crochet kit** (only if a supplier quote shows it is light and cheap enough) **[E]**. Backups are an electric scalp massager gift set at $49.99, a personalised necklace for Valentine's Day, a USB heated scarf and a motion-sensor light pack **[E]**. Only one product on the whole list, the resistance-band set, was confirmed as advertised for more than 30 days with many ad versions **[V]**; everything else is early or unproven. The hardest finding is about money, not products: the typical Meta cost per sale is reported at about $39 **[U]**, which sits inside your break-even range of $28–$45 (your figure), so a single $40 item is a coin-toss even when things go normally. Sell $45–$60 bundles of broad, everyday, non-health products, and expect January to make ads perhaps 15–25% cheaper than a normal month, not three times cheaper **[E]**. Skip hand warmers, star projectors, book nook kits, lint removers, posture and pain products, kids' cameras and anything Christmas-only.

## A $40 product needs ad prices near $15–$20 per thousand views; your test paid $63

Your test spent £224 for 2 sales, which is £112 (about $144) per sale against a break-even of £22–£35 ($28–$45), at a CPM (the price of showing your ad 1,000 times) of about £49, or $63 (your figures). The sum that links these is simple: cost per sale = CPM × 10 ÷ (click rate % × purchase rate %) **[E]**. With a decent 1.5% click rate and a 3% purchase rate, a $15 CPM gives a cost per sale of $33, a $25 CPM gives $56, and a $63 CPM gives $140 **[E]**. So a $40 product only works when the CPM is roughly $20 or less. Above about $35 no ad or page can save it **[E]**.

The benchmarks say that target is reachable but tight. The largest dataset found, Triple Whale's figures for a reported 40,000+ online brands from August 2025 to July 2026, puts the **median Meta CPM at $15.06 and the median cost per purchase at $38.99** **[U]** ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)). That means the typical established brand pays about $39 for a sale, which is the top half of your break-even range. A new store with a $39.99 single item has to beat the typical brand, not match it **[E]**. This is why every lead below is priced as a bundle or set at $45 or more.

Which product you pick changes the ad price. In the same dataset, health and wellness is the dearest group to reach at a $21.80 CPM, beauty is $19.53, clothing and accessories about $13–$14 and baby $11.50 **[U]** ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)). Median cost per purchase is lowest for "lifestyle and boutique" ($32.04) and clothing and accessories ($37.41), then pets ($39.23), health and wellness ($42.30), home and garden ($48.61) and electronics ($51.06) **[U]**. Age matters too: one agency reports a $35.14 CPM for people aged 65 and over, far above younger groups, though it says a sale from them costs about half as much **[U]** ([Noble Growth](https://noblegrowth.co/tools/meta-ads-benchmarks/)). Your result, with both buyers women aged 65+ and a $63 CPM, fits that pattern **[E]**. No reliable US table of CPM by age and sex exists, so this rests on one agency's numbers **[E]**.

Meta's own help pages explain the rest. Meta sets its bid for each view as "bid per estimated action × estimated action rate" **[V]** ([Meta: bid cap](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/272503946776144)), so when you ask for purchases it pays a lot for the few people it expects to buy. An ad set stays "learning limited" if it cannot get about 50 results a week, and Meta names low budget and small audience as causes **[V]** ([Meta: learning limited](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981)). At $45 a day you can afford about 8 sales a week at best, so results will be noisy for weeks and that is normal, not a fault **[E]**. Meta can also class a website that sells "health-related products and services" as a health and wellness data source and then limit or block purchase tracking **[V]** ([Meta: data source categories](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1402913027039332); [Meta: restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698)), and it bans ads that create "negative self-perception" or make sensational health claims **[V]** ([Meta: health and wellness policy](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2489235377779939)). Together these point to one rule for choosing: **a broad, everyday product for many ages, with no health condition in the pitch** **[E]**.

Costs set the second rule. Tracked shipping for one parcel from China is about $4.90–$8 at 100–300 g and $7–$12 at 500 g **[U]** ([Fulfido](https://fulfido.com/shipping/china-to-us); [OneShipPros](https://oneshippros.com/how-to-ship-from-china-to-usa-fast/); [Easync](https://easync.io/articles/cj-dropshipping-review/)). The old $800 duty-free allowance is suspended indefinitely, so every parcel now pays duty **[U]** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other)). For most Chinese goods the duty is the normal rate plus either 20 or 37.5 percentage points, charged on the product's declared value, not your selling price **[U]** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies); [ImportCostPro](https://importcostpro.com/blog/section-301-tariffs-2026-guide)). On a $4 item that is only about $0.80–$1.60 **[E]**. Shipping is the real cost, and it puts a floor of about $6–$7 under any product **[E]**. To keep landed cost under 30% of a $40–$45 price, the item must cost under about $5 from the factory and weigh under about 300 g **[E]**. Most battery gadgets land at 35–50% of price instead **[E]**. Zendrop says duty is included in the shipping price it shows; for CJdropshipping and AliExpress this could not be confirmed for 2026 **[U]** ([Zendrop](https://support.zendrop.com/en/articles/16100468-how-do-tariffs-and-customs-duties-affect-my-zendrop-orders)). Ask any supplier to confirm "duty paid" in writing, or your customer may be asked to pay at the door **[E]**. Also allow about $1.46 per $39.99 order for Shopify card fees and 5–15% of orders for refunds **[U]** ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/credit-card-processing-fees); [AliDropship](https://alidropship.com/dropshipping-wiki/returns/)).

### 1. Bluetooth sleep headband set: the best all-round fit, on thin evidence

This is a soft headband or sleep mask with flat speakers inside, for people who fall asleep to audio. A page called "monoscus.pk" has 11 active US ads, all started between 19 and 23 November, with headlines such as "Ordinary Sleep Mask vs. MONOSCUS" and "Give the gift they'll enjoy long after the holidays!" **[V]** ([Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1114290121573082); [Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1602772314489912)). In 15 ads sampled for this search, it was the only store selling the product **[V]** ([Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1147105888039240)). So it looks early, not crowded, but four days of ads prove nothing about profit **[E]**. Wider demand for sleep masks is large: the Amazon leader is estimated at about 100,000 sales a month with the top ten averaging $14.47 **[U]** ([AsinSight](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/sleep-mask)), and Oprah's 2026 list includes a silk sleep mask at $40 **[U]** ([Yahoo Shopping](https://shopping.yahoo.com/gift-ideas/articles/oprah-2026-favorite-things-list-163040484.html)), which suggests older women will pay $40 for a premium sleep item **[E]**.

Alibaba listings show $2.89–$3.20 a unit at 50 pieces and $4.45–$7.20 for one or two pieces **[U]** ([Alibaba](https://www.alibaba.com/wholesale/bluetooth-eye-sleeping-mask.html)). At an estimated 120–180 g, landed cost is $8.37–$15.67, about $12 in the middle: 30% of $39.99 or 27% of $44.99 **[E]**. Adding a plain silk-style mask to make a "sleep set" should lift the price more than the cost, but that mask was not priced **[E]**. It fits your current sleep store and can be sold all year, with a natural "better sleep" pitch in January **[E]**. The risks are cheap alternatives (plain sleep masks at $14–$17 on Amazon, and an older round-up of Reddit favourites that names a headphone sleep mask at about $20) **[U]** ([Mic](https://www.mic.com/impact/45-cheap-products-that-get-raved-about-the-most-on-reddit-81068714)), battery shipping rules (batteries sent by air must be at 30% charge or less and need a test document) **[U]** ([Flexport](https://www.flexport.com/blog/2025-and-2026-regulatory-changes-for-lithium-ion-and-sodium-ion-batteries/); [DHL](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-vn/ship-with-dhl/export-with-dhl/lithium-batteries-dangerous-goods-checklist)), and the health line: sell comfort and audio, never insomnia, snoring or tinnitus **[E]**.

## When to launch: prepare now, spend from 26 December

Ad prices and shipping both say the same thing. In past years Meta ad prices in Black Friday week ran about 59% above September, with Black Friday itself about 84% above **[U]** ([Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/are-meta-ads-worth-it-black-friday)). Black Friday is 27 November and Cyber Monday 30 November **[U]**. Prices stayed high into mid-December, fell in the week before Christmas, and hit their low right after it: one tracker shows the week of 29 December 2025 about 60% below the peak week, and every January week cheaper than every November week **[U]** ([GAAS, citing Gupta Media](https://www.trygaas.com/blog/january-meta-cpm-window); [Gupta Media](https://www.guptamedia.com/insights/q5-marketing-holiday-advertising)). Sources disagree on the size of the fall: another puts January only about 17% below October, and one found Meta shopping ads just 3% cheaper after Christmas **[U]** ([Biddy](https://www.biddyco.com/blog-posts/meta-ads-black-friday-cpm); [Right Side Up](https://www.rightsideup.com/blog/q4-advertising-trends)). The safe reading is that January is 15–25% cheaper than a normal month **[E]**. Meta's ad prices in the US and Canada were also up 23% on a year earlier last quarter, so this year starts from a higher base **[U]** ([Meta results via Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/405782/meta-reports-third-quarter-2026-results)). Applied to you, a $63 CPM might fall to $40–$50 in January **[E]**. That alone will not fix your current product. A different product has to do most of the work.

On shipping, China-direct parcels normally take 8–17 days and 12–22 days in the holiday rush **[U]** ([Piratify](https://piratify.io/blog-posts/shipping-times-china-to-us-dropshipping-ul4h)). That makes the last safe order date for Christmas roughly 30 November to 5 December **[E]**. A product launched today has about one week of honest "arrives by Christmas" selling, during the most expensive ad week of the year **[E]**. Stock already in a US warehouse can sell until about 14–15 December, since the USPS Ground Advantage deadline is 17 December **[U/E]** ([Doba](https://www.doba.com/blog/marketing-and-sales-growth/marketing-tips/christmas-shipping-deadlines-2026-usps-ups-and-fedex-cutoffs-for-online-sellers-39879)). But US stock costs roughly 25–33% more per unit **[U]** ([Ecommerce Times](https://ecommerce-times.com/spocket-vs-cj-dropshipping-in-2026-which-supplier-platform-wins-9/)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Finding a "winning product" matters less than fixing the shape of the offer: a $40 single item needs a better-than-typical result on Meta, while a $45–$60 bundle of a light, broad, health-free product only needs a normal one. The cost research and the ad research also pull in opposite directions, and it is worth knowing that: the items with the best margins (posture straps, neck stretchers) are the ones Meta treats hardest, and the items with the strongest ad activity (bands, scalp massagers, hand warmers) are the ones Amazon sells cheapest. The top three leads are the places where those two pressures overlap least, not places where they vanish.

Because so little could be verified, four short checks will tell you more than this report can. Open each Ad Library link and look at the actual ads and the oldest start date. Search each product on Amazon and note the price you must explain away. Get one written quote per lead with weight, shipping and "duty paid" stated. And look in Meta Events Manager (Datasets, then Settings) for a "Manage data source categories" section, which would show whether Meta has classed your store as health and wellness **[V]** ([Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/467621355878794)). Any lead that survives all four is worth a £250–£350 test after Christmas; any that fails one should be dropped without spending on it **[E]**.
