# The second band beats every add-on

The upsell worth most to SleepLabs is a second wristband, not a sleep guide. Each buyer who takes the 2-pack instead of a single band adds about $17 of profit, while a $6.99 digital guide sold as a tick-box realistically adds $0.15 to $0.70 per order. The first move therefore costs nothing: pre-select the 2-pack, show the price per band, and give the guide away as a bonus with the 2-pack and 3-pack instead of charging for it. The second move is a one-click offer of a second band at $25 straight after payment, worth roughly $0.35 to $1.00 per order, but only once order volume covers an app fee. Paid warranty cover, "priority handling", gift wrap and subscriptions are either legally risky for a UK-run dropshipper selling into the US or cannot be honestly fulfilled, and the existing $2.99 shipping protection should stay exactly as it is, unticked. Taken together the realistic gain is about **$2 to $6 per order against a $10.50 shortfall**, so upsells narrow the gap but do not close it; ad cost and conversion remain the main problem. Evidence quality is uneven: competitor prices, sleep-medicine guidance and Shopify's developer rules were read directly on 17 November 2026, whereas nearly every take-rate and order-value figure is an app vendor's claim seen only as a search summary, and with two orders the store cannot yet measure anything itself.

## A $6.99 sleep guide adds under $1 per order, so give it away

### Sleep brands give guides away or sell memberships, not $7 PDFs

The closest competitor, FitSleeps, sells the same kind of vibrating wristband from $39 and lists two digital items, "Calm Start" and "Night Reset" guided breathwork, at **$0.00**. They have no description and a "sold out" standalone page, which points to free gifts added in the cart, not products for sale ([FitSleeps catalogue](https://fitsleeps.com/products.json?limit=250)). Manta Sleep includes a "Free Napping Guide" with a stated **$18 value** beside a $159 mask bundle ([Manta Sound Calm Bundle](https://mantasleep.com/pages/sound-calm-bundle)). Larger hardware brands charge for digital content only as an ongoing membership tied to an app: Hatch+ at $5.99 a month or $59.99 a year after a 30-day trial ([Hatch](https://www.hatch.co/restore-3)), Oura at $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/membership)), and Apollo's $99 membership ([Apollo catalogue](https://apolloneuro.com/products.json?limit=250)). Supplement sellers attach free ebooks to multi-bottle packs, according to search summaries of pages such as [Yu Sleep](https://yusleepsupplements.com/) and [NooCube](https://noocube.com/pages/sleep-upgrade).

**No named sleep brand was found selling a $5 to $10 PDF as a tick-box add-on.** That is partly a limit of the research: several sites (Hostage Tape, Pavlok, Dodow, Morphee) could not be opened and no brand's cart or checkout was viewed. But FitSleeps' own paid add-ons are telling. It charges $2.95 for shipping insurance, $7.95 for a "waterproof upgrade", $8.80 for a charger, $9.95 for a case and $18 for a warranty, and gives the digital content away ([FitSleeps catalogue](https://fitsleeps.com/products.json?limit=250)). A paid guide would be different from the competition, and also unproven.

### Realistic take rates are 2 to 10 percent, not the 30 to 40 percent quoted for courses

The price itself is sensible. Practitioner guides suggest pricing a small add-on at 10 to 20 percent of the main item, and $6.99 is 17.5 percent of $39.99 ([Course Coach](https://course-coach.pages.dev/courses/create-product-suite/09-order-bumps/), search summary). The problem is the take rate, meaning the share of shoppers shown the offer who accept it. The widely quoted 30 to 40 percent figures come from checkouts for online courses and other digital products, such as a 37.8 percent average from a study of 1,847 digital businesses ([Focus Digital](https://focus-digital.co/average-upsell-conversion-rate-2025-report/)) and SamCart's 35 to 40 percent ([SamCart](https://www.samcart.com/blog/how-to-increase-average-order-value-%287-proven-ways%29)). Those buyers are already purchasing digital content, so the figures do not transfer to a PDF beside a physical gadget.

The Shopify-specific data is far lower. An upsell app's dataset of 3,199 stores puts the **median take rate at 2.4 percent**, with the top tenth of stores near 10 percent ([Candy Rack benchmarks](https://www.digismoothie.com/blog/upsell-benchmarks)). A second vendor study of 16,600 stores found pop-up offers shown after add-to-cart converted 13.3 percent of views ([Zipify study](https://www.einnews.com/pr_news/957117063/zipify-study-of-123-9m-upsell-offers-finds-pre-checkout-pop-ups-convert-best-yet-half-as-many-shopify-stores-use-them)). One course platform says plainly that "there's no published benchmark we could verify" ([Ruzuku](https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/order-bumps-for-courses)). No source measured a digital guide attached to a physical product. On these numbers a $6.99 guide earns about **$0.17 per order at the median, $0.70 at a top-tenth rate and $0.91 at the best vendor figure**, nearly all of it profit. That is 2 to 9 percent of the $10.50 gap.

### Free with the 2-pack beats a paid tick-box at this stage

No test was found comparing a paid digital add-on with the same item given free. The nearest evidence is one vendor-published test at a bedding brand, where a free gift converted slightly better (2.13 percent against 2.01 percent) but a discounted paid upsell made about 19 percent more gross profit per visitor ([Intelligems](https://www.intelligems.io/resources/blog/what-should-i-offer-at-checkout-to-improve-profit)). Both items were physical and the order value was much higher, so it is a weak guide here. Academic work adds one practical point: shoppers devalue a free gift when no price is attached to it ([Raghubir, 2005](https://www.emeraldinsight.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/10610420510592617/full/html)), and judge a free bonus against the price of the main product ([Palmeira and Srivastava, via Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2013-08-consumers-freebie-valuable-discounted-product.html)).

Three things tip the decision towards free. First, the product page already asks two questions before purchase (which bundle, and shipping protection), so a paid guide would be a third. Single-store tests show what extra asks can cost: removing a "priority processing" option above the buy button raised conversion 9 percent ([Blue Stout](https://bluestout.com/cro-wins/could-your-upsell-be-causing-confusion-try-this/)), and adding a shipping-protection widget cut first-time conversion 26 percent in another ([Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/growth/shipping-protection-conversion-rate)). At an ad cost of about £36 (roughly $47) per order, a 9 to 10 percent drop in conversion costs about $4.65 to $5.20 per order, several times the guide's best case. Second, the guide's revenue is small either way. Third, a free guide can be pointed at the lever that is large: "free sleep guide with 2 or more bands" gives a reason to choose the 2-pack, the same mechanic supplement sellers use for bigger packs.

The honest way to state a value is Manta's: a modest, specific figure. List the guide as a real standalone product at $6.99 so the "worth $6.99" line is true, and state the terms of the free offer up front, as the FTC's guide on the word "free" requires ([16 CFR 251.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-251/section-251.1), search summary). The FTC's separate rules on "value" claims were not read, so if in doubt say "free bonus" with no dollar figure. A paid tick-box remains a reasonable later test once about 100 orders exist.

### A short, band-specific plan with two audio tracks fits the price

Nothing found shows which format sells best, so this is a judgement from what brands do. FitSleeps splits its audio by moment of use, one wake-up track and one fall-asleep track ([FitSleeps catalogue](https://fitsleeps.com/products.json?limit=250)). Manta picks a single problem, napping ([Manta](https://mantasleep.com/pages/sound-calm-bundle)). Dubs Labs offers a list of 52 tips ([Dubs Labs](https://dubslabs.com/pages/sleep-tips), search summary). Guidance for low-priced digital add-ons favours companions that help the buyer use the main purchase, such as a checklist or workbook ([Teachable](https://teachable.com/blog/how-to-price-an-order-bump)), and prices short checklists at $2.99 to $5.99 with fuller ebooks from $7 ([SamCart](https://www.samcart.com/blog/how-to-price-an-ebook), search summary).

The best fit is a guide that makes the band work better, not a generic sleep ebook: a "first seven mornings" plan covering where to wear the band, a backup alarm for heavy sleepers and a fixed wake time; one page for couples with different wake times; one page for shift workers; and one or two short audio tracks for waking and winding down. Ten to twenty skimmable pages is enough, though no source gives a page-count norm. The tone should be calm. An American Academy of Sleep Medicine survey found **76 percent of US adults say they have lost sleep worrying about sleep problems** ([AASM](https://aasm.org/sleep-tracking-and-sleepmaxxing-change-bedtime-behaviors-and-keep-some-americans-awake-at-night/)), which argues for habits over scores and fear. The product listing should say exactly what is inside and how it arrives, which FitSleeps does not.

### What the guide can say safely

General sleep-habit advice from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) can be restated with attribution. Anything that promises a health outcome needs scientific evidence the store does not have: the FTC's guidance for health-related products expects "competent and reliable scientific evidence", normally controlled human trials, and says hedges like "may help" do not protect a claim ([McDermott summary of the FTC guidance](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/after-almost-25-years-ftc-issues-new-health-products-compliance-guidance/)). Writing "AASM recommends" is accurate; implying AASM endorses the guide is not.

| Topic | What to write | Status and source |
|---|---|---|
| Wake time | Get up at the same time every day, weekends included | Safe; read directly in the [AASM handout](https://aasm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/healthy-sleep-habits-AASM.pdf) |
| Sleep length | Choose a bedtime that allows at least 7 hours | Safe; [AASM handout](https://aasm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/healthy-sleep-habits-AASM.pdf) |
| Light and screens | Light and darkness are the strongest timing cues; dim lights and stop screens 30 minutes before bed | Safe; [AASM on light](https://aasm.org/embracing-the-eclipse-shedding-light-on-the-importance-of-light-for-our-sleep-and-daily-routines/) |
| Caffeine | Limit caffeine later in the day; one small study of 12 people found 400 mg taken six hours before bed cut sleep by more than an hour | Safe if described as one small study; [AASM release](https://aasm.org/late-afternoon-and-early-evening-caffeine-can-disrupt-sleep-at-night/) |
| Morning grogginess | It usually fades within about 30 to 60 minutes | Check the live page first; search summary of [NIOSH training](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/niosh/work-hour-training-for-nurses/longhours/mod7/03.html) |
| Snoozing | Research is mixed; say "set the alarm for when you need to get up" and make no health claim | Small conflicting studies, seen via [Scientific American](https://scientificamerican.com/article/is-snoozing-the-alarm-good-or-bad-for-your-health) and [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/968205) |
| Night shifts | Basic tactics such as a nap before a night shift and going straight to bed afterwards; "no one-size-fits-all solution" | Check the live [NIOSH page](https://cdc.gov/niosh/bulletin/2016/shift-work.html); the quote was read in the [AASM guideline release](https://aasm.org/new-guideline-provides-recommendations-on-managing-shift-work-disorder/) |
| Leave out | Treating or curing insomnia or any disorder, "fall asleep in 10 minutes", "more REM", "clinically proven", melatonin or drug doses | [FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Health-Products-Compliance-Guidance.pdf) |

Breathwork audio should be described as relaxation, not as a proven way to fall asleep faster; no authoritative source supporting that claim was found. The guide should also carry the AASM's own closing line: if sleep difficulties persist, talk to a health care provider.

### Delivery uses Shopify's Digital Downloads app, with one test order

Shopify's own Digital Downloads app hosts files up to 5 GB and supports a digital item in the same order as a physical one ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/digital-products)). Its help page describes per-product download limits and a button to re-send the delivery email ([Shopify Help Center](https://help.shopify.com/manual/apps/apps-by-shopify/digital-downloads), search summary). Whether the app is still free and exactly how the link is shown were not confirmed in this research. The set-up is to create the guide as its own product, untick "This is a physical product" so it adds no shipping ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/shipping-rates-for-digital-physical-products/166135)), attach the file, and set the digital item to fulfil automatically. The guide must not be linked to an AliExpress supplier in DSers.

One risk needs a live test. Merchants report that when the digital line is fulfilled instantly, the order becomes "partially fulfilled" and can confuse the system that sends the physical item to the supplier ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/flow-template-fulfill-any-digital-items-in-an-order-and-orders-with-physical-digital-products/389073)). Place one mixed order end to end before promoting the guide. US sales tax is not a current issue: the usual threshold for registering in a state is $100,000 of sales into that state ([Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus), vendor guide), though roughly 30 states tax digital downloads ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/sales-tax-digital-goods-by-state)), so it becomes a question for an adviser as volume grows.

## Moving buyers to the 2-pack is the only lever sized to the gap

### Each 10 points of orders shifted to the 2-pack adds $1.70 per order

On the store's own numbers, a buyer who moves from one band to two adds $25 of revenue and about $17 of profit ($45 against $28). Every 10 percentage points of orders that shift to the 2-pack therefore add about $2.50 to average order value and **$1.70 to profit per order**. A 20-point shift is worth $3.40 and a 30-point shift $5.10. No other upsell in this report can plausibly move by that much. The existing discounts are already generous: 2 for $64.99 is 18.7 percent off and 3 for $89.99 is 25 percent off, deeper than FitSleeps' 10 and 20 percent ([FitSleeps product page](https://fitsleeps.com/products/fitsleeps-pulse-standard)) and Manta's 7 to 10 percent on two-packs ([Manta catalogue](https://mantasleep.com/products.json?limit=250)). Raising the single-band price is not the lever either: Homedics, a mainstream brand, sells its own vibrating "WakeBand" at $39.99 ([Homedics catalogue](https://www.homedics.com/products.json?limit=250)). The discount does not need to grow; the presentation does.

The best evidence is from the sleep niche. An agency reports that for SomniFix mouth strips, making the three-pack the default selection and showing the price per unit **lifted average order value 27 percent**; before the change most buyers took the smallest pack because it was pre-selected ([conversionrate.store](https://conversionrate.store/increase-ecommerce-store-sales/)). A second vendor case reports 26 percent more revenue per visitor from a pre-selected bundle, though that test also removed the single-unit option ([Intelligems](https://www.intelligems.io/resources/customer-stories/how-a-home-essentials-brand-turned-a-bundle-only-offer-into-a-22-profit-lift)). The maker of the quantity-break app FitSleeps uses reports a 23 percent lift for one client ([Kaching case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/kaching-appz/case-study/how-axzenia-boosted-their-aov-by-23-and-generated-109800-in-additional-revenue)). All three are self-reported by sellers of the service and were seen only as search summaries.

Two cautions apply. A wristband is not a consumable, so the case for two rests on couples, gifts and spares, and the share choosing two will sit below what mouth-tape brands see. And no published benchmark exists for what share of orders take two or three units; guides tell merchants to read it from their own order history ([Ninety9](https://ninety9.dev/blog/quantity-breaks-vs-volume-discounts.html)). There is also a counter-example: a $50 charger brand dropped its volume discounts for a lower base price and reported roughly double the conversion ([Intelligems](https://intelligems.io/clutchchargercasestudy)). The practical change is small: select the 2-pack by default with its price plainly visible, show "$32.50 per band" and "$30.00 per band" against $39.99, and label it "best value", which is arithmetically true. "Most popular" is not yet a claim two orders can support.

### A one-click second band after payment is worth $0.35 to $1.00 per order

Shopify allows a one-click offer page between payment and the thank-you page on every plan except Starter; offers inside the checkout steps themselves need Shopify Plus ([Shopify developer docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/checkout-extensions)). Because payment is already complete, this is the one placement that cannot lower conversion of the original order. For a one-product store the natural offer is the same band again at $25, the existing step-up to the 2-pack, so it does not undercut the product page.

Acceptance rates are modest. The two largest datasets report **8.7 percent** of offer views across 13,614 stores ([Zipify study](https://www.einnews.com/pr_news/957117063/zipify-study-of-123-9m-upsell-offers-finds-pre-checkout-pop-ups-convert-best-yet-half-as-many-shopify-stores-use-them)) and **4.7 percent**, with a 5.6 percent average lift in order value ([ReConvert](https://reconvert.io/blog/the-impact-of-post-purchase-upselling-2023)). Zipify's marketing pages quote 16.2 percent, which conflicts with its own study ([Zipify](https://zipify.com/blog-how-to-upsell-in-2026/)). At 4 to 9 percent acceptance and $17 profit, the offer earns $0.70 to $1.55 per single-band order that sees it. Not every order does. Shopify skips the page for Apple Pay, Google Pay, Amazon Pay, Klarna, Affirm and Afterpay, and for anything other than a credit card ([Shopify developer docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/checkout/product-offers)); app vendors say Shop Pay paid in full still qualifies but Shop Pay instalments do not ([Aftersell docs](https://aftersell.mintlify.app/aftersell/payment_methods)). If half to two-thirds of buyers see it, which is an assumption, the value falls to roughly $0.35 to $1.00 per order.

The obstacle is the app fee. Aftersell is reported at $34.99 a month for up to 500 orders ([Aftersell listing](https://apps.shopify.com/aftersell), search summary), and sources disagree on whether ReConvert has a free tier for stores under about 50 orders a month ([app-compare](https://app-compare.pages.dev/pricing/reconvert/)). **No app price was confirmed on a live listing.** At about $1 of gain per order, a $35 fee needs roughly 35 orders a month to break even. Until then, a free substitute is a discount code for a second band in the order-confirmation email, which converts worse because the buyer must check out again. Two operational points: whether DSers picks up a quantity added after payment is untested, and Meta's pixel receives only the original purchase value, so Shopify's own reports are the place to judge the offer ([Shopify developer docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/checkout/product-offers)).

## Protection, warranties, gifts and priority fees add cents and carry risk

### Shipping protection is worth $0.15 to $0.60 and should stay unticked

Vendors quote opt-in rates of 25 to 45 percent for unticked shipping protection ([Cartylabs](https://cartylabs.com/blog/shopify-shipping-protection-upsell)), but the cases with stated numbers are lower: one merchant's protection offer started at 2 to 3 percent of orders and reached 8 to 19 percent after work on the design ([Extend: Felix Gray](https://extend.com/merchant-case-studies/felix-gray)), and another plateaued near 16 percent ([Extend: Voro Motors](https://www.extend.com/merchant-case-studies/voro-motors)). At 5 to 20 percent, $2.99 earns $0.15 to $0.60 per order before the cost of reshipping. A flat-fee app such as Navidium, reported at $29.99 a month ([Navidium listing](https://apps.shopify.com/navidium-shipping-protection)), would cost more than the add-on earns.

Pre-ticking is not an option. Since 18 February 2025 Shopify has required explicit buyer consent before any optional paid item is added, and bars pre-selected charges ([Shopify changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/posts/new-buyer-consent-requirement)). UK law also bans pre-ticked paid extras ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/11/18/breaking-news-the-uks-consumer-law-revolution-starts-here-cma-fires-starting-p-102luuz)). Two further risks are unresolved. US insurance regulators warn that protection sold without a licensed insurer may be unlicensed insurance ([NAIC consumer alert](https://content.naic.org/index.php/article/consumer-alert-beware-package-protection-insurance-scams)), and class actions filed in 2025 and 2026 target pre-selected protection fees ([Mertson v. Route complaint](https://www.classaction.org/media/mertson-et-al-v-route-app-inc-complaint.pdf)); no rulings were found. The store's unticked $2.99 avoids the pre-selection complaint. Keep it itemised as its own cart line, label it optional, do not call it insurance, and keep it away from the buy button given the one test showing a conversion drop.

### A paid warranty is the competitor's biggest virtual add-on and the riskiest to copy

FitSleeps sells a "10-Year Warranty" for **$18, which is 46 percent of its $39 band**, as an own-brand promise with no insurer named, while advertising a free 2-year warranty on the same page ([FitSleeps catalogue](https://fitsleeps.com/products.json?limit=250)). On paper it is attractive: a replacement band costs about $8.33. Reported attach rates (the share of all orders that include the add-on) are 4 percent for a "typical" consumer brand ([Clyde](https://joinclyde.com/blog/the-metrics-that-matter)) and 9 to 14 percent in vendor case studies on small electronics, including 11.4 percent on a roughly $50 blender ([Extend: BlendJet](https://www.casestudies.com/company/extend/case-study/blendjet-increases-sitewide-conversion-rate-with-500k-in-extend-product-protection-sales)). Extend's co-founder says attach rates rise mainly above $100 ([Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/extend-ramps-retailers-eye-extended-warranty-shipping-protection-plans)). At $18 and 4 to 12 percent, that is $0.70 to $2.15 per order before claims.

The law is the problem. A promise included in the product price is a warranty; the same promise sold for an extra charge is a service contract under federal definitions ([16 CFR 700.11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/700.11)). State laws regulate service contracts: Texas exempts only warranties given "without additional consideration" ([Texas Occupations Code](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_1304.004)), Washington treats selling one without registration as an illegal practice ([Washington insurance regulator](https://insurance.wa.gov/media/7062)), California requires registration and financial backing ([California BHGS](https://www.bhgs.dca.ca.gov/licensee/bear_faqs.shtml)), and Florida requires a licence ([Florida Statutes ch. 634](https://law.justia.com/codes/florida/2017/title-xxxvii/chapter-634/part-iii/)). Secondary sources also list "extended warranties" among businesses prohibited by Shopify Payments ([shopping-cart-migration FAQ](https://www.shopping-cart-migration.com/faq/22-shopify/prohibited-businesses-ip-infringement-regulated-or-illegal-products-and-services)); the official list was not opened. Licensed third-party providers are unrealistic at two orders: Clyde, for one, reviews each store's eligibility before onboarding ([Clyde listing](https://apps.shopify.com/clyde-warranty-platform), search summary). This is background research, not legal advice, but the lawful version is clear: **include a free written "Limited Warranty" of one year in the price**, with its terms on the site before purchase as federal warranty law requires ([Foster Swift](https://www.fosterswift.com/communications-Warranty-Full-or-Limited.html)). It earns nothing directly, costs $8.33 per claim, and answers the trust question a paid plan exploits.

### Gifts, priority handling, subscriptions and accessories do not fit this store

No brand examined sells paid gift wrap, gift messaging or priority handling as a line item. Vendor guides put gift wrap at 8 to 15 percent of orders for giftable goods ([GroPulse](https://gropulse.com/how-to-add-paid-gift-wrap-to-shopify/)), worth $0.15 to $0.75 per order at $3 to $5, but an AliExpress supplier cannot wrap a parcel. One vendor claims a 22.8 percent take rate for priority processing with no method shown ([Supercart](https://supercartapp.com/features/shopify-rush-priority-vip-processing-boost-aov-and-sales/)); the only test found is the Blue Stout one in which removing it raised conversion 9 percent. If dispatch is not actually faster, the fee is also a misleading claim. FitSleeps' $7.95 "Upgrade to Waterproof", a paid toggle that ships nothing ([FitSleeps catalogue](https://fitsleeps.com/products.json?limit=250)), is the same problem and should not be copied.

Recurring revenue in this niche comes from app memberships (Hatch, Oura, and Eight Sleep at $199 to $399 a year, per [Eight Sleep](https://www.eightsleep.com/autopilot/)) or consumable refills, such as Hostage Tape's $49.99 every three months ([Hostage Tape](https://hostagetape.com/pages/mt-why-mouthtape-mouthbreathing-sleep-sub), search summary). A wristband with no app and no consumable has neither, and FitSleeps itself sells no subscription. Physical accessories are the remaining option: FitSleeps lists a spare strap at $25, a case at $9.95 and a charger at $8.80 ([FitSleeps catalogue](https://fitsleeps.com/products.json?limit=250)). They work only if the supplier packs them in the same parcel, which is unknown, and median take rates for cart offers are 1.4 to 1.9 percent ([Candy Rack benchmarks](https://www.digismoothie.com/blog/upsell-benchmarks)), so they are a later test.

## Shopify and US rules allow these upsells only unticked, itemised and true

None of the recommended upsells is prohibited; the risk lies in how each is presented. Shopify's rule is the simplest: no optional charge may be added automatically or pre-selected, its cost must be shown clearly, and checkout extensions may not carry countdown timers ([Shopify App Store requirements](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/app-store-requirements)). A customer can accept at most three one-click offers per checkout, only one app can run them, and offers must show the same name and price as the store ([Shopify developer docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/checkout/product-offers)). Pasted scripts on the thank-you page stopped working for non-Plus stores on 26 August 2026, so any offer there must be an app block ([Shopify developer docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/online-store/script-tag-deprecation/order-status)). These rules were read directly.

US federal rules are narrower than often claimed. The FTC's "click-to-cancel" rule was struck down on 8 July 2025 ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule)) and would matter only for subscriptions. The federal junk-fees rule covers only event tickets and short-term lodging ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/de/pubs/2025/01/ftc-issues-final-junk-fees-rule-to-crackdown-on-aggressive-pricing-practices)). State laws reach further: California requires the advertised price to include every mandatory fee, including "handling", while actual shipping can stay separate ([California Attorney General](https://www.oag.ca.gov/hiddenfees)), and Massachusetts requires optional fees to be labelled optional ([940 CMR 38.00](https://www.mass.gov/doc/junk-fee-regulations-940-cmr-3800-0/download)). Optional add-ons shown unticked with a price beside them satisfy both.

Three rules matter more for a new store than any upsell. The FTC's fake-reviews rule, in force since 21 October 2024, bans fake or misattributed reviews with penalties cited at up to $53,088 per violation ([Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ftc-finalizes-rule-on-fake-reviews); [KJK](https://kjk.com/2026/08/06/ftc-consumer-review-rule-enforcement-what-businesses-need-to-know/)), so supplier reviews should not be shown as the store's own. The FTC names countdown timers that reset as a deceptive design ([FTC dark patterns report](https://www.ftc.gov/node/79647)). FitSleeps shows "72% of stock sold already!" while its own page data reports 10,746 units in stock ([FitSleeps product page](https://fitsleeps.com/products/fitsleeps-pulse-standard)), which is an observation, not a model. And Meta's advertising rules bar copy that implies the viewer's health condition ([Meta Personal Attributes policy](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/personal-attributes)), so ads should describe the band ("wakes you without waking your partner"), not the reader ("struggling with insomnia?"). The legal points in this section come from search summaries of statutes and law-firm notes, not full readings.

## What to do first: a ranked plan with per-order profit

The ranking below orders actions by expected profit per order, net of cost and risk. Profit figures are calculations from the store's own margins multiplied by the take rates cited above; they are planning ranges, not forecasts.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pre-select the 2-pack, show "$32.50 per band" and "$30.00 per band", label it "best value" | +$1.70 for every 10 points of orders moved to the 2-pack; $1.70 to $5.10 for a 10 to 30 point shift | $0 | Arithmetic from store margins; size of shift rests on vendor and agency cases |

| 4 | Keep $2.99 shipping protection unticked, itemised, labelled optional and away from the buy button | +$0.15 to $0.60, already in place | $0 | Vendor case studies; one test showing conversion harm |

| 6 | After about 100 orders, test the guide as a paid $6.99 offer in the cart or after payment, and an accessory if the supplier will pack it in the same parcel | +$0.15 to $0.70 for the guide | $0 | Vendor take-rate data of 2.4 to 10 percent |

Ranks 1 to 3 together are worth roughly **$2 to $6 per order** if the 2-pack shift lands between 10 and 30 points, which nobody can promise. That closes between a fifth and a little over half of the $10.50 gap. The comparison that matters: a 20 percent fall in ad cost per order would save more than $9 on a $47 ad cost, more than every upsell combined.

Measurement has to wait. With no takers in 30 orders, the true take rate is probably under 10 percent; about 100 orders pins a rate to within 6 to 9 points; and detecting whether an upsell hurts conversion would need about 21,000 visitors per version, which is unaffordable at this ad cost. These are the researchers' own statistical calculations; published guidance simply asks for 200 to 1,000 conversions per version ([Fudge](https://www.fudge.ai/guides/shopify-ab-test-sample-size/)). Change one thing at a time and compare profit per order over 50 to 100 orders.

## Conclusion

The research set out to price a sleep guide and ended by demoting it. The guide's best use is as the reason to buy two bands, because the store's most profitable upsell is the product it already sells, and the direct competitor's catalogue shows the same logic: digital content free, extra units and add-ons paid. What FitSleeps earns from an $18 warranty and a $7.95 "waterproof" toggle is real money, but it rests on promises a two-order UK dropshipper cannot lawfully or honestly sell into the US.

The harder implication is about scale. Every upsell here is a percentage of orders, and at two orders none of them can be measured, let alone tuned. Even in a good case they recover half the shortfall, so the store's survival still turns on cheaper customer acquisition; the upsells decide how much each hard-won order is worth once it arrives.
