Group 1

Fix these first

Quick changes that protect you before you spend on ads.

Only show real reviews

Do first10 minutes

Your page shows reviews like "Very comfortable to sleep and wake up in… Paul F, Verified buyer". The store opened on 29 Oct. If those didn't come from real buyers, take them down now: the US FTC rule on fake reviews (2024) allows fines per review. Install Judge.me (free), let it email buyers for reviews automatically, and turn the section back on once real ones come in. The same goes for the "Real mornings" videos: keep them only if those are customers or creators you paid and can disclose.

Check three claims against the real band

Do firstNeeds supplier answer

Your page says "7-day battery" (in the table) and "Zero disruption to anyone else. Your partner, baby or roommate sleeps right through it." All the ads lean on the same promise. Get the battery life in writing from your supplier, and do the quiet-bedroom test with the band buzzing on a pillow. If either doesn't hold, soften the wording before the ads run. It's the cheapest way to avoid refunds and bad reviews.

Longer trial: 60 nights instead of 30 days

Medium impactYour call on risk

FitSleeps offers 100 days, HiStrips 100, Manta 60, Hatch 30. Buyers compare. 60 nights, framed as "nights" like Hatch's "30-night bedside trial", sounds more generous without going as far as 100. The trade-off is a slightly longer refund window.

Add to cart bar that stays on screen

Medium impactTheme setting or small app

Your page is long (comparison table, steps, FAQ). Once people scroll past the button, there's no way to buy without scrolling back up. A slim bar at the bottom of the phone screen fixes that. Most Shopify themes have it as a setting.

Seen, but skip

Don't copy these

They work short-term for dropship stores and make a brand look cheap. Some are also legal risks.

Fake "72% of stock sold" bars

FitSleeps shows this on every visit. It's the classic dropship tell, and fake scarcity counts as a deceptive practice under FTC rules.

Countdown timers and "up to 70% off"

HiStrips runs a ticking timer and 54–76% off on almost everything. You can't match that at $39.99, and inflated "was" prices are an FTC risk.

"Clinically studied", "As seen in Forbes"

HiStrips claims both. Use lines like these only with a real study or real press. The same goes for "100% wake-up guarantee": don't promise anything the band can't guarantee.
