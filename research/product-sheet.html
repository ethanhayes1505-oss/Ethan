Seven Product Sheet

Research date: 28 November 2026. Every number below was read from the seller's own page, Meta's Ad Library, AliExpress or a named source. Anything I estimated or could not check is labelled.

How to read the money

  • Left per order is the selling price minus supplier cost, shipping and card fees. It is before ad spend.
  • The wake band test showed we need about £40 left per order to survive Meta's ad costs.
  • Supplier prices are AliExpress single-unit prices. Bulk prices from Alibaba should be lower.
  • Shipping figures are my estimates, not quotes. Card fees are 2.9% plus $0.30. $1 = £0.76.

What the numbers show, criterion by criterion

  • Most money per order: Canivet, by a wide margin. The herding ball and cat cave come next.
  • Most ad activity: the herding ball (644) and SmoothPaw (626).
  • Longest running: Cattasaurus (four to five years) and TruLink (16 months).
  • Can be sold with no health claim: SmoothPaw, herding ball, wrist trainer, cat cave.
  • Claim disputed by an authority: PureNest (EPA) and Canivet (veterinary bodies).
  • Cannot be copied as it is: the cat cave (design patent) and the Uahpet purifier (their own design).
  • Lightest to ship: wrist trainer, tooth cleaner, odour puck.

PureNest cat odour puck

Zoosnug · a small rechargeable disc that sits near the litter box and releases negative ions
Sells for$45.99
Supplier (matching puck)−$16.54
Shipping (estimate)−$4.50
Card fees−$1.63
Left per order$23.32 · £18
Live US ads: 198, from a page called "Your pet trainer"
On sale since: 11 July 2026
Guarantee: 60 days, no questions
Other ionisers on AliExpress: $3–$20

Who buys

Indoor-cat owners, often with two or more cats. Every review on the page is from a woman, and the ads are written to women who host family at home. That is the seller's targeting, not measured data.

What they want

To stop being the house that smells of cat. The page leans on shame: avoiding guests, being told by a sister, lighting candles before visitors.

Angles

  • Seller leads with: "What Happens to Your Nose After Living With Cats for a Year" (you have gone nose-blind).
  • Also used: sprays only mask it; host again without worrying.
  • Usable honestly: none of the strong ones, because the product would have to deliver the odour removal.

Positives

  • Small and light, cheap to ship.
  • Strong emotional pull, and the nose-blind idea makes people doubt their own home.
  • One per room, so two- and three-packs are natural.
  • About 53 million US households own a cat.

Negatives

  • The EPA says ion generators do not remove gases or odours. That is the whole promise.
  • California requires every indoor air cleaner sold there to be certified, including online sales.
  • Ionisers can produce ozone, so "100% safe for cats" is a claim you would have to prove.
  • Left per order is about £18 on a single unit.
  • The odour pitch is crowded: Petmizer alone runs 716 ads, with many look-alike pages.
  • Their page shows "June: sold out, 5,320 sold" for a product created on 11 July. We would not copy that.

TruLink golf wrist trainer

TruLink Golf · a strap-on brace with a rigid plate that stops the lead wrist bending
Sells for (£24.99 seen)about $32.73
Supplier (matching brace)−$7.33
Shipping (estimate)−$4.00
Card fees−$1.25
Left per order$20.15 · £15
Live US ads: 182 across all their products
On sale since: 28 July 2025
Guarantee: 30 days
Weight: 337 g in their product data
Cheapest supplier: $3.45

Who buys

Golfers who play regularly and want to fix their own swing. In 2025 the US had 29.1 million on-course golfers, average age 44, and about 72% were men.

What they want

Clean, repeatable contact and more distance without paying for lessons. The pain is hitting one shot fat and the next thin in front of friends.

Angles

  • Seller leads with: "2 Weeks to Consistent Contact" and "Free Video Series From Pro Coach".
  • Page line: "No swing thoughts. Just put it on and go."
  • Usable honestly: what it physically does (keeps the wrist flat), plus a free drill video as the extra.

Positives

  • Sixteen months on sale, the longest of the products we could copy.
  • Light and very cheap to source.
  • No health claim needed.
  • Buyers are mostly men aged 25 to 50, the cheapest group to reach in the wake band test.
  • A free video series adds value at no cost per order.

Negatives

  • About £15 left per order. It only works as a bundle or with add-ons.
  • At least six funded golf-aid advertisers, including Finalputt and Torc.
  • Walmart sells similar braces for $6–$20, which caps the price.
  • "More distance" is a results promise that leads to refunds.
  • Winter is the off-season in much of the US.

Cheaper versions: $4–$7, lower quality

Who buys

Cat owners who dread nail trims. The ads run from pages posing as older women's cat diaries, which points to women aged 45 and over. That is the seller's targeting, not measured data.

What they want

No more wrestling the cat into a towel, no scratched arms or sofa, and no guilt about hurting her.

Angles

  • Seller leads with: "Cat breeder of 30 years nail care secret" and "They almost cut my cat's toe off...".
  • Page line: "No clippers, no towel burritos and no stressed out cat."
  • Usable honestly: a treat puzzle she scratches at, shown on video. Promise the play, not the result.

Positives

  • No health claim needed.
  • Very high ad activity for a five-month-old product.
  • Easy to show working in a short video.
  • Simple object with no electronics to fail.
  • Only one other seller appeared in my ad search.

Negatives

  • I found no vet or independent source saying it shortens nails.
  • Similar boxes on Walmart score 1 to 3 stars on small samples.
  • About £18 left per order, and wood is heavy to ship.
  • Their own page warns of copycats, so the product is easy to clone.
  • A "nails not smoother" guarantee invites returns.
  • Their ads use invented diary personas, which we would not copy.

Canivet dog tooth cleaner

Canivet · a pen-shaped ultrasonic scaler with five strength levels for scraping tartar off a dog's teeth at home
Sells for$99.90
Supplier (5-mode scaler)−$9.35
Shipping (estimate)−$4.00
Card fees−$3.20
Left per order$83.35 · £64
Live US ads: 234, from four pages
On sale since: 28 March 2026
Guarantee: 60 days, based on visible results
Supplier orders: 2,000 on the matching listing

Who buys

Dog owners worried about bad breath and brown teeth, and about the vet bill. A professional clean under anaesthesia is usually quoted at $300–$700, and about 80% of dogs show gum disease by age three.

What they want

To avoid the cost and the fear of putting the dog under anaesthesia, and to feel they are looking after the dog properly.

Angles

  • Seller leads with: "Silent Ultrasonic Plaque & Tartar Remover for dogs".
  • Page lines: "That brown buildup isn't a stain" and "no need for a general anesthesia".
  • Usable honestly: hard to find one. The selling point is replacing the vet, which vets say it cannot do.

Positives

  • About £64 left per order, the only product clearly above our bar.
  • Small, light and cheap to source.
  • Eight months on sale with steady ad activity.
  • A real, expensive problem that most dog owners have.
  • No other brand appeared in the 20 ads I saw for this search.

Negatives

  • No major vet body recommends cleaning without anaesthesia, according to PetMD, which names the AVMA, AAHA and AVDC.
  • A Swedish university found ultrasonic toothbrushes do not remove tartar in dogs.
  • A pointed metal tip on an awake dog can injure the gums.
  • The page promises to prevent gum disease. That is a health claim, which our rules exclude.
  • The page header says 863 reviews while its own review box says 38.
  • A results-based guarantee invites refunds. One Trustpilot review calls theirs misleading.

Who buys

Cat owners who spoil their cats, often with several cats or a large one. The page speaks to older buyers too ("no problem with old fingers").

What they want

A happy, busy cat, claws kept off the furniture, and something that looks good in the living room.

Angles

  • Seller leads with: "Cat Loves It or Your Money Back!"
  • Page line: "9 reasons why this is a BIG hit with cats, even with picky ones".
  • Usable honestly: cats playing in it on video, and furniture saved.

Positives

  • No health claim needed.
  • Only three small copycats appeared in my ad search.
  • Naturally sold in pairs, which lifts the order value.

Negatives

  • Bulky to ship.
  • Buyers complain about zips, cats that ignore it and returns.
  • Generic versions are $19–$45 at Walmart.
  • A "cat loves it or money back" promise is costly to match.

Who buys

Pet owners bothered by floating hair, dust and smell, and people with allergies who still want pets. Uahpet is an established pet-tech brand with feeders, fountains and trackers.

A cleaner home with less hair on everything, fewer sneezes and quieter nights.

Chewie herding ball

Chewie · a 22-inch inflatable ball in a tough fabric cover that dogs push around a yard
Sells for (£54 seen)about $70.73
Supplier (ball, cover, pump)−$18.00
Shipping (estimate)−$7.50
Card fees−$2.35
Left per order$42.88 · £33
Live US ads: 644
On sale since: 26 March 2026
Guarantee: 30 days, plus a free replacement if the dog destroys it
Supplier range: $10.95–$26.75

Who buys

Owners of large, high-energy dogs with a yard: shepherds, collies, cattle dogs, Malinois. The German Shepherd is the fourth most popular US breed. Men and women both buy.

What they want

A tired, calm dog, a toy that survives more than ten minutes, and less guilt about not exercising the dog enough.

Angles

  • Seller leads with: "45% Off + Free Gift Today Only!"
  • Other sellers use: "Instantly tires dogs out" and "Give That Herding Instinct a Job to Do".
  • Usable honestly: the dog chasing it on video and the nap afterwards. Avoid the word indestructible.

Positives

  • The most ad activity of the seven, sustained for eight months.
  • No health claim needed.
  • About £33 left per order, close to our bar.
  • Sells itself on video.
  • Ships deflated.

Negatives

  • "Indestructible" with free replacements is a cost that grows with sales.
  • A similar ball on Chewy scores 3.0 from 97 ratings, with punctures and broken zips.
  • Amazon has 139 herding balls, mostly $20–$50.
  • Needs a yard, which narrows the audience.
  • Supplier listings have few orders, so quality is untested.

What I could not verify

  • Whether any of these sellers is profitable. Ad counts show activity, not profit.
  • What these audiences cost to reach on your ad account.
  • Real shipping costs and parcel sizes.
  • Supplier quality, and bulk prices from Alibaba.
  • The US prices for TruLink and Chewie.
  • The age and sex split of buyers. The pet industry survey that holds it is paywalled.
  • The "12,000+", "20,000+" and "5,000+" customer counts on the sellers' pages.
