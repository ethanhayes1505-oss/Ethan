## Why this one
- **Different from all five ads so far.** It's the only one showing the gift being given, with two people's hands in warm Christmas tree light, forest green and amber. The others are night-blue lifestyle, a dark still life, a hallway, a comparison split and a bright gift box.
- **The buyer is the woken partner.** The headline speaks for the person who hears every alarm and buys the fix. That's the strongest buyer in the research ("I'm done being the backup alarm"; the Mumsnet poster who bought her husband a vibrating watch so his phone alarm would stop "waking me up"; "Their Alarm Has Gone Off 12 Times in the Last Hour").
- **Product first.** The display faces the camera straight on and reads 05:30, with the three grip ridges matching your real band.
- **A gap among competitors right now:** their gift ads are discount banners (Loftie 15% OFF, FitSleeps 54% OFF) or personal-attribute lines (Rise Bands). Nobody is selling it as a gift from the partner who gets woken.

## Before you spend
- "Only he can feel" and "you stay asleep" claim the partner isn't woken. Do the quiet-bedroom test first.
- The opening line describes his phone alarm. It doesn't promise the band wakes someone who sleeps through everything. Keep it that way: "slept through it" is the category's most common 1-star review.
- The hands are a staged scene, not customers. Don't add stars or a customer name.
- Only add "Order by [date] for Christmas" once your carrier has confirmed the cutoff.
